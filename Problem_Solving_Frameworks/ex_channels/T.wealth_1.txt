A BIG SALARY DOESN’T MEAN A RICH LIFE!!
Money is only one of seven types of wealth.
Here’s what the other six look like in real life 👇🏽👇🏽

1. TIME WEALTH
Imagine earning enough to take your family on holiday, but being unable to take a week off. Or paying for a beautiful home you barely get to spend time in.
That’s why time wealth isn’t about doing nothing. It’s having some control over your day: being able to rest, see people you love, and make plans without work cancelling everything.
Try this: look at the last seven days. Which hours did you choose how to spend, and which were decided for you? You may not be able to change your entire schedule, but you might be able to protect one evening, turn down one recurring commitment, or stop answering work messages at a set time.
When you consider a higher-paying opportunity, ask about the hours it will take as well as the salary it will bring.

2. HEALTH WEALTH
There’s a difference between having money for the things you enjoy and having the energy to enjoy them.
Think of someone who finally earns what they wanted but is constantly exhausted, sleeps badly, and spends every day off recovering from work. The income improved; their day-to-day life didn’t.
Health wealth means looking after the body that has to carry you through your plans. Start with what your actual week allows: regular movement, enough sleep where possible, meals that leave you feeling good, and time to recover. You don’t need an extreme routine to make progress.
Ask yourself: “Is the way I’m working today something my body could keep doing for years?” If the answer is no, that deserves attention before burnout makes the decision for you.

3. RELATIONSHIP WEALTH
You can have thousands of contacts and still have nobody you feel comfortable calling on a bad day.
Relationship wealth is the people you trust, the conversations you can be honest in, and the time you make for each other when nobody needs a favour. A better income can create opportunities for the people you love, but it can’t replace being present with them.
Pick three people who matter to you. When did you last spend time with each of them without rushing or checking your phone? Send a message, arrange a meal, or make that call you keep putting off.
Also ask whether you show up for them. Strong relationships are built during ordinary weeks, long before anyone needs help.

4. EMOTIONAL WEALTH
Two people can earn the same amount and experience completely different lives. One can enjoy what they’ve built. The other can spend every evening anxious, comparing themselves with someone who has more.
Emotional wealth isn’t being happy all the time. It’s being able to notice what’s going on inside you, handle setbacks, and ask for help instead of pretending every problem will disappear after your next pay rise.
If something keeps weighing on you, name it specifically. Is it a difficult conversation, pressure to maintain a lifestyle, or a schedule that never lets you switch off? Once you know what it is, you can decide on a real next step.
Money can ease many problems. It doesn’t mean you have to deal with every other problem alone.

5. KNOWLEDGE WEALTH
Your job title can change. The useful things you’ve learned stay with you.
Knowledge wealth includes skills, judgment, and experience you can carry into a new role, a business, or a difficult situation. It’s the difference between depending entirely on one position and knowing you can adapt when things change.
Think of one problem people already come to you for help with. What have you learned by solving it? Could you explain your process, improve it, or use it in another setting?
Then choose one skill to strengthen over the next month and make something with it. A finished project, a better result at work, or a useful example teaches you more than collecting another list of courses you never finish.

6. PURPOSE WEALTH
It’s possible to reach a goal and immediately wonder, “Was that it?” Sometimes the goal was yours. Sometimes you chased it because it looked impressive to everybody else.
Purpose wealth means knowing what you want your money, work, and time to make possible. It could be caring for your family, doing work you respect, building something useful, having freedom, or contributing to your community. It doesn’t have to sound impressive to anyone else.
Here’s a question worth sitting with: if nobody could see your job title, purchases, or achievements, which goals would you still choose?
Keep pursuing the ones that matter to you. Then let your income support that life, instead of letting the pursuit of income take up all of it.

##AUTOMATION
This is the extra skill I would add to the original list. You do not need to build a complicated AI agent to begin. Find one repetitive task and make the next step happen reliably.
Example: someone fills in an inquiry form; the answers go into a sheet; the owner receives a notification; the inquiry gets marked for follow-up. First draw the steps on paper. Decide what happens if a field is missing or the same person submits twice. Then build and test the simplest version.
For a portfolio sample, use dummy inquiries and show the form, the resulting sheet, the notification and your error checklist. Never put a real client's private information in a public demo.
