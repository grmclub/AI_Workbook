The Kind of Thinker Who Will Thrive in the AI Era | Head of Google Research (Yossi Matias)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FlOufV3hZFo

TL;DR

Future roles will prioritize critical questioning, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and adaptability. As AI becomes ambient, the focus shifts from technical execution to strategic problem-solving and ethical implementation.
The Evolving Role of Scientists and Researchers

Yossi Matias, VP of Google Research, highlights that the role of a scientist is shifting from manual experimentation toward acting as a "polymath" advisor. With the help of AI agents that function as virtual, mobile labs, researchers are increasingly focused on asking the right questions, iterating quickly, and connecting disparate disciplines that were previously siloed by intense specialization.
Ambient Intelligence and Societal Impact

The concept of "ambient intelligence" suggests a future where technology is so effective and integrated that it becomes invisible to the user. This shift is already enabling significant real-world applications:

    Crisis Management: Projects like "Flood Hub" use AI to provide predictive data that helps governments, such as Nigeria, take proactive financial and safety measures before disasters occur.

    Practical Assistance: Future AI will likely transition from a tool we actively manage to an ambient layer that provides actionable information for daily life, such as health monitoring or infrastructure maintenance.

Skills for the Future Workforce

As technology reduces the friction of technical tasks like coding or data analysis, the focus for future hiring will shift toward human-centric capabilities. Key requirements for the modern professional include:

    Systems Thinking: Acting as an "organization of one" who can manage multiple "departments"—such as ethics, research, and product development—simultaneously.

    Strategic Questioning: The ability to identify high-impact opportunities and apply sound judgment to complex problems.

    Adaptability: A capacity to learn rapidly as tools and workflows evolve at an accelerating pace.

    Emerging Roles: Future needs may include specialized roles like "AI-tricians"—professionals dedicated to maintaining and fixing AI systems embedded within homes and devices.

Leadership Challenges and Priorities

For research organizations like Google, the primary challenge is not a lack of ideas, but the need for prioritization. Leaders are tasked with balancing immediate, high-impact projects with long-term horizon scouting. The goal is to maximize societal benefit across fields like healthcare, energy, and crisis resilience by focusing talent on the most urgent and meaningful questions.

## AI changing how we work. These 15 career paths could become increasingly valuable through 2030 👇


1. AI AUTOMATION CONSULTANT
Companies want to use AI.
Many still don't know where to start.
People who can automate real business workflows could become incredibly valuable.

2. AI CONTENT OPERATOR
One person can now do work that once required an entire content team.
The advantage?
Knowing how to combine creativity with AI.

3. CYBERSECURITY SPECIALIST
AI is making cyber threats more sophisticated.
It's also changing how we defend against them.
Cybersecurity skills aren't going away anytime soon.

4. PERSONAL BRAND STRATEGIST
Founders, executives and creators all want attention.
But building an audience is a skill.
People who understand content + distribution could have a major advantage.

5. AI PRODUCT DESIGNER
AI is changing how software works.
That means someone has to design these new experiences.
AI-first product design could become a valuable specialty.

6. AI WORKFLOW BUILDER
Forget memorizing the "perfect prompt."
The bigger skill is knowing how to make AI actually useful.
Think prompts + tools + agents + workflows.

7. DATA ANALYST
Companies have more data than ever.
The valuable skill?
Turning all that information into decisions people can actually use.
AI could make great analysts even more productive.

8. AI VIDEO CREATOR
Creating professional video is getting dramatically easier.
AI can already generate, edit and transform footage.
That opens the door to entirely new kinds of creators.

9. COMMUNITY BUILDER
Followers are useful.
A loyal community can be even more powerful.
Brands and creators increasingly need people who know how to build real engagement.

10. AI EDUCATOR
Millions of people are still figuring out AI.
The opportunity isn't knowing every tool.
It's being able to explain complicated technology simply and practically.

11. REMOTE SALES SPECIALIST
AI can automate parts of sales.
Human trust is harder to automate.
People who can understand customers and close complex deals will still have valuable skills.

12. HEALTHCARE TECH SPECIALIST
Healthcare is becoming increasingly digital.
AI is adding another layer.
People who understand both healthcare and technology could sit at a powerful intersection.

13. INDIE SOFTWARE FOUNDER
Building software once required a team.
AI coding tools are lowering that barrier.
Small teams can now test and launch ideas faster than ever.

14. DIGITAL PRODUCT CREATOR
Templates. Courses. Apps. Guides. Systems.
AI makes digital products faster to create.
The real advantage is knowing what people actually want to buy or use.

15. HUMAN + AI OPERATOR
This might be the biggest skill of all.
Don't just ask AI questions.
Learn how to use it to research, create, analyze, automate and execute.
AI fluency could become a career advantage almost everywhere.

## AI isn’t just changing businesses anymore.
It’s creating new ones in marketing, cybersecurity, commerce, and agentic workflows.
Here are 15 AI business opportunities to watch in 2026 👇

1/ AI Agents Are Becoming Business Infrastructure
AI is moving beyond chatbots and one-off prompts toward agents that can plan, coordinate, and execute multi-step workflows.
➔ AI workflow implementation.
➔ Agent integration services.
➔ AI operations consulting.
Google Cloud identifies agentic workflows as a major 2026 business trend, while the OECD reports that SMEs are increasingly experimenting with AI agents.
Other opportunities:
➔ AI-powered customer service.
➔ AI sales assistants.
➔ AI research agents.

2/ Lean AI-Powered Businesses Are Growing
AI is lowering the cost of running a small business.
Founders can now use AI across marketing, finance, sales, operations, and product development without building large teams.
➔ AI-first agencies.
➔ Solo consulting businesses.
➔ Productized AI services.
➔ Niche SaaS products.
➔ AI-assisted professional services.
OpenAI reports that at least four million people in the U.S. used ChatGPT during March 2026 to help plan, start, run, or grow a business.

3/ AI Security Is Becoming a New Market
As businesses deploy more AI, they also create new security risks.
That creates demand for:
➔ AI security audits.
➔ AI governance.
➔ Agent access controls.
➔ Shadow AI monitoring.
➔ Prompt-injection protection.
➔ AI security training.
Recent security reporting highlights growing risks from AI-enabled attacks and uncontrolled employee use of AI tools.

4/ AI Is Creating New Customer Experiences
AI agents are changing how customers discover products, get support, and make purchasing decisions.
Opportunities include:
➔ AI shopping assistants.
➔ Personalized customer service.
➔ Conversational commerce.
➔ AI product recommendations.
➔ AI-powered customer success.
➔ Brand visibility in AI search.
Google Cloud predicts a shift toward more personalized, "concierge-style" customer experiences powered by AI agents.

5/ The Biggest Opportunity Is Solving Specific Problems
Don't build a business simply because AI can do something.
Look for expensive, repetitive, time-consuming problems where AI can create measurable value.
A practical approach:
➔ Find a painful workflow.
➔ Identify who already pays to solve it.
➔ Use AI to reduce time or cost.
➔ Build a narrow solution.
➔ Measure the result.
➔ Expand only after proving demand.
The opportunity isn't "AI" by itself.
It's applying AI to a problem businesses already care enough to pay to solve.
