##deep-dive into specific frameworks for IT consultancy

IT architecture, system integration, and digital transformation require specialized consulting frameworks to manage technical complexity and risk.
Here is a deep-dive into the most popular frameworks used by IT consultants at firms like Accenture, McKinsey Digital, and Gartner.

Enterprise Architecture & Governance
    • TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework): Divides enterprise architecture into Business, Application, Data, and Technology domains using its core Architecture Development Method (ADM).
    • Zachman Framework: A 6 × 6 matrix that organizes IT artifacts by asking fundamental questions (What, How, Where, Who, When, Why) across different stakeholder perspectives.
    • COBIT (Control Objectives for Information and Related Technology): Connects business goals to IT goals, providing metrics and maturity models to balance risk and investment returns.
    •
Digital & Cloud Transformation
    • Gartner’s 5 Rs of Cloud Migration: Outlines options for moving legacy systems to the cloud via Rehost (lift-and-shift), Refactor, Revise, Rebuild, or Replace.
    • McKinsey’s Tech Debt Framework: Quantifies the hidden cost of maintaining legacy IT systems to prioritize modernization budgets based on risk and business value.
    • Bain’s Digital Radar: Assesses a company's digital maturity across 30 dimensions to benchmark technical capabilities against industry competitors.
    •
IT Service & Delivery Management
    • ITIL (Information Technology Infrastructure Library): Focuses on aligning IT services with business needs through a lifecycle of Service Strategy, Design, Transition, Operation, and Continual Improvement.
    • Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe): Scales Lean and Agile practices across large enterprise IT engineering organizations to synchronize delivery, alignment, and governance.

##list IT consulting frameworks to identify change dependency

Summary: How a Consultant Uses Them Together
A senior IT consultant rarely uses just one. A standard approach for a major transformation looks like this:
    1. Use TOGAF to understand the high-level architecture dependencies.

    2. Use McKinsey 7-S to map how the tech changes will impact the HR and business structure.

    3. Consult the ITIL CMDB to identify the exact physical servers and software licenses affected.

    4. Use SAFe Dependency Boards to ensure the development teams don't block each other while building it.

1. Enterprise Architecture Frameworks (Structural & Tech Dependencies)
These frameworks help consultants map cross-layer dependencies between business goals, applications, data, and underlying IT infrastructure.
TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework)

    • Focus: Enterprise-wide architecture transition.
    • Dependency Identification Method: Uses the Architecture Development Method (ADM), specifically Phase E (Opportunities & Solutions) and Phase F (Migration Planning). It utilizes Gap Analysis and Consolidated Gaps, Solutions, and Dependencies Matrices to map technical and business dependencies required to move from the Current State (As-Is) to Target State (To-Be).

2. IT Operations & Service Management Frameworks (Operational Dependencies)
These frameworks map operational, software, and hardware dependencies during live environments and deployment.

ITIL 4 (Information Technology Infrastructure Library)
    • Focus: IT Service Management (ITSM) and Change Enablement.
    • Dependency Identification Method:
        ◦ Configuration Management Database (CMDB) / CMS: Maps Relationships between Configuration Items (CIs) (e.g., Server A →→ Database B →→ Business App C).
        ◦ Service Value System (SVS) & Service Mapping: Analyzes upstream and downstream risks before approving a Change Request.

3. Execution & Delivery Frameworks (Project & Team Dependencies)
These frameworks identify functional, feature, and delivery dependencies across software development and transformation programs.

Value Stream Mapping (VSM)
    • Focus: End-to-end process flow and handoffs.
    • Dependency Identification Method: Maps every step from a business request to delivery. It highlights process dependencies, queue times, handoff friction, and upstream/downstream system bottlenecks when altering an IT workflow.

4. Strategic & Change Management Frameworks (People & Business Dependencies)
IT changes often fail due to human and organizational dependencies. These frameworks map the non-technical impact.

    • McKinsey 7S Framework
        ◦ Focus: Organizational alignment during major change.
        ◦ Dependency Identification Method: Maps dependencies across 7 interconnected elements: Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared Values, Style, Staff, and Skills. Changing an IT system (Systems) immediately triggers required changes in Skills (training), Structure (team alignment), and Staff.
    • Prosci ADKAR & PCT (Project Change Triangle) Model
        ◦ Focus: Change Management & Adoption.
        ◦ Dependency Identification Method: The PCT model maps dependencies between Project Management, Leadership, and Change Management. It identifies whether a technical change is dependent on user awareness, capability building, or executive sponsorship to succeed.
    • Wardley Mapping
        ◦ Focus: Strategic value chain mapping.
        ◦ Dependency Identification Method: Maps components based on their Value Chain position (top to bottom) and Maturity/Evolution (Genesis →→ Custom →→ Product →→ Commodity). It shows structural dependencies (e.g., a custom AI feature depends on a commodity cloud infrastructure) to help consultants evaluate the impact of technological obsolescence or migration.

5. Analytical & Technical Matrix Frameworks
Mathematical and structural methods used by consultants for deep-dive technical and data impact analysis.

    • DSM (Dependency Structure Matrix / Design Structure Matrix)
        ◦ Focus: Complex system and software architecture analysis.
        ◦ Dependency Identification Method: An N×NN×N square matrix where rows and columns represent system components. Markings in the grid reveal Cyclic/Reciprocal dependencies (System A needs B, but B needs A), Sequential dependencies, and potential single-points-of-failure before refactoring code or microservices.
    • CRUD / Impact Analysis Matrix
        ◦ Focus: Data and functional dependency mapping.
        ◦ Dependency Identification Method: Maps Application Functions against Data Entities (Create, Read, Update, Delete). If an IT change alters how an entity is "Updated," the matrix instantly shows all applications that "Read" or "Delete" that data downstream.

##Anatomy of an IT Value Stream Map
Information / Demand Flow (Top): How requests are triggered, prioritized, and communicated (e.g., Jira tickets, architecture review boards, email requests).

Process / Workflow Steps (Middle): The sequential activities required to transform a request into value (e.g., Refinement →→ Development →→ Code Review →→ Automated Test →→ Security Gate →→ Prod Deployment).

Timeline Ladder (Bottom): The core diagnostic tool. It contrasts Process Time (PT) with Lead Time (LT).
