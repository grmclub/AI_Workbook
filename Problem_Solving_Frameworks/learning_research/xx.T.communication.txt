"Stop Over-Explaining: The 3 S’s Rule For Projecting Authority" by Codie Sanchez (BigDeal Podcast):

Core Insight

Smart, high-achieving individuals often sabotage their authority not because their ideas are weak, but because of poor speech delivery. Audiences evaluate speakers based on two primary dimensions: warmth and competence. To project authority, perceived competence must be prioritized through clean, deliberate communication [00:50].
Key Communication Traps & How to Fix Them
1. Excessive Hedging

    The Problem: Using qualifiers like "I could be wrong, but..." or "I'm not sure, maybe..." drastically lowers perceived competence and authority [00:38]. Research shows that speakers who present arguments with certainty—even when factually incorrect—are often judged as more credible than hesitant speakers who are right [01:46].

    The Fix: State conclusions directly grounded in data or explicit confidence levels [03:33].

        Instead of: "I might be wrong, but I think we should cut this feature."

        Use: "Based on the data we have, cutting this feature is the right move." [03:33]

2. Over-Explaining (Low Processing Fluency)

    The Problem: Clarifying, restating, and over-illustrating signals that you either doubt your audience's intelligence or doubt that your idea can stand on its own [04:23]. Cognitive psychology shows that simple, easily processed ideas (high processing fluency) are judged as more truthful and intelligent [05:05].

    The Fix: Deliver the core message concisely, then pause and let silence work [05:30]. Trust the listener to ask follow-up questions if they need deeper details [05:37].

3. Speaking Too Fast Under Pressure

    The Problem: Anxiety triggers the sympathetic nervous system, causing speech rate to accelerate [07:34]. Listeners unconsciously associate rushed delivery with uncertainty or a desire to finish before being rejected [07:40].

    The Fix: Apply the 3 S’s Rule: Shorter, Slower, Stronger [08:41]. Slow down by ~20% on key points, insert micro-pauses, and land important statements deliberately [08:13].

4. Specifying Specs Instead of Telling a Story

    The Problem: Leading with complex features or technical bullet points makes information forgettable [10:02].

    The Fix: Structure presentations around a narrative arc: Status Quo → Problem → Bold Promise → Proof → Future [10:12]. (e.g., Steve Jobs pitching the original iPod as "1,000 songs in your pocket" rather than listing hard drive specifications) [04:16].

5. Complex Frameworks vs. Simple Demonstrations

    The Problem: Over-complicating ideas or hiding behind jargon reduces perceived capability [12:40]. Secure speakers simplify complexity; insecure speakers try to prove their intelligence by piling on layers [13:02].

    The Fix: Aim for radical clarity [13:16]. "If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough." [12:56]

6. Skipping Deliberate Practice

    The Problem: Assuming natural delivery comes without effort leads to rambling, losing timing, and panic [14:22].

    The Fix: Rehearse key presentations out loud multiple times [14:33]. Deliberate practice is what creates the illusion of effortless charisma and allows real-time adaptability [15:12].

7. Chronic Self-Deprecation

    The Problem: Downplaying achievements or joking about flaws undercuts credibility, especially with new contacts [15:32]. Studies on job interviews demonstrate that strategic self-promotion increases ratings of competence and hireability, whereas excessive modesty reduces perceived capability [16:05].

    The Fix: Accept compliments cleanly with a simple "Thank you" rather than deflecting or minimizing your work [17:27].

Pre-Speech Reset Checklist [18:35]

Before entering high-stakes meetings or presentations, run through these quick diagnostic questions:

    Am I hedging unnecessarily?
    Am I over-explaining?
    Am I rushing key points?
    Am I over-complicating the core idea?
    Am I landing statements confidently?
    Am I comfortable with silence?

How to Speak Better Than 99% of People (5 Frameworks) | Codie Sanchez

In this video, Codie Sanchez breaks down 5 practical communication frameworks designed to elevate speaking clarity, influence, and executive presence in business, pitch, and leadership contexts.
The 5 Communication Frameworks

    The BLUF Framework (Bottom Line Up Front)

        Concept: Lead with the final takeaway, conclusion, or recommendation within the first 10 seconds, then back it up with context and supporting data.

        Why It Works: Respects the listener's time—especially C-suite stakeholders—and prevents rambling or losing audience focus before reaching the core point.

    The PREP Framework (Point, Reason, Example, Point)

        Structure:

            Point: State your main thesis clearly.

            Reason: Explain the underlying rationale or data supporting it.

            Example: Provide a concrete story, case study, or real-world example.

            Point: Restate your core message to lock in alignment.

        Why It Works: Highly effective for structured, off-the-cuff responses during meetings or Q&A sessions.

    The Rule of 3s

        Concept: Group complex ideas, arguments, or proposals into three distinct pillars or action steps.

        Why It Works: Human memory processes information in triads naturally. Expressing arguments in sets of three makes communication instantly more memorable and persuasive.

    The "So What?" Filter

        Concept: Before presenting features, data, or technical details, continually ask yourself, "So what does this mean for the listener?"

        Why It Works: Shifts your communication from being feature-heavy to benefit-driven, directly addressing the listener's explicit needs or ROI.

    Eliminating Filler Words & Harnessing the Strategic Pause

        Concept: Replace filler words ("um," "ah," "like," "you know") with deliberate 1- to 2-second silent pauses.

        Why It Works: Pausing demonstrates confidence, allows the audience time to process your point, and projects immediate authority.

In his course video, "How to Speak Better Than 99% of People (Complete Course)", Prince Ea outlines 10 foundational principles for public speaking, content creation, and powerful verbal communication [00:00].

Key Takeaways & The 10 Secrets of Speaking
1. The Power of the Pause [03:47]

    Don't rush into speaking: Silence creates authority and anticipation.
    Dramatic silence: Before starting or after being asked a question, hold eye contact silently for a moment [05:40].
    Break predictions: Avoid generic openers like "I'm glad to be here." Say something unexpected to command attention [06:29].

2. Conviction & Enthusiasm Over Logic [09:02]

    People make snap decisions about your credibility within the first 7 seconds [07:27].
    7-38-55 Rule: Communication impact is 55% visual, 38% vocal tone, and only 7% actual words [07:44].
    Enthusiasm and conviction win debates: Audiences are moved more by the depth of your conviction than by complex logic or dry facts [09:36].

3. Tone Matters Most [12:01]

    Tone conveys your true attitude toward the audience [12:31]. Changing the emphasis on a single word in a sentence completely alters its meaning [12:48].
    A brilliant idea poorly delivered dies, while a simple idea brilliantly delivered commands attention [13:22].

4. Enunciate Over-Articulately [13:47]

    Clear pronunciation increases believability and perceived confidence [13:54].
    Crisp articulation makes speech more trustworthy and easier for the listener to digest [14:02].

5. Speak to Be Felt, Not Just Heard [14:21]

    Before delivering any speech or video, answer three questions [14:33]:

        What do I want them to feel?
        What do I want them to know?
        What do I want them to do?

    Humans process emotions first and justify with logic later [15:07].

6. Radical Authenticity [16:08]

    Stop trying to appear hyper-professional—focus on being human and embracing your distinct voice [16:14].
    Authentic delivery straight from conviction is far more moving than rehearsed or mechanical notes [17:22].

7. Make It About the Audience [17:43]

    People care most about themselves ("WIIFM" - What's In It For Me) [18:49].
    Use terms like "you" frequently, understand your audience's emotional needs, and share relatable personal stories [20:56].
    Speak to one person: Treat a public talk or video like an intimate 1-on-1 conversation rather than addressing a crowd [21:46].

8. Keep It Simple (KISS Principle) [22:36]

    Avoid jargon or overcomplicating concepts. Simplify your core message so it can be grasped easily [22:50].
    Beware of the "curse of knowledge"—assuming others already understand your complex background context [24:46].
    Cutting unnecessary filler words takes effort, but brevity delivers maximum punch [26:13].

9. The Length Rule [26:36]

    The Woman's Skirt Rule: Keep your talk long enough to cover the core topic, but short enough to maintain continuous interest [26:51].

10. Overcome Fear ("Big Me" vs. "Little Me") [26:59]

    Speech anxiety is universal—even seasoned professionals feel nerves [27:19].
    Shift your state from your ego ("Little Me" afraid of judgment) to your message ("Big Me" serving the audience) [28:12].
