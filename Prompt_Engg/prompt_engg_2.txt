deeper breakdown of the AI integration skills
AI integration is no longer a niche skill for machine learning engineers. It is now expected of general developers, including frontend engineers.

The core skills required move beyond simple API prompts into building intelligent data systems:

1. Working with Embeddings for Search
    • What it means: Converting text, images, or data into mathematical vectors that capture semantic meaning.
    • Why it matters: It allows you to build "fuzzy" or conceptual search engines. Instead of matching exact keywords, your application can find relevant information based on the user's intent or context.

2. Advanced Prompt Engineering & Structuring
    • What it means: Moving away from casual chatting to designing predictable, programmatic prompts.
    • Why it matters: Applications require consistent, machine-readable data (like JSON) from an AI model. You must master techniques like few-shot prompting, system instructions, and schemas to ensure the AI seamlessly powers your application's UI without breaking it.

3. Building RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) Systems
    • What it means: Connecting a Large Language Model (LLM) to a private database or document store to extract facts before generating an answer.
    • Why it matters: LLMs do not know your company's private data or real-time changes. RAG prevents AI "hallucinations" by feeding the model exact, retrieved text chunks, allowing it to answer user queries with perfect accuracy based on your own documentation.

4. Moving Beyond Traditional CRUD
    • What it means: Transitioning from standard "Create, Read, Update, Delete" databases to applications that actively assist the user.
    • Why it matters: Modern companies want features that auto-summarize, predict user needs, clean messy data text input, or provide interactive chat interfaces natively inside the product.
