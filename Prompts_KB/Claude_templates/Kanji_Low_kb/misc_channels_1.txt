📌 1 | The R.I.S.E. Prompt Architecture
(Role → Instruction → Specifics → Examples)
This framework upgrades vague requests into professional-grade instructions.
Instead of asking for output, you define the thinking environment first.
How to use it:
Assign a credible role
Give one clear task
Lock in constraints (scope, length, timeframe)
Show what “good” looks like
Prompt:
ROLE: You are a senior product manager at a B2B SaaS company.
INSTRUCTION: Create a product roadmap presentation.
SPECIFICS: Target Q2 2025, focus on enterprise features, max 10 slides.
EXAMPLES: Slide format = Title → 3 bullets → 1 success metric per slide.

🔥Why it works:
Models perform better when they know who they are, what they’re doing, and how success is measured.

📌 2 | The C.O.D.E. Prompt System
(Context → Objective → Details → Examples)
This structure mirrors how AI researchers internally guide models, and it shows.
Instead of dumping a task, you frame the situation, then narrow the outcome.
Prompt:
CONTEXT: I’m launching a B2B AI tool for marketing teams.
OBJECTIVE: Write 5 LinkedIn posts that drive demo signups.
DETAILS: Each post should be 150–200 words, include a hook, pain point, solution, and CTA.
EXAMPLES: Hook style “Most marketers lose 10 hours/week on [problem]. Here’s how to fix it in minutes.”

🔥Why it works:
Context prevents generic answers.
Details prevent rambling.
Examples anchor quality.

📌 3 | The Sequential Chain Method
(Think in steps, not blocks)
Asking for everything at once kills quality.
High-performing prompts break thinking into stages.
You don’t just get better output, you get editorial control.
Prompt flow:
Step 1: Generate 10 content ideas about [topic].
Step 2: Rank the top 3 based on audience relevance and demand.
Step 3: Create a detailed outline for the #1 idea.
Step 4: Write only the introduction (200 words max).
Best used for:
Content (idea → outline → draft → polish)
Strategy (research → framework → execution)
Code (logic → pseudocode → build → refine)

🔥Why it works:
Each response becomes input, not noise.
You guide the model instead of reacting to it.

📌 4 | The Persona + Constraint Stack
(Precision through pressure)
If you want sharp answers, corner the model with rules.
Stack constraints until fluff has nowhere to hide.
Prompt:
You are a venture capital partner reviewing early-stage startups.
Respond in exactly 3 short paragraphs.
Use only evidence-based reasoning.
End with a single decision: Invest or Pass.
If Pass, give exactly 2 reasons, no more, no less.
Pitch: [Paste content]

🔥Why it works:
Constraints force discipline.
Discipline forces clarity.
Clarity produces insight.
