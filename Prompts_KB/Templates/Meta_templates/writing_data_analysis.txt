1. The Universal "Analyst Framework" Prompt

Use this structure whenever you have a new task. Fill in the brackets to instantly professionalize your output.

    Role: "Act as a Senior Business Analyst with 15+ years of experience in [Your Industry]."
    Context: "I am preparing a report on [Project/Issue] for [Target Audience, e.g., C-Suite executives/Project Stakeholders]. The current situation is [briefly describe the state]."
    Task: "Analyze the following [data/meeting notes/requirements]. Identify the top 3 high-impact risks, 3 potential opportunities for efficiency, and summarize the core bottleneck."
    Constraints: "Focus on long-term business impact. Avoid technical jargon where possible. Keep the tone objective and data-driven."
    Format: "Provide an executive summary followed by a structured table for the findings. Use clear, concise headings."
	
2. High-Impact Prompts for Report Refinement

If you have already drafted a report, use these prompts to "level up" the writing and analysis.
For Executive Summaries (The "So What?" Filter)

    "Review this report draft. Rewrite the executive summary to focus entirely on the 'So What?' for the stakeholders. Identify the three most consequential findings and explain the business impact of each in one sentence. Ensure the language is punchy, authoritative, and free of filler words."
	
For Tone and Objectivity (The "Devil’s Advocate")

    "Act as a critical editor. Review my analysis for potential biases or unsupported claims. Identify any parts of the argument that rely on conjecture rather than empirical evidence. Suggest more neutral, evidence-based phrasing for these sections."

For Data-to-Narrative Translation

    "Translate the following statistical findings into a plain-English narrative for non-technical leadership. Focus on the trend, the 'why' behind the anomaly, and one recommended next step. [Paste your data/stats here]"

## list best 5 AI prompts for refining economic analysis report for executive summary 

1. The "Executive Filter" (Refining for Impact)

Use this prompt when your draft is technically sound but too long or "in the weeds."

    "I have written an economic analysis on [Topic]. I need to condense this for a C-suite executive summary. Please rewrite this focusing only on the 'So What?'.

        Lead with the core bottom-line impact.

        Replace granular data points with high-level trend analysis (e.g., instead of quoting monthly fluctuations, describe the annual trajectory).

        Use the 'BLUF' (Bottom Line Up Front) method: State the conclusion first, then the three most critical supporting data points.

        Ensure the tone is authoritative and objective."

2. The "Risk & Opportunity" Pivot

Executives want to know how the economic environment creates exposure or competitive advantage.

    "Review my economic analysis. Identify the top three strategic risks and the top three untapped opportunities for our organization based on these trends. For each risk, suggest a concise mitigation strategy. For each opportunity, suggest one actionable 'next step.' Present this in a 2x3 table: [Opportunity/Risk] | [Business Impact] | [Recommended Action]."

3. The "Devil’s Advocate" (Stress Testing)

Use this to ensure your report is resilient before it goes to leadership.

    "Act as a skeptical CFO reading this economic analysis. Find the weaknesses in my logic.

        Where have I made assumptions that aren't fully backed by the data provided?

        What are the most likely counter-arguments to this conclusion?

        Are there any macroeconomic variables I have overlooked that could invalidate this forecast?

        Suggest more precise language to qualify these statements where appropriate."

4. Simplifying Macro Complexity

Use this when your analysis involves complex metrics (e.g., interest rate transmission mechanisms, yield curves, or debt-to-GDP dynamics).

    "I need to explain [Complex Economic Concept/Metric] in this report to non-financial stakeholders. Rewrite the following paragraph using a business-casual but professional tone. Use an analogy to make the economic impact intuitive without sacrificing accuracy. Ensure the focus remains on the organizational implication rather than the theory."

5. The "Action-Oriented" Conclusion

Ensure your report doesn't just describe the status quo, but prompts a decision.

    "Based on the economic trends identified in my report, draft a 'Management Recommendations' section. The tone should be professional and forward-looking. Provide 3 concrete, strategic recommendations that the leadership team should consider in the next quarter to prepare for [Economic Trend]. Keep it brief—no more than 3 bullet points, each with a clear rationale."
