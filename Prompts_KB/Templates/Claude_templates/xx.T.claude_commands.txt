##5 Secret Codes For Claude

#1: /ghost
what it does:
Makes any AI-written text sound like a real human wrote it — strips out robotic phrasing and AI patterns completely. Use it before sending a cold email or any copy that needs to sound like you.

#2: /redteam
what it does:
Claude attacks your idea from every angle a critic, competitor, or skeptic would use. It's a military term for "find the holes before the enemy does." Use it before any public launch or pitch.

#3: /premortem
what it does:
Imagines your project already failed 6 months from now, then works backwards to figure out why. Forces you to find the cracks before you're in too deep.

#4: /tripwire
what it does:
Identifies the single thing most likely to blow up your entire plan. Not a list of risks — just the one that would kill everything. Huge for decisions with a lot of moving pieces.

#5: /ultrathink
what it does:
Type this word in any prompt and Claude shifts into its deepest reasoning mode — slower, more thorough, and more deliberate than a normal response. It's the difference between Claude giving you an answer and Claude actually thinking through the problem.

8 Claude fable 5 commands that help you get more from every prompt—and make every token count. 🧵

1.
/compact : Your context window fills up fast. When it does, Claude starts forgetting what you said 20 minutes ago.
/compact compresses the conversation into a summary without losing the thread.
This one alone saves you from wasting tokens on repeated context.

2.
/goal : Set a finish line. Tell Claude what "done" looks like and it keeps working across turns until it gets there. A separate smaller model checks after every step whether the goal is met. You don't babysit it.
/goal all tests in auth pass and lint is clean
Walk away.

3.
/ultrareview : This one's wild. It doesn't do a single code review. It spins up multiple agents that each review your code independently with different checklists, then synthesizes the results.
Issues flagged by multiple agents get bumped to high priority.
Run it before every PR.

4.
/btw : Ask a side question without polluting your main conversation. Need to check something quick mid-task?
/btw keeps it out of the context so Claude doesn't get confused about what it's actually working on.
Small command, saves a lot of wasted turns.

5.
/loop : Set a recurring check on a schedule. Different from /goal. This one watches for something to change, not push toward a finish line.
/loop 5m check if the deploy went green
It checks every 5 minutes and reports back. You're not driving. You're monitoring.

6.
/effort : Not every task needs max reasoning.
/effort low for boilerplate, /effort max for hard architecture problems. You control how much thinking Claude does per turn.
At API rates, this is the difference between a $2 session and a $20 one.

7.
/plan : Before any big change, switch to plan mode. Claude shows you exactly which files it'll touch, why, and in what order. You approve the plan before a single character changes.
One bad refactor on API pricing can burn through your entire budget.

8.
/resume : Pick up yesterday's session exactly where you left off. No re-explaining, no rebuilding context from scratch.
Most people restart every morning and waste the first 10 minutes getting Claude back up to speed. After tomorrow, those 10 minutes cost you.

05 Techniques to Get the Most Out of Claude

1. Gmail Connector (The AI Inbox Assistant)
Instead of searching your mailbox or typing replies manually, let Claude filter and summarize information directly from your emails.
Step 1: Open the Claude workspace sidebar and click Customize, then select Connectors.
Step 2: Choose Gmail from the list of integrations and click log in.
Step 3: Authorize the security permissions to securely link your workspace inbox.
Step 4: Give your Executive Assistant a shared seat on that specific Claude account so they can self-serve on historical data questions instead of asking you.

2. Calendar Connector (The AI Executive Coach)
Connect Claude to your Google Calendar to run autonomous time audits against your business milestones instead of just checking free slots.
Step 1: Go to Customize ➡️ Connectors on the left sidebar and select Google Calendar.
Step 2: Click log in and link your primary business calendar.
Step 3: Paste your core business goals, target projects, or quarterly KPIs straight into the chat viewport.
Step 4: Run this prompt: "Analyze my calendar time blocks alongside my emails over the past 30 days. Run a brutal time audit and tell me exactly where my hours are mismatched with my stated targets."

3. Co-Work Mode (Hands-Free Desktop Automation)
Allow Claude to take over your screen layout, control your cursor, and execute multi-app processes across your computer while you step away.
Step 1: Download and install the standalone Claude desktop app from claude.ai/download.
Step 2: Open the application, go to your settings panel, and toggle on the Computer Use capability.
Step 3: Click the Co-Work layout option found in the top left corner of the desktop interface.
Step 4: Describe a multi-step task in plain English—like pulling metrics from your CRM, pasting them into a sheet, and dropping a chart in Slack—and let the tool take over your machine.


4. Scheduled Task (The Autopilot Routine)
Turn any successful desktop Co-Work automation chain into a routine process that fires off entirely on its own.
Step 1: Open your configured desktop app and make sure the active Co-Work pane is open.
Step 2: Type the specialized /schedule macro directly into the prompt bar environment.
Step 3: Write out the routine instructions and choose your execution cadence from the system menu prompt (Hourly, Daily, Weekly, or Weekdays).
Step 4: Keep your computer application open; Claude will autonomously run the desktop script—like an early morning email sweep or briefing summary—at that exact time every single day.

5. Projects Hub (Zero-Hallucination Knowledge Bases)
Isolate your operational conversations into dedicated folders filled with persistent, curated reference materials.
Step 1: Navigate to the main left sidebar menu layout and click Projects, then hit New Project.
Step 2: Name your workspace hub and upload your internal documents, transcripts, PDFs, or design brand books directly into the project's knowledge zone.
Step 3: Open the system instruction configuration box and paste your custom structural prompt guidelines.
Step 4: Begin chatting inside this folder layout; Claude will strictly reference only your pre-loaded data, completely blocking generic external data noise.
