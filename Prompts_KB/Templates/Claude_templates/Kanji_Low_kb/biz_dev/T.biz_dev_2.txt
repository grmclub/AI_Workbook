##Build Your Personal Brand Using Alex Hormozi's Strategy Playbook

Prompt 1 — Find Your Content Corner
<role>Act as a personal brand strategist who reads every signal a creator sends through their expertise, their experience, and their market — and builds a complete map of the specific corner of the internet they can own completely, so every piece of content they publish compounds authority instead of adding noise.</role>
<task>Step into my market and show me exactly which content corner I can own — so every post, video, and article I publish builds toward a position nobody else in my space holds.</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my current expertise, my target audience, and the last three pieces of content I published before starting
2. Map the content landscape in my niche — who owns what position, what is oversaturated, and where the gaps are
3. Identify the gap between what I currently talk about and what I could uniquely own based on my real experience
4. Name the one specific content corner that sits at the intersection of my expertise, my audience's biggest problem, and the market's blind spot
5. Predict every content topic I'm currently covering that dilutes my position and should be cut or deprioritized
6. Deliver the complete content corner map, the position to own, and the 3 content pillars that reinforce it every week
</steps>
<rules>
- The content corner must be specific enough to say no to off-topic requests — "business advice" doesn't count
- Market gaps must be named explicitly — not described as "be more authentic" or "add more value"
- Gap between current content and ownable position must be named honestly
- Every cut recommendation must connect to the position — not personal preference
- Test: if someone read 10 of my posts in a row would they know exactly what I stand for and who I serve
</rules>
<output>Content Landscape Map → Current vs Ownable Position Gap → The One Content Corner → 3 Content Pillars → What to Cut</output>

Prompt 2 — Build Your Value Giving Engine
<role>Act as a content value strategist who reverse-engineers why certain personal brands make people feel indebted before they ever buy anything — and builds a complete map of the specific knowledge, frameworks, and insights a person can give away freely that make paid offers feel inevitable.</role>
<task>Step into my audience's shoes and show me exactly what free value would make them feel like buying from me is the obvious next step — so my content does the selling before I ever make an offer.</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my expertise, my paid offer, and the most common question my audience asks me before starting
2. Map everything my audience is currently paying others to learn that I could teach for free
3. Identify the gap between the value I give away and the value I hold back — most personal brands protect the wrong things
4. Name the 5 most valuable pieces of knowledge I could give away completely for free without cannibalizing my paid offer
5. Predict the one free resource — a framework, a system, a breakdown — that would make my audience share my content without being asked
6. Deliver the complete value map, the 5 give-away assets, the one shareable resource, and the content calendar to deploy them in the next 30 days
</steps>
<output>Audience Payment Map → Give-Away vs Hold-Back Gap → 5 Free Assets → The One Shareable Resource → 30-Day Deployment Calendar</output>

Prompt 3 — Design Your Offer So Good They Feel Stupid Saying No
<role>Act as an offer architect who reads every signal a market sends through its objections, its frustrations, and its unspoken desires — and builds a complete map of the specific offer structure that makes saying yes feel obvious and saying no feel like leaving money on the table.</role>
<task>Step into my buyer's decision moment and show me exactly what my offer needs to look like for a qualified person to feel like saying no would be a mistake — so my conversion rate goes up without lowering my price.</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my current offer, its price, and the most common objection I hear before starting
2. Map every risk, doubt, and friction point standing between my ideal buyer and a yes
3. Identify the gap between the value my offer delivers and the value my ideal buyer perceives before purchasing
4. Name the specific guarantees, bonuses, or reframes that collapse each objection without discounting
5. Predict the one structural change to my offer that increases perceived value more than any price reduction would
6. Deliver the complete objection map, the reframed offer structure, and the exact language to use when presenting it
</steps>
<rules>
- Every objection must be addressed structurally — not with better copywriting over a weak offer
- Value gap must be named from the buyer's perspective — never from what I think they should value
- Guarantees must be specific enough to be believable — vague guarantees increase skepticism
- The one structural change must be implementable before the next sales conversation
- Test: if my ideal buyer read my offer and still said no would I know exactly why
</rules>
<output>Buyer Risk and Friction Map → Perceived vs Delivered Value Gap → Objection Collapse Plan → Reframed Offer Structure → Exact Presentation Language</output>

Prompt 4 — Audit Your Content for Maximum Shareability
<role>Act as a content shareability analyst who reads every post, video, and article a personal brand publishes — and builds a complete map of why certain pieces travel far beyond the original audience while others die with the people who already follow you.</role>
<task>Step into a stranger's first encounter with my content and show me exactly what makes them share it or scroll past — so every piece I publish is engineered to reach people who have never heard of me.</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my last 5 pieces of content, their format, and their engagement numbers before starting
2. Map the specific elements that made my best-performing content travel — the hook, the structure, the insight, the emotion
3. Identify the gap between content I create for my existing audience and content a complete stranger would stop and share
4. Name the 3 shareability triggers missing from my current content — the specific emotional or intellectual responses that make people forward something
5. Predict the one content format in my niche that consistently reaches cold audiences and why my current format isn't doing it
6. Deliver the complete shareability map, the 3 missing triggers, the recommended format shift, and the rewritten hook for my next piece
</steps>
<rules>
- Shareability triggers must be named specifically — not described as "make it more relatable"
- Gap between existing-audience content and stranger-shareable content must be named explicitly
- Format recommendation must fit my current production capacity — not aspirational formats I can't sustain
- The rewritten hook must be specific enough to use in my next post without editing
- Test: if someone who had never heard of me saw this piece of content would they send it to someone they know
</rules>
<output>Best-Performing Content Map → Existing Audience vs Stranger Shareability Gap
→ 3 Missing Triggers → Format Shift → Rewritten Hook for Next Post</output>

Prompt 5 — Build Your Personal Brand Compounding System
<role>Act as a personal brand compounding strategist who takes a creator's current content output and builds the complete system for turning one idea into ten assets, one platform into five audiences, and one piece of content into six months of compounding reach — without creating more from scratch.</role>
<task>Step into my current content workflow and show me exactly how to turn what I already create into a compounding system that grows my personal brand across every platform — without doubling my working hours.</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my primary content format, my current platforms, and my weekly content output before starting
2. Map every platform my audience lives on and the specific content format each one rewards
3. Identify the gap between the content I create once and the reach I could get if I repurposed it intelligently
4. Name the exact repurposing chain for my primary content format — how one long-form piece becomes short clips, carousels, threads, emails, and quotes
5. Predict the one distribution channel I am currently ignoring that would compound my reach the fastest given my current audience
6. Deliver the complete compounding plan, the one-piece-to-many system, the weekly schedule for executing it, and the 90-day milestone that proves it's working
</steps>
<output>Platform and Format Map → Single-Piece to Platform Gap → Repurposing Chain → The Ignored Distribution Channel → Weekly Execution Schedule → 90-Day Milestone</output>
