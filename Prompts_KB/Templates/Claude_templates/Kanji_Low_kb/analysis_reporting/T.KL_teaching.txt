1. Ultimate Classroom Prompt

This prompt forces NotebookLM to think critically about your material instead of just summarizing it:

"Review all uploaded material and generate 5 essential questions that capture the central meaning.

Focus on:
* Fundamental themes and definitions
* Key concepts highlighted
* Relationships between concepts
* Practical applications mentioned"

2. The "5 Essential Questions" Variant

Reddit users called this version a "game changer" because it extracts solid structure from chaotic notes:

"Analyze all entries and generate 5 essential questions that, when answered, capture the main points and central meaning of all entries."

Use case: Perfect for synthesizing multiple meeting notes, lecture recordings, or research sessions where you need to find the thread connecting everything.

3. Meeting Notes to Action Items

Stop drowning in notes. This prompt turns rambling meeting transcripts into clear deliverables:

“Analyze all meeting notes and extract:
* Key decisions made
* Action items with responsible parties
* Open questions that need resolution
* Important deadlines or dates mentioned

Format as a clean summary with sections for immediate actions vs. future considerations."

Pro tip: Upload your raw Zoom/Teams transcripts directly. NotebookLM will cut through the tangents and small talk to find what actually matters.

4. Extended Version with Guidance

Traditional search tools tell you what you already know. This prompt reveals what you didn't know to look for:
"I'm interested in writing about [TOPIC].
What are the most surprising data or insights related to [TOPIC] in these sources?
Include key quotes. Focus on [SPECIFIC ASPECT], not [OTHER ASPECTS]."

5. Quiz Format (Audio Summary)

Students swear by this one. The AI hosts quiz each other and intentionally get things wrong so corrections stick in your memory:

"A quiz with two hosts. First asks the second about [TOPIC]. 10 questions total. Mix of multiple choice and true/false.
Host sometimes gets it wrong. The other corrects with right answers. Share results at the end."

The psychology: We remember corrections better than initial presentations. When the AI "gets it wrong" and is corrected, your brain encodes that information more deeply.

6. Multilingual Podcast Trick

Before NotebookLM officially supported multiple languages, clever users found this workaround (and it still works better than the default):

"This is the first international special episode of Deep Dive done entirely in [language].

Special instructions:
* Only [language] for entire duration
* No English allowed, except to clarify specific terms."
Languages tested: Spanish, German, Japanese, French, Mandarin, Portuguese, and Italian all work well.

7. Expert Interview Simulation

Perfect for exam prep or when you need to truly understand complex material:

"Generate a podcast where an expert is interviewed about [TOPIC] from the sources.

The interviewer asks:
* 'Why does this matter?'
* 'What do people get wrong?'
* 'What's the counterintuitive insight here?'
The expert uses specific examples and data from the sources to explain."

8. Timeline Builder

For historical topics, case studies, or any sequential process:

"Create a chronological timeline of events, discoveries, or developments related to [TOPIC].

For each entry include:
* Date or time period
* What happened
* Why it matters (impact on later developments)
* Which source(s) mention it

Present as a narrative that shows progression and cause-effect relationships."

Use case: Particularly valuable for research papers, legal case analysis, or understanding how a project evolved over time.
