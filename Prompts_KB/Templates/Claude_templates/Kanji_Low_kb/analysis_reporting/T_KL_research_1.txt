2. PRODUCT MANAGER PERSONA (Official Google)
Transforms documents into decision memos: "Act as a Lead Product Manager reviewing internal documentation. Ruthlessly scan for actionable insights, ignoring fluff. Synthesize into "Decision Memo" format:
* User Evidence: Direct quotes indicating user problems
* Feasibility Checks: Technical constraints mentioned
* Blind Spots: What's missing from source text Use bullets. If I ask vague questions, force me to clarify."

3. SCIENTIFIC RESEARCHER PERSONA (Official Google)
For academics who need methodology over conclusions: "Act as research assistant for a senior scientist. Tone: strictly objective, formal, precise. Assume advanced knowledge of [FIELD]. Don't define standard terminology. Focus on methodology, data integrity, and conflicting evidence. Prioritize sample size, experimental design, and statistical significance over general conclusions. Format with bolded sections:
* Key Findings
* Methodological Strengths/Weaknesses
* Contradictions"

4. LITERATURE REVIEW THEMES PROMPT
For researchers synthesizing multiple papers: "From papers on [TOPIC], identify 5-10 most recurring themes. For each theme provide:
1. Short definition in your own words
2. Which papers mention it (with citations)
3. One sentence on how it's treated (debated, assumed, tested) Present as a structured table."

5. FINDING CONTRADICTIONS PROMPT
Surfaces disagreements across your sources: "From papers on [TOPIC], identify major contradictions or conflicting findings. For each contradiction provide:
1. Specific claim from each side (quoted with citations)
2. Possible reasons for disagreement (method, sample, context)
3. What evidence would resolve the conflict"

3. Expert Explainer

"Explain [Topic] as if you're teaching an intelligent beginner. Break down complex concepts into simple language, analogies, examples, and step-by-step explanations."

4. Research Gap Finder

"Analyze the current knowledge around [Topic] and identify unanswered questions, underexplored areas, emerging opportunities, and potential future research directions."

5. Critical Thinking Analyzer

##Debate

Prompt #1: The Steel Man Destroyer
"I'm about to make this decision: [paste]. Steel man every argument AGAINST it. Be brutal. Don't soften anything. Then tell me what I'm missing that I'm probably too close to see."
Most people use AI to confirm what they already think.
This one stops you from making expensive mistakes.

Prompt #2: The Constraint Architect
"Here is my goal: [X]. Here are my real constraints: time [Y], budget [Z], team size [N]. Don't give me the ideal solution. Give me the best solution that works WITHIN these exact constraints. Flag any assumption you make."
Generic advice is useless. Constrained advice is actionable.

Prompt #3: The Second Order Thinker
"I'm considering this move: [paste]. Map out the first, second, and third order consequences. Include what could go right, what could go wrong, and what most people in my position fail to anticipate."
Chess players think 3 moves ahead. This prompt does it for your business.

Prompt #4: The Invisible Bottleneck Finder
"Here is everything I do in a typical week: [paste]. Identify the single constraint that, if removed, would create the most leverage across everything else. Reference the Theory of Constraints in your reasoning."
You're not short on effort. You're short on clarity about where effort actually compounds.

Prompt #5: The Pricing Psychologist
"My offer is [X] priced at [Y]. Analyse my pricing using these three lenses: anchoring theory, loss aversion, and value perception. Then rewrite how I present the price so it feels like a no-brainer without discounting."
Price is never just a number. It's a story.

Prompt #6: The Assumption Auditor
"Here is my current business model / strategy: [paste]. List every assumption this depends on being true. Rate each assumption by how confident I should actually be in it. Flag the ones that, if wrong, would collapse the whole thing."
Most strategies fail not from bad execution but from unchecked assumptions.

Prompt #7: The Narrative Extractor
"Here are some rough notes from my experience / story: [paste]. Extract the core narrative arc. Identify the moment of tension, the turning point, and the insight. Then write it as a 150-word story that makes someone feel something."
Facts inform. Stories convert. This prompt finds the story buried in your experience.

Prompt #8: The Pre-Mortem
"Assume it's 12 months from now and this project / launch / strategy completely failed. Walk me through exactly what went wrong, in order. Be specific. Then tell me what I should do differently starting Monday."
This is the most uncomfortable prompt on this list.
It's also the most valuable.
