##prompts to generate lessons, activities, assessments, and more

1.The Course Builder
“Turn this book into a complete course focused on a specific topic I want to teach.
Break the content into structured modules and lessons. For each lesson, define clear learning objectives, key concepts, and simple explanations suitable for beginners using clear language, relatable analogies, and practical examples.
For each module, include a short activity or exercise that helps apply the concepts, and identify common misunderstandings students may have, along with clear explanations to address them.
Finally, create a simple assessment that tests understanding by requiring application of the concepts in new situations not directly covered in the material.”

2.The Smart Summarizer
“Summarize this book with a focus on the parts most relevant to the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Organize the summary so it can be used directly within lessons, clearly indicating where each concept fits within a lesson or module and how it supports the overall learning objectives.
Use clear, simple language that students can easily understand, and include relatable examples or brief explanations where necessary to improve clarity.
Highlight the most important ideas, concepts, and supporting examples, and present them in a logical sequence that builds understanding step by step.
Ensure the final output is detailed enough to support teaching while remaining concise and easy for students to follow.”

3.The Lesson Designer
“Turn the key ideas from this book into well-structured lessons for the topic I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Break the content into clear lessons within modules, and for each lesson, define clear learning objectives, key concepts, and step-by-step explanations that are easy for students to follow.
Use simple language, relatable analogies, and practical examples drawn from the book to make the material understandable for beginners while maintaining accuracy.
For each lesson, include at least one example and one short activity based on the book’s content to help students apply what they have learned.
Identify common points of confusion within each lesson and provide clear explanations to prevent misunderstandings.
Ensure the lessons follow a logical progression, allowing students to move from basic understanding to deeper comprehension, using the book as the primary source.”

4.The Practice Generator
“Create practice activities based on this book for the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Design exercises for each key concept that help students apply what they learn rather than simply recall information.
Include a mix of question types, such as short-answer questions, scenarios, and problem-solving tasks, all grounded in the book’s content.
For each activity, clearly state the objective, provide instructions, and include a sample answer or explanation to guide understanding.
Identify common mistakes students might make when attempting these exercises and explain how to avoid them.
Ensure the activities progress from basic application to more advanced use, allowing students to gradually build confidence and mastery.”

5.The Misconception Clarifier
“Identify common misunderstandings students may have about the lesson I want to teach from this book: [insert lesson topic].
For each misconception, explain why it seems correct at first, then clarify why it is incorrect using clear reasoning and examples from the book.
Provide simple, accurate explanations that replace each misunderstanding with the correct concept in a way students can easily grasp.
Include brief examples or comparisons drawn from the book to reinforce the correct understanding.
Highlight the most critical misconceptions that could hinder deeper learning if not addressed early.
Ensure the explanations are clear, concise, and directly tied to the book’s content so they can be used effectively during teaching.”

6.The Assessment Creator
“Create assessments based on this book for the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Design questions that test students’ understanding of key concepts rather than simple memorization, using material directly from the book.
Include a mix of question types, such as short-answer questions, application scenarios, and analytical questions.
For each question, provide a clear model answer and explain the reasoning behind it.
Ensure the assessment covers the most important ideas from the lesson and aligns with the learning objectives.
Include one or two higher-level questions that require students to apply the concepts in new situations not directly covered in the book.”

7.The Lesson Planner
“Create a structured lesson plan based on this book for the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Organize the lesson into a clear sequence, including an introduction that builds interest, a main explanation section, guided examples, and a short practice activity.
Define clear learning objectives and identify the key concepts that should be covered using content from the book.
Use simple language, relatable examples, and explanations drawn from the book to make the material easy for students to understand.
Include a section that addresses common challenges students may face and provide strategies to clarify them during the lesson.
End the lesson with a brief assessment or reflection activity to check understanding and reinforce learning.”

8.The Content Organizer
“Organize the content of this book for the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic].
Break the material into clear sections and subtopics that can be easily transformed into lessons and modules.
Identify the most important concepts, supporting ideas, and examples from the book, and arrange them in a logical order that builds understanding step by step.
Show how each section connects to the next so the lesson flows naturally and students can follow the progression with ease.
Highlight which parts of the book are essential for the lesson and which can be treated as supporting or optional material.
Ensure the final structure is clear, practical, and ready to use for teaching.”

9.The Key Concepts Prioritizer
“Analyze this book for the lesson I want to teach: [insert lesson topic], and identify the most important concepts students must understand.
Select and rank the top ideas, frameworks, or principles based on their importance and impact on understanding the topic.
For each concept, explain why it matters, how it connects to other ideas in the book, and what understanding it enables for the student.
Focus on the content that delivers the greatest learning value while avoiding less relevant details.
Indicate which parts of the book can be deprioritized without affecting students’ core understanding.
Ensure the final output helps me focus my teaching on what matters most.”

10. The Teaching Readiness Evaluator
“Use this book to prepare me to teach the lesson: [insert lesson topic].
Identify the core concepts I must fully understand before teaching, and explain them clearly using the book as the primary source.
Outline a practical teaching plan I can follow, including how to introduce the topic, explain the key ideas, and guide students through relevant examples.
Include a brief self-assessment that allows me to test whether I truly understand the material well enough to teach it without relying on notes.
Highlight any areas where teachers commonly struggle and suggest practical ways to strengthen my understanding before delivering the lesson.
Ensure the final output helps me progress from understanding the book to confidently teaching the lesson.”
