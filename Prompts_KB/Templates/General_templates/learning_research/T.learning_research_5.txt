AI workflow, learning 10 prompts

1. Personalized Learning Roadmap Builder
Prompt:
“Act as an expert instructional designer. Review my uploaded materials and build an 8-week mastery curriculum.
→ Define the core learning objective for each week.
→ Assign the exact sources and sections to study.
→ Create one active recall exercise and one deliberate practice task per week.
→ Include one spaced review session covering previously studied material.
→ End each week with a real-world milestone project.”

2. Socratic Learning Coach
Prompt:
“Act as a Socratic tutor for the material in these uploads. Do not provide direct answers.
→ Ask one increasingly difficult question at a time.
→ Require me to justify every answer using evidence from the sources.
→ Point out misconceptions, weak reasoning, and missing assumptions.
→ Provide hints instead of answers when I struggle.
→ Periodically revisit earlier concepts to strengthen retention.”

3. First Principles Explainer
Prompt:
“Analyze all my uploaded materials and reduce every major idea to its first principles.
→ Identify the foundational truths behind each concept.
→ Explain how complex theories emerge from those fundamentals.
→ Highlight dependencies and assumptions between concepts.
→ Point out areas of uncertainty or disagreement within the sources.
→ End by asking me to reconstruct the theory from memory.”

4. Feynman Learning Coach
Prompt:
“Explain the most difficult concepts in these sources using the Feynman Technique.
→ Step 1: Explain the concept in simple language using an everyday analogy.
→ Step 2: Connect the analogy to the technical terms used in the text.
→ Step 3: Ask me to teach the concept back from memory.
→ Step 4: Evaluate my explanation strictly against the sources and identify gaps.
→ Step 5: Present a new scenario that requires applying the concept.”

5. Expert Thinking Framework Extractor
Prompt:
“Scan my uploaded materials and identify the most important mental models used by experts.
→ Define each model in plain language.
→ Show where it appears in the sources.
→ Explain why experts rely on it.
→ Describe common beginner mistakes.
→ Present a realistic scenario in which I must apply the model.”

6. Knowledge Transfer Accelerator
Prompt:
“I understand [Concept A] but struggle with [Concept B]. Analyze these uploads and accelerate my learning.
→ Identify the structural similarities between both concepts.
→ Explain Concept B using frameworks and analogies from Concept A.
→ Highlight where the analogy breaks down.
→ Create comparison tables showing similarities and differences.
→ Test my ability to transfer knowledge between both concepts.”

7. Real-World Practice Simulator
Prompt:
“Act as a realistic simulator based entirely on my uploaded materials. Present progressively difficult scenarios one at a time.
→ Score my decisions using explicit criteria drawn from the sources.
→ Explain the short- and long-term consequences.
→ Compare my reasoning with the reasoning demonstrated by experts in the materials.
→ Identify recurring mistakes in my thinking.
→ Adapt future scenarios to target my weakest skills.”

8. Critical Thinking Reviewer
Prompt:
“I am going to explain my understanding of the uploaded material below. Act as a rigorous academic reviewer.
→ Identify unsupported claims, logical fallacies, and weak arguments.
→ Expose missing evidence and overlooked edge cases.
→ Present counterarguments drawn from the sources.
→ Highlight alternative interpretations.
→ Ask probing questions that force me to strengthen my reasoning.
My explanation: [Insert Explanation]”

9. Long-Term Memory Builder
Prompt:
“I need to permanently retain the core ideas from these uploads. Generate a 30-card active recall deck based entirely on the text.
→ Prioritize counterintuitive insights and common misconceptions.
→ Include scenario-based application questions.
→ Focus on concepts with the highest long-term value.
→ Tag each card by difficulty level.
→ Format the entire deck as a table for export to Anki or Quizlet.”

10. Final Mastery Assessment
Prompt:
“Based entirely on the material I have uploaded, generate a rigorous mastery assessment.
→ Part 1: 5 conceptual short-answer questions.
→ Part 2: 3 real-world problem-solving scenarios.
→ Part 3: 1 complex synthesis question that combines multiple sources.
Wait for my answers, grade them strictly using citations, explain every deduction, and identify my remaining knowledge gaps.”

Finding reliable academic references with AI involves more than simply generating citations. It requires identifying peer-reviewed sources, verifying publication details, retrieving accurate DOIs, excluding non-academic content, and ensuring that references align with specific sections of a research paper without introducing fabricated citations.

1. The Master Reference Finder
"You are an experienced academic researcher with expertise in scholarly literature retrieval. Provide only peer-reviewed references indexed in recognized academic databases such as Scopus, Web of Science, PubMed, IEEE Xplore, DOAJ, Springer, or other reputable discipline-specific databases.
Recommend 10 references on: [Topic].
Apply the following criteria before selecting any reference:
- Prioritize primary peer-reviewed research articles over secondary summaries whenever appropriate.
- Prefer studies published within the last five years while retaining seminal or foundational studies when they remain essential to understanding the topic.
- Ensure that each reference is directly relevant to the research question, variables, constructs, hypotheses, or objectives under investigation.
- Verify that each reference contains complete bibliographic information, including authors, publication year, article title, journal name, volume, issue, page range, and DOI whenever available.
- Exclude references with incomplete, inconsistent, or unverifiable publication details.
- Do not infer, reconstruct, or fabricate missing citation information.
- If the peer-review status or authenticity of a source cannot be reasonably verified, exclude it from the final output.
- Clearly justify the inclusion of each accepted reference based on its methodological quality, theoretical contribution, empirical significance, or relevance to the selected manuscript section.
Link each reference directly to one section of a scientific paper: Introduction, Theoretical Framework, Methodology, Results, or Discussion.
For each reference, provide:
- Full APA 7 citation.
- Authors.
- Publication year.
- Journal name.
- DOI (if available).
- Assigned manuscript section.
- A brief justification explaining why the reference is appropriate for that section.
Present the references as an organized list grouped according to the relevant section of the scientific paper.
Before presenting the final output, confirm that every reference:
✓ Is peer-reviewed.
✓ Is directly relevant to the research topic.
✓ Meets the predefined inclusion criteria.
✓ Contains complete bibliographic information.
✓ Includes a DOI whenever available.
✓ Has not been fabricated or inferred from incomplete information.
✓ Is presented according to APA 7 guidelines.
If sufficient high-quality peer-reviewed evidence is unavailable, clearly state: 'No sufficient peer-reviewed sources available.' When uncertainty exists regarding the accuracy or existence of a citation, acknowledge the limitation rather than presenting potentially misleading information."
