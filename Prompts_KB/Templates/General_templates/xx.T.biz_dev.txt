1. The Role + Stakes Prompt 

"Act as a senior [role] who has seen people fail at this exact problem a hundred times. I'm about to [describe the situation]. What mistakes am I about to make and how do I avoid them?" Adding stakes forces sharper, more specific answers.

2. The Contrarian Take 

"Here is a popular belief in my industry: [paste it]. Now argue against it using real logic, not just counterpoints. Give me the version of this argument that would actually make someone stop and reconsider." Use this when you want content that cuts through noise.

3. The Decision Filter 

"I need to make a decision between [Option A] and [Option B]. My goal is [outcome]. My biggest fear is [risk]. Don't give me a balanced answer, tell me which one you'd choose and why, then tell me what I'm probably not seeing." Stops AI from giving you the both-sides answer that helps nobody.

4. The Email That Gets Read 

"Write a cold email to [type of person]. The goal is [one specific action]. Open with a line about a problem they actually have not a compliment. No more than 100 words. No 'I hope this finds you well.' Subject line under 6 words." Paste the output. Edit one line. Send it.

5. The Idea Stress Test 

"Here is my business idea / offer / plan: [paste it]. Pretend you're an investor who has heard 500 pitches and funded 3. Tell me the first 3 things that would make you say no and what would have to change for you to say yes." Brutal. Useful. Saves months.

6. The Writing Sharpener 

"Here is a piece of writing: [paste it]. Remove every sentence that doesn't earn its place. Flag anything that sounds like AI. Rewrite the opening line so it stops someone mid-scroll. Don't explain your changes, just make them." This is the edit your first draft always needs.

7. The Learning Accelerator 

"I want to understand [topic] deeply not just the basics. Teach me the part most beginners skip, the most common misconception, and the one mental model that changes how everything else makes sense. Use one real example." Better than any course for getting past surface-level understanding.

##using Claude as a founder

1. Start here
Paste this first, before anything else:
"You are now in founder-operator mode. Stop giving me balanced overviews. When I bring you a problem, give me the single decision you'd actually make if it were your company and your money. Include the trade-off you're accepting and what you'd watch for in the next 90 days. Start every answer with 'Here's what I'd do.'"

2. Hiring
Same chat, next message:
"I'm about to hire my first [role]. Give me 5 interview questions that separate people who are actually good at the job from people who just interview well. Then tell me the one red flag most founders don't catch until month 3."

3. Pricing
"Here's what I sell and what I currently charge: [describe]. Tell me honestly if I'm underpriced, and what signal in my own business is telling me the answer. Don't ask me to guess tell me what to look for."

4. Competitor positioning
"My closest competitor is [name or describe them]. Find the gap in how they're positioning themselves that I can own. Give me 3 moves that make them less relevant to my exact customer within 12 months."

5. The "should I even do this" filter
"I'm considering this new offer/product: [describe]. Stress test it like an investor who's seen 500 pitches this year. Tell me what breaks first, and what I'm probably not seeing because I'm too close to it."

6. Time and focus
"Here's everything on my plate this week: [list it]. Tell me what to drop completely, not just deprioritize. Be specific about why each thing doesn't move the business forward."

7. The reality check
"I'm about to make this decision: [describe]. Before I commit, tell me the version of this that fails quietly over 6 months, not the dramatic failure, the slow one. What would I notice first if I were paying attention?"
