## 8 ChatGPT Prompts for Creating Comprehensive English Lesson Plans in Word Format 👇👇

1️. Create a Reading Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English reading lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] on [topic/text]. Use the PWP framework: Pre Reading, While Reading, and Post Reading. Include clear learning objectives, required materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. In the Pre Reading stage, activate prior knowledge, introduce the context, and prepare learners for the text. In the While Reading stage, progress from understanding the main idea to identifying key details and deeper comprehension where appropriate. In the Post Reading stage, extend the text through meaningful speaking, writing, or language work. Ensure all activities are scaffolded, engaging, and appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

2️. Create a Listening Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English listening lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] on [topic/listening text]. Use the PWP framework: Pre Listening, While Listening, and Post Listening. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. In the Pre Listening stage, activate prior knowledge, establish context, and prepare learners for the listening. In the While Listening stage, progress from listening for the main idea to specific details and deeper understanding where appropriate. In the Post Listening stage, extend learning through meaningful speaking, writing, or language activities. Ensure all tasks are scaffolded, engaging, and appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

3️. Create a Grammar Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English grammar lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] to teach [target grammar point] in the context of [topic/situation]. Use the PPP framework: Presentation, Practice, and Production. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Present the target grammar in a meaningful context, clarify its meaning, form, and use, then move from controlled practice to freer communicative use. Include appropriate concept checking and opportunities for learners to use the grammar meaningfully. Ensure all activities are scaffolded, engaging, and appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

4️. Create a Vocabulary Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English vocabulary lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] to teach [target vocabulary] in the context of [topic/theme]. Use the ECRIF framework: Encounter, Clarify, Remember, Internalize, and Fluently Use. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Introduce the vocabulary in meaningful context, clarify meaning, form, and pronunciation, and provide activities that move from recognition and retrieval to meaningful and increasingly independent use. Ensure all activities are scaffolded, engaging, and appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

5️. Create a Speaking Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English speaking lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] on [topic/communicative goal]. Use the TBL framework: Pre Task, Task Cycle, and Language Focus. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Prepare learners for the speaking task with useful language and context, then engage them in a meaningful pair or group task that requires genuine communication. Follow the task with feedback and focused work on useful language, pronunciation, accuracy, and fluency where needed. Ensure activities maximize learner speaking time and are appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

6️. Create a Writing Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English writing lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] to develop [target writing skill/text type] on [topic]. Use a Task Based Learning framework: Pre Task, Task Cycle, and Language Focus. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Prepare learners with relevant ideas, vocabulary, and a clear model where appropriate, then guide them through planning, drafting, and completing a meaningful writing task. Include opportunities for feedback and revision, followed by focused work on useful language, organization, accuracy, and conventions. Ensure all activities are scaffolded and appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

8️. Create a Diagnostic and Responsive Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] to diagnose and address their understanding of [target language/skill]. Use the TTT framework: Test, Teach, Test. Begin with a short diagnostic task to identify what learners can already do and where they need support. Use the results to provide focused teaching and practice, then repeat a similar communicative task so learners can demonstrate improvement. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Ensure the teaching stage responds directly to the needs revealed by the initial task and that all activities are appropriate to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

7️. Create an Integrated Skills Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a comprehensive English lesson plan for [age/grade] learners at [CEFR level] on [topic/theme], integrating listening, speaking, reading, and writing where appropriate. Use the ESA framework: Engage, Study, and Activate. Include clear learning objectives, materials, stage by stage procedures, timing, interaction patterns, teacher instructions, differentiation, anticipated problems and solutions, and assessment. Begin with an engaging activity that activates learners’ interest and prior knowledge, provide focused work on relevant language and skills, and then move to a meaningful communicative task where learners apply what they have learned. Ensure the skills are logically connected rather than taught as separate activities, and adapt all tasks to the learners’ CEFR level. Present the complete lesson plan in a professionally structured, editable Word document, using clear headings and tables where appropriate."

## Here are 7 ChatGPT prompts to help teachers create clear, practical, and customizable resources. 👇

1️. Create a Professional Teaching Portfolio
Prompt:
"Create a professional teaching portfolio based on [subject, grade/age/level, experience, qualifications, roles, and other relevant details] for [evaluation/job application/promotion/accreditation/professional development/other purpose]. Organize it into relevant sections such as professional profile, teaching philosophy, qualifications and experience, curriculum and instructional planning, teaching and assessment practices, evidence of student learning, professional development, collaboration, achievements, and reflective practice. For each section, identify what information and supporting evidence to include, distinguish claims from evidence, and suggest appropriate artifacts such as plans, assessments, anonymized student work, observation feedback, certificates, and reflections. Keep the portfolio clear, concise, professional, and coherent. Do not invent information or evidence, and identify anything important that is missing."

2️. Create an Annual Curriculum Distribution
Prompt:
"Create an annual curriculum distribution for [subject] for [grade/age/level] during [academic year], based on these curriculum requirements: [units, topics, objectives, standards, or curriculum content]. Consider [teaching weeks, terms/semesters, holidays, exams, school events, and other constraints]. Distribute the curriculum realistically and logically, allowing sufficient time for instruction, practice, review, assessment, and consolidation. Give more time to demanding content where needed and explain why. Do not add, omit, or alter curriculum requirements. Present the plan as a clear table or timeline showing the term, week/period, unit/topic, key objectives/content, duration, and assessment/review points. Flag any scheduling conflicts, unrealistic pacing, or content that cannot fit within the available time."

3️. Create a Term or Semester Plan
Prompt:
"Create a detailed [term/semester] plan for [subject] for [grade/age/level] during [term/semester and academic year], based on [annual curriculum distribution and curriculum requirements]. Consider [teaching weeks, lesson frequency and duration, holidays, exams, school events, and other constraints]. Organize the required curriculum into a logical, realistic sequence of units, topics, and objectives, allowing sufficient time for instruction, practice, review, assessment, and consolidation. Align it with the annual curriculum distribution without adding, omitting, or altering required content. Include assessment and review points, with flexibility for interruptions and different learning paces. Present the plan as a clear table showing the week/period, unit/topic, objectives/content, duration, assessment/review points, and notes. Flag any pacing problems, scheduling conflicts, or requirements that may need adjustment."

4️. Create a Unit Plan
Prompt:
"Create a unit plan for [unit/topic] in [subject] for [grade/age/level], based on these curriculum requirements, standards, or objectives: [details]. Plan the unit across [number of lessons/weeks], with each lesson lasting [duration], and organize learning in a logical progression toward the objectives. For each lesson, include learning objectives, key content, teaching and learning activities, resources, practice opportunities, differentiation or support, and formative assessment. Include review, consolidation, and summative assessment where appropriate, ensuring alignment with the objectives. Do not add, omit, or alter required curriculum content. Present the plan as a clear table showing the lesson/sequence, objectives, content, activities, resources, assessment, and differentiation/support. Check the final plan for curriculum alignment, progression, realistic pacing, and coherence."

5️. Create a Lesson Plan
Prompt:
"Create a practical lesson plan for [lesson topic] in [subject] for [grade/age/level], based on [curriculum requirements, learning objectives, unit plan, and prior learning]. Plan the [duration] lesson with a clear beginning, development, and conclusion aligned with the objectives. For each stage, include its purpose, teacher actions, student activities, timing, resources, and checks for understanding. Include explanation, modeling, questioning, guided and independent practice, interaction, feedback, and consolidation where appropriate. Anticipate likely difficulties or misconceptions and provide suitable scaffolding, differentiation, or support. Include a brief formative assessment or exit task. Do not add or alter curriculum requirements. Present the plan as a clear, classroom-ready table and check it for alignment, sequencing, pacing, feasibility, and assessment."

7️. Create a Professional Reflection and Development Record
Prompt:
"Create a professional reflection and development record based on [teaching experience, professional development activity, classroom evidence, observation feedback, or professional goal]. Document what occurred, what I learned, how it relates to my teaching, and my next steps. Go beyond description by identifying key insights, challenges, assumptions, and implications for future practice. Distinguish clearly between what the evidence demonstrates and what can only be inferred. Identify professional learning priorities and realistic actions, including applying new learning, seeking feedback or collaboration, collecting further evidence, and reviewing progress. Do not invent experiences, achievements, evidence, activities, or outcomes. Present the record in a clear, professional format suitable for a teaching portfolio, appraisal record, professional development file, or learning journal."

6️. Create Assessment and Progress-Tracking Materials
Prompt:
"Create assessment and progress-tracking materials for [subject] for [grade/age/level], based on [curriculum requirements, learning objectives, and assessment criteria]. Choose the most appropriate format, such as a rubric, checklist, student progress tracker, or class assessment record. Ensure all criteria are clear, observable, age appropriate, and aligned with the intended learning. Where performance levels are needed, provide meaningful descriptors that clearly distinguish achievement levels. Organize objectives, skills, or criteria so progress can be recorded and reviewed over time. Do not invent student results, curriculum requirements, or assessment evidence. Present the materials in a clear, practical format suitable for regular classroom use."

NotebookLM can help you turn your sources into a complete course.

Define the course purpose, intended learners, prerequisite knowledge, overall goals, duration, delivery format, and key considerations or constraints supported by the sources. Identify the major areas the course should cover and organize them into a clear course overview that can guide the next stages of course development. Base all recommendations on the uploaded sources, and clearly indicate when the sources do not provide enough information to make a specific decision."

2️. Write Learning Outcomes

"Using only the sources added to NotebookLM, write clear, measurable learning outcomes for a course on [subject/course topic] for [educational level/age group]. Define what learners should know, understand, and be able to do by the end of the course. Use specific, observable action verbs and include an appropriate range of knowledge, skills, and higher order thinking. Ensure the outcomes are realistic, relevant to the course purpose, appropriate to the learners’ level, and supported by the uploaded sources. Do not introduce outcomes that cannot be justified by the sources."

3️. Organize the Course
Prompt:
"Using only the sources added to NotebookLM, organize a course on [subject/course topic] for [educational level/age group] into a clear and logical sequence of units or modules. Use the course learning outcomes and source content to determine what should be taught, in what order, and at what depth. For each unit, provide a title, key topics, related learning outcomes, and suggested duration where the sources provide enough information. Ensure the sequence builds progressively from foundational knowledge and skills to more advanced learning. Do not introduce content that is not supported by the uploaded sources."

4️. Plan the Lessons

"Using only the sources added to NotebookLM, plan the lessons for each unit of a course on [subject/course topic] for [educational level/age group]. Align each lesson with the relevant course learning outcomes and unit goals. For each lesson, include clear objectives, key content, suggested teaching and learning methods, learning activities, required resources, estimated timing, and appropriate checks for understanding. Ensure the lessons follow a logical progression, build on prior learning, and fit the available course time. Do not introduce content that is not supported by the uploaded sources."

5️. Create Learning Activities

"Using only the sources added to NotebookLM, create engaging learning activities for a course on [subject/course topic] for [educational level/age group]. Align each activity with the relevant learning outcomes, unit content, and lesson objectives. Include an appropriate variety of individual, pair, group, practical, problem-solving, discussion, inquiry, or project-based activities depending on the subject and learners. For each activity, provide its purpose, clear instructions, expected learner output, and suggested duration. 6️. Create Assessments

🚨 IF YOU’RE A TEACHER, LEARNING HOW TO USE CHATGPT PROPERLY CAN SAVE YOU HOURS EVERY WEEK.
Here are 10 copy-paste prompts👇

1️. CREATE A LESSON PLAN
Act as an experienced teacher. Create a detailed lesson plan for [SUBJECT/TOPIC] for [CLASS/AGE]. Include learning objectives, prerequisite knowledge, introduction, teaching activities, examples, learner activities, assessment, differentiation and homework. Use simple language appropriate for the learners.

2️. BREAK DOWN A DIFFICULT TOPIC
Explain [TOPIC] to [CLASS/AGE] in very simple language. Start with what learners already need to know, then explain the concept step by step using everyday examples. Include 3 common misconceptions and how I can correct them.

4️. CREATE TEST QUESTIONS
Create 20 assessment questions on [TOPIC] for [CLASS]. Include multiple-choice, short-answer and structured questions. Provide the correct answers and a marking guide. Make the questions test understanding, not just memorization.

5️. CREATE A QUIZ
Create a 15-question quiz on [TOPIC] for [CLASS]. Use a mixture of easy, moderate and challenging questions. Include answers separately so I can use the questions directly with my learners.

6️. DIFFERENTIATE MY LESSON
I am teaching [TOPIC] to a class with different ability levels. Create three versions of the learning activity: beginner, intermediate and advanced. Keep the same learning objective but adjust the level of support and difficulty.
7️. CREATE TEACHING NOTES
Turn the following topic into clear teaching notes for [CLASS]: [TOPIC]. Include definitions, key points, explanations, examples, common mistakes, practical applications and a short revision section. Avoid unnecessary jargon.

8️. CREATE HOMEWORK
Create 10 meaningful homework questions on [TOPIC] for [CLASS]. Include different question types and make at least 3 questions require learners to apply what they learned to real-life situations.

9️. TURN MY NOTES INTO TEACHING MATERIAL
I will paste my rough teaching notes below. Organize them into a clear teaching resource without changing the meaning. Add suitable headings, examples, bullet points and a short recap. Flag anything that appears incomplete instead of inventing information.
My notes:
[PASTE NOTES]

10. CREATE A COMPLETE TEACHING PACKAGE
Act as my teaching assistant. I am teaching [TOPIC] to [CLASS] for [DURATION]. Create a complete teaching package containing:
1. Learning objectives
2. Lesson plan
3. Teacher explanation
4. Examples
5. Class activity
6. Assessment questions
7. Answer key
8. Homework
9. Revision questions
10. Common misconceptions
Keep everything appropriate for the learners’ age and curriculum level. Do not invent curriculum requirements or factual information.

A useful workflow is:
Curriculum → Lesson objective → Lesson plan → Teaching material → Class activity → Assessment → Review.
You can use AI at almost every stage while keeping the final decision and quality control in your hands.

🔥 BONUS PROMPT: MAKE CHATGPT ASK QUESTIONS FIRST
This one can make your results much better:
Before creating anything, ask me the most important questions you need about the learners, subject, topic, curriculum, lesson duration, learning objectives and available resources. Do not ask unnecessary questions. After I answer, create the material.
One important rule:
Don’t blindly copy AI-generated teaching materials into your classroom.
Check:
✅ Facts
✅ Answers
✅ Calculations
✅ Curriculum alignment
✅ Age appropriateness
✅ Examples
✅ Assessment difficulty
Think of ChatGPT as your assistant, not your replacement.
You bring the teaching experience.
AI helps you reduce the repetitive work.

10 ready-to-use prompts for creating classroom visuals for math, science, languages, history, geography, and more. 👇👇

1/ Concept Flashcard Creator
Use this when you want to turn key terms, facts, formulas, or concepts into clear visual flashcards.
Prompt:
"Create visual flashcards for [topic] in [subject] for [grade/level]. Include [key terms/concepts]. Give each item a clear visual and short, accurate text. Use a consistent [visual style] and a simple, classroom-friendly design."
Example:
"Create visual flashcards about the solar system for 5th-grade science. Include the eight planets. Give each planet its own card with an accurate illustration, its name, and one key fact. Use a colorful, consistent design with clear, readable text."

2/ Concept Visualizer
Use this when you want to make a difficult or abstract concept easier for students to understand visually.
Prompt:
"Create a clear educational visual explaining [concept] in [subject] for [grade/level]. Use [diagram/illustration/comparison] to show how it works. Label the key parts and keep the information accurate, simple, and easy to understand."
Example:
"Create a clear educational visual explaining the water cycle for 4th-grade science. Show evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection in one connected diagram. Label each stage and use simple illustrations with arrows to show the process."

3/ Discussion Scene Creator
Use this when you want students to observe, analyze, and discuss a topic through a detailed visual.
Prompt:
"Create a detailed discussion scene about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Show [people, objects, events, or details] that encourage observation, interpretation, and discussion. Include enough visual clues for students to form ideas and support their answers. Do not add written answers."
Example:
"Create a detailed scene of a crowded medieval marketplace for 6th-grade history. Show merchants selling goods, craftspeople working, travelers arriving, different social groups interacting, and typical buildings and objects from the period. Include enough historically appropriate details for students to observe and discuss daily life. Do not add written answers."

4/ Visual Sequence Creator
Use this when you want to show a process, event, cycle, or procedure step by step.
Prompt:
"Create a [number]-panel visual sequence showing [process/event] in [subject] for [grade/level]. Show each stage clearly and in the correct order. Keep the visuals consistent, accurate, and easy to follow. Add short labels only where needed."
Example:
"Create a 6-panel visual sequence showing how a seed grows into a plant for 3rd-grade science. Show planting the seed, germination, root growth, the first shoot, leaf development, and the mature plant. Keep each stage scientifically accurate and easy to follow, with short labels for each stage."

5/ Learning Illustration Creator
Use this when you want to create a visual that supports a lesson, text, event, or concept.
Prompt:
"Create an educational illustration about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Show [key people, objects, setting, or concept] clearly and accurately. Include relevant visual details that help students understand the topic. Keep the design age-appropriate and do not add unnecessary text."
Example:
"Create an educational illustration of an ancient Egyptian farming community for 5th-grade history. Show the Nile River, farmers irrigating crops, people harvesting wheat, simple farming tools, and nearby homes. Keep the historical details appropriate and do not add unnecessary text."

6/ Classroom Poster Creator
Use this when you want to turn key information, rules, formulas, or concepts into an easy-to-read classroom poster.
Prompt:
"Create a classroom poster about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Include [key information] using short text, clear visuals, and a logical layout. Highlight the most important points and keep the design simple, accurate, and easy to read."
Example:
"Create a classroom poster about the order of operations for 6th-grade math. Include parentheses, exponents, multiplication, division, addition, and subtraction in the correct order. Add one simple worked example and use a clean, easy-to-read visual layout."

7/ Spot-the-Differences Creator
Use this when you want students to practice observation, comparison, and subject-specific thinking.
Prompt:
"Create two similar images about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level], with [number] meaningful differences. Keep the main scene consistent and make the differences relevant to [learning objective]. Do not mark or reveal the differences."
Example:
"Create two similar images of a healthy ecosystem for 5th-grade science, with 8 meaningful differences involving plants, animals, water, and human activity. Make the differences useful for discussing how environmental changes affect ecosystems. Do not mark or reveal the differences."

8/ Scenario Card Creator
Use this when you want students to apply their knowledge through problem-solving, decision-making, or discussion.
Prompt:
"Create visual scenario cards about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Show [number] realistic situations that require students to [analyze/solve/decide/explain]. Include only the information needed for each task and do not provide the answers."
Example:
"Create 6 visual scenario cards about saving and spending for 5th-grade math. Show everyday situations involving a fixed budget, item prices, discounts, and change. Ask students to decide what they can afford and calculate the cost. Do not provide the answers."

9/ Observation & Analysis Image Creator
Use this when you want students to examine a visual closely and draw conclusions using subject knowledge.
Prompt:
"Create a detailed image about [topic] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Include [key features/details] that students can observe, classify, compare, interpret, or make inferences from. Keep the visual accurate and do not add labels or answers."
Example:
"Create a detailed coastal landscape for 7th-grade geography. Show cliffs, a beach, a cave, an arch, a stack, waves, and visible signs of erosion. Include enough realistic detail for students to identify landforms and infer how erosion shaped the coastline. Do not add labels or answers."

10/ Visual Task Prompt Creator
Use this when you want to spark writing, problem-solving, prediction, investigation, or creative thinking with a visual.
Prompt:
"Create an intriguing visual about [topic/situation] for [subject] at [grade/level]. Include [key elements or clues] that encourage students to [predict/investigate/solve/explain/create]. Leave some information open to interpretation and do not provide answers."
Example:
"Create an intriguing visual for 6th-grade science showing a mysterious plant growing differently under three conditions: sunlight, partial shade, and darkness. Include visible differences in height, leaf color, and growth. Give students enough clues to predict what caused the differences and design an investigation. Do not provide answers."
