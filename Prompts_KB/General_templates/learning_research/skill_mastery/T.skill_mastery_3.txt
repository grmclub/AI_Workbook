##10 prompts can unlock a 1000-page PDF
Skip the page-by-page reading and extract what actually matters.
Here are the 10 prompts 👇

1. **Explain the entire PDF simply**
> Read this PDF carefully from beginning to end and explain it in simple, beginner-friendly language. Break it into clear sections based on the main topics. For each section, explain the key idea, important concepts, major arguments, examples, and conclusions. Avoid unnecessary jargon, and define technical terms in plain English. Finish with the 10 most important things I should remember.

2. **Turn the PDF into a 10-minute read**
> Analyze this entire PDF and condense it into a summary I can understand in about 10 minutes. Remove repetition, filler, and minor details, but keep the most important ideas, facts, statistics, examples, arguments, recommendations, and conclusions. Use clear headings and bullet points so I can scan it quickly without losing the meaning of the original document.

3. **Explain it like I am a complete beginner**
> Teach me the contents of this PDF as if I know absolutely nothing about the topic. Start with the basics and gradually move toward the more advanced concepts. Explain difficult ideas using simple language, analogies, and real-world examples. Define important technical terms before using them. After every major section, give me a short recap in 2–3 sentences.

4. **Find the 20% that gives me 80% of the value**
> Apply the 80/20 principle to this PDF. Identify the small number of concepts, ideas, frameworks, facts, or arguments that provide most of the value and understanding. Explain each one clearly, tell me why it matters, and show how it connects to the bigger picture. End with a section titled "If You Remember Only 10 Things."

5. **Create complete study notes**
> Turn this PDF into organized study notes that I can use without reopening the original document. Include clear headings, important definitions, key concepts, examples, frameworks, formulas if relevant, important facts, and conclusions. Remove unnecessary repetition while keeping enough explanation for every major idea to make sense. Highlight the information that is most important to learn or revise.

6. **Act as my personal tutor**
> Act as an expert tutor and teach me this PDF step by step. Start with the foundational ideas and gradually move toward the more difficult concepts. Explain everything in simple language and use practical examples whenever possible. After each major topic, give me 2–3 quick questions to test my understanding. If a concept is especially difficult, explain it again using a different example.

7. **Extract the most important insights**
> Read this entire PDF and identify the 20 most important insights, facts, statistics, findings, claims, or conclusions. For each one, explain what it means, why it matters, and how it connects to the main subject of the document. Prioritize insights that would significantly improve my understanding instead of simply listing random facts.

8. **Create a practical cheat sheet**
> Analyze this PDF and create a compact cheat sheet containing the most useful information from the entire document. Include the most important concepts, definitions, frameworks, processes, formulas, examples, statistics, and conclusions. Keep the explanations short and easy to scan. Organize related ideas together and prioritize information that is most useful for quickly understanding or revising the document.

9. **Find the hidden insights**
> Go beyond simply summarizing this PDF. Analyze it deeply and identify important patterns, connections, assumptions, contradictions, implications, recurring themes, and insights that an average reader might miss. For each insight, explain what you noticed, why it matters, and which parts of the document support it. Clearly separate facts stated in the PDF from your interpretation.

10. **Ultimate PDF analysis prompt**
> Analyze this entire PDF thoroughly from beginning to end. First, give me a simple 10-point overview of the document. Then explain each major section in clear, beginner-friendly language. Extract the most important concepts, definitions, facts, statistics, examples, frameworks, arguments, conclusions, and recommendations. Highlight anything surprising, especially useful, confusing, or easy to miss. Finally, give me 10 key takeaways, a short cheat sheet, and the most important things I should understand first if I only have 15 minutes.

##Here are 10 ready-to-use prompts to build better reading lessons with ChatGPT. 👇

1/ Activate Prior Knowledge & Predict
Use this when you want learners to think about a text before reading and make meaningful predictions.
Prompt:
"Create a pre-reading activity for [text/topic] for [level]. Use [title/image/key clues] to activate learners’ prior knowledge and generate predictions about the text. Include [number] short prediction questions. Do not reveal the text’s content."

2/ Read for Gist
Use this when you want learners to understand the overall meaning of a text without focusing on every word.
Prompt:
"Create a gist-reading activity for [text] for [level]. Give learners a clear reason to read quickly for the overall meaning. Include [number] short tasks or questions focused only on the main idea, purpose, or general message. Avoid questions about specific details."

3/ Practice Skimming
Use this when you want learners to read quickly to identify the main points and overall organization of a text.
Prompt:
"Create a skimming activity for [text] for [level]. Give learners a short time limit and [number] tasks focused on identifying the topic, main ideas, and overall organization. Avoid questions that require careful reading for details."

4/ Practice Scanning
Use this when you want learners to locate specific information quickly without reading the entire text carefully.
Prompt:
"Create a scanning activity for [text] for [level]. Ask learners to find specific information such as [names/dates/numbers/places/key facts] as quickly as possible. Include [number] short search tasks and encourage learners to scan rather than read every word."

5/ Handle Unknown Vocabulary
Use this when you want learners to deal with unfamiliar words without immediately using a dictionary.
Prompt:
"Create a vocabulary-in-context activity for [text] for [level]. Select [number] unfamiliar words that learners can work out from context. Ask them to identify contextual clues, guess each word’s meaning, and decide which unknown words are actually necessary for understanding the text."

6/ Make Inferences While Reading
Use this when you want learners to understand ideas that are implied rather than directly stated.
Prompt:
"Create an inference activity for [text] for [level]. Write [number] questions that require learners to combine clues from the text with their own knowledge to infer unstated meanings. Ask them to identify the textual evidence that supports each inference."

7/ Recognize Text Organization
Use this when you want learners to understand how ideas are connected and organized within a text.
Prompt:
"Create a text-organization activity for [text] for [level]. Ask learners to identify how ideas are organized, such as [sequence/comparison/cause and effect/problem and solution]. Include [number] tasks that help them notice paragraph structure, connections between ideas, and organizational clues."

8/ Build Reading Speed & Flexibility
Use this when you want learners to adjust their reading speed according to their purpose.
Prompt:
"Create a timed reading activity for [text] for [level]. Include [number] tasks that require learners to read at different speeds for different purposes, such as getting the main idea, finding specific information, and reading important sections carefully. Encourage efficient reading rather than word-by-word reading."

9/ Scaffold a Difficult Reading Text
Use this when you want to make a challenging text more accessible without explaining everything for learners.
Prompt:
"Create a scaffolded reading activity for [text] for [level]. Identify the parts learners may find difficult and provide strategic support through prompting, guiding questions, modeling, or clarification. Help learners work out the meaning themselves rather than translating or explaining the entire text."

10/ Turn Comprehension Questions into Learning Tasks
Use this when you want comprehension work to develop reading skills rather than simply test understanding.
Prompt:
"Create a reading activity for [text] for [level] that teaches rather than simply tests comprehension. Include [number] tasks that guide learners back to the text to identify clues, explain how they reached an answer, and practice useful reading strategies. Avoid questions based only on recalling information."
