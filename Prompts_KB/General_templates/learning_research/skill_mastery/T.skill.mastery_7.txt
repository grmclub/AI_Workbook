🚨 7 practical tips for differentiating learning to meet the needs of every student. 👇👇

1. Assess Readiness First
Before starting a lesson, find out what students already know, understand, and can do. Use quick questions, discussions, observations, or short pre-assessments to identify different readiness levels. Then use this information to adjust the level of challenge, support, and learning activities so students can build from their current understanding.

2. Focus on Essential Concepts
Identify the most important concepts, principles, and skills students need to understand. Build the lesson around these essentials rather than disconnected facts or activities. Students may take different learning paths, but they should all work toward the same core understandings.

3. Differentiate the Learning Process
Give students different ways to work with and make sense of the same essential concepts. Adjust activities, complexity, pacing, and support based on students’ readiness, interests, and learning profiles. The goal is not to give everyone the same task, but to provide appropriate pathways toward the same important learning goals.

4. Use Flexible Grouping
Organize students in different ways depending on the learning task. Use whole-class instruction, pairs, small groups, and individual work, and change the groups as students’ readiness, interests, and needs change. Avoid keeping students in fixed ability groups; flexible grouping gives them opportunities to work with different classmates and in different learning situations.

5. Offer Meaningful Choices
Give students appropriate choices in how they explore ideas, complete activities, or demonstrate their understanding. Choices can reflect students’ interests, strengths, and preferred ways of working while keeping everyone focused on the same essential learning goals.

6. Adjust Challenge and Support
Match the level of challenge and support to students’ readiness. Provide additional guidance, examples, or scaffolding when students need help, while offering greater complexity and independence to students who are ready for more challenge. Differentiation should change the quality and nature of the work, not simply give some students more or less work.

7. Use Multiple Ways to Show Learning
Allow students to demonstrate what they have learned in different ways. Depending on the learning goals, students might create a presentation, write a report, complete a project, solve a problem, or produce another appropriate product. Different formats can give students multiple ways to express understanding while still working toward the same essential learning goals.
