🚨 STOP READING EVERYTHING.
CLAUDE can turn ANY document into the 5 things that matter.
5 prompts to do it in minutes.

1/ Contract Risk Scanner
"Read this contract and act as a lawyer. Highlight every clause that could hurt me, anything I am agreeing to that I might regret, hidden auto-renewals, liability traps, and anything that is non-standard. Explain each one in plain English."

2/ Long Report Summarizer
"Here is a report I need to understand: [paste]. Give me the 5 most important findings, what they mean for someone in my position [describe role], and the 3 decisions I need to make based on this information."

3/ Email Thread Untangler
"Here is a long email chain: [paste]. Tell me what was agreed, what is still unresolved, who is responsible for what, and what I need to reply to move this forward. Write the reply for me."

4/ Research Paper Decoder
"Explain this research paper to me like I am smart but not an expert in this field: [paste or describe]. What did they find, why does it matter, what are the weaknesses in their methodology, and how confident should I be in these results."

5/ Meeting Notes Action Extractor
"Here are my notes from today's meeting: [paste]. Extract every action item, who owns it, the deadline if mentioned, and any decisions that were made. Then write a follow up summary I can send to everyone who attended."

## research like a PhD student Deeper research. Better sources. A clear reading path.
Here are 7 prompts to do it yourself. 👇🏻


1/ The Research System
"Act as a doctoral-level research supervisor and design a rigorous research system for [TOPIC]. Start by identifying the central problem, define the primary research question and its highest-value subquestions, map the major academic perspectives and disagreements, determine the depth required, recommend the most authoritative source types, build a targeted search strategy, and create a reading sequence that moves from foundational knowledge to the latest credible research. Prioritize depth, evidence quality, and intellectual clarity over surface-level summaries."


2/ The Source Triage Pass
"Act as an expert academic research librarian. Audit the sources I collected for [TOPIC] and separate valuable evidence from noise. Evaluate each source by authority, methodology, publication quality, recency, relevance, potential bias, and directness to my research question. Rank the sources by priority, explain why each deserves or does not deserve attention, flag anything that should be replaced, map each source to the specific question it supports, and identify the most important evidence gaps still missing from my research."

3/ The Fast Comprehension Pass
"Act as an exceptional subject-matter tutor. Take me from zero to confident, conversational understanding of [TOPIC] in one intensive learning session. Build the mental model I need first, then explain the essential concepts, vocabulary, competing schools of thought, historical development, common misconceptions, and unresolved debates. Use plain language without oversimplifying. Finish with a 12-term expert glossary, a concise timeline, real-world examples, and five progressively harder questions that reveal whether I truly understand the subject."

4/ The Argument Map
"Act as a neutral but deeply critical analyst. Deconstruct the intellectual debate surrounding [TOPIC]. Identify the major positions, strongest evidence supporting each side, hidden assumptions, methodological differences, disputed definitions, and points where opposing arguments talk past one another. Build a clear argument map showing how the positions connect, present the strongest possible case for each side, expose the weakest link in each argument, and identify what new evidence would genuinely change the debate."

5/ The Sourced Brief
"Act as a senior research strategist writing for an intelligent, time-constrained reader. Transform my research on [TOPIC] into a concise, decision-ready brief. Identify the three most consequential findings, support every major claim with credible sources, distinguish established evidence from interpretation, assign a confidence level to each finding, highlight meaningful uncertainties and unanswered questions, and end with practical recommendations. Remove unnecessary background and make every paragraph earn its place."

6/ The Counterargument Drill
"Act as a hostile peer reviewer whose job is to find every weakness in my research on [TOPIC] before a specialist does. Stress-test my claims, evidence, methodology, definitions, assumptions, causal reasoning, and logical transitions. For every vulnerability, state the strongest objection an expert could make, explain why it matters, identify the evidence required to defend or revise the claim, and provide a prioritized revision plan based on the risk each weakness creates."

7/ The Citation Cleanup
"Act as a meticulous academic editor and citation auditor. Examine my draft on [TOPIC] line by line and identify every claim that requires evidence, every citation that is weak or mismatched, every unsupported assertion, and every passage that may paraphrase a source too closely. Recommend stronger sources where necessary, correct the in-text citations, standardize the reference list according to [CITATION STYLE], flag claims that cannot currently be verified, and produce a final checklist for making the research academically defensible."

##Research article introduction

1. Establish the Research Context
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, and scholarly writing specialist. Analyze the uploaded literature to establish the research context needed for the opening section of a research article introduction.
Systematically identify:
The broader research area
The specific topic or phenomenon being investigated
Key concepts, constructs, or variables
Important definitions or conceptual distinctions
Major developments in the research area
Established knowledge directly relevant to the study
Important theoretical or conceptual perspectives
Relevant populations, settings, or contexts
Recent developments or emerging issues supported by the literature
Why the broader research area deserves scholarly attention
Synthesize evidence across the uploaded sources rather than summarizing individual studies one by one. Prioritize information that helps readers understand the study and exclude background material that is interesting but not directly relevant.
Organize the analysis under:
Broader Research Area
Specific Research Context
Key Concepts and Variables
Relevant Theoretical or Conceptual Foundations
Major Developments
Established Knowledge
Relevant Population and Context
Importance of the Research Area
Essential Background for the Introduction
Then develop a concise Research Context Framework showing how the introduction should move logically from the broader research area toward the specific topic of the article.
Conclude by drafting 2–3 academically written opening paragraphs that establish the research context using evidence from the uploaded sources and appropriate citations.
Do not introduce the research gap, research problem, objectives, or study contribution yet. These will be developed systematically in subsequent steps.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent facts, trends, theories, findings, statistics, concepts, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded sources."

2. Synthesize What Is Already Known
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, literature synthesis specialist, and scholarly writing expert. Analyze the uploaded literature to establish what is currently known about the specific research topic and synthesize the most relevant evidence for the research article introduction.
Systematically identify:
Major findings directly related to the research topic
Key relationships among concepts, constructs, or variables
Findings consistently supported across multiple studies
Important areas of agreement in the literature
Areas where findings differ or remain inconsistent
Dominant theoretical or conceptual explanations
Important empirical patterns
Relevant findings concerning the study population or context
Recent developments that have shaped current understanding
Evidence most directly connected to the present study
Synthesize the literature thematically and conceptually rather than summarizing studies one by one. Show how findings from different sources relate to, support, extend, or differ from one another.
Distinguish between:
Well-established knowledge
Findings supported by several studies
Emerging evidence
Inconsistent or contested findings
Claims supported by limited evidence
For each major theme, provide:
Theme or Area of Knowledge
What the Literature Establishes
Supporting Sources
Degree of Agreement Across Studies
Relevance to the Present Study
Then develop a concise Current Knowledge Synthesis that explains what researchers presently understand about the topic and creates a logical transition toward the specific research problem.
Conclude by drafting 2–3 academically written paragraphs that synthesize the most relevant existing knowledge for inclusion in the research article introduction, using appropriate citations from the uploaded sources.
Keep the synthesis selective. Include only literature necessary to establish the argument for the present study rather than attempting to reproduce a full literature review.
Do not identify or claim the definitive research gap yet. Do not write the research objectives or contribution at this stage. The purpose of this step is to establish what is already known before determining what remains problematic or unresolved.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent findings, relationships, theories, trends, statistics, populations, contexts, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded sources."

3. Identify the Research Problem
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, research methodologist, and scholarly writing specialist. Analyze the uploaded literature to identify and clearly articulate the specific research problem that should be established in the introduction of the research article.
Using the research context and current knowledge synthesis developed in the previous steps, determine the specific issue, limitation, contradiction, unresolved concern, or knowledge deficiency that creates the need for the present study.
Systematically examine the literature for:
Persistent problems reported across studies
Important unresolved questions
Contradictory or inconsistent findings
Relationships among concepts or variables that remain unclear
Limitations in current explanations or approaches
Problems affecting particular populations, settings, or contexts, where supported
Theoretical or practical issues that remain insufficiently addressed
Evidence that the problem is significant enough to justify further investigation
Distinguish clearly between:
The broad research topic
The research problem
The research gap
The research problem should describe the specific issue that requires investigation, while the research gap should explain what is missing or unresolved in the existing knowledge. Do not treat these as interchangeable.
For each potential research problem, provide:
Potential Research Problem
Evidence from the Uploaded Literature
What Is Already Known
What Remains Problematic or Unresolved
Why the Problem Matters
Population or Context Involved, where applicable
Relevance to the Present Study
Strength of Supporting Evidence
Compare the potential problems and identify the single strongest research problem based on its significance, clarity, evidence, and relevance to the proposed article.
Then develop a concise Research Problem Logic Statement using this progression:
Established knowledge → specific unresolved problem → evidence that the problem exists → why further investigation is needed.
Conclude by drafting 1–2 academically written paragraphs that establish the research problem for inclusion in the article introduction and create a logical transition toward the research gap.
Do not formulate the final research gap, study objectives, or contribution yet. These will be developed in the next steps.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent problems, findings, statistics, populations, contexts, relationships, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded sources."

4. Establish the Research Gap
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, literature review specialist, and scholarly writing expert. Using the research context, current knowledge synthesis, and research problem developed in the previous steps, identify and establish the specific research gap that justifies the present study.
Systematically examine the uploaded literature to determine what remains:
Unknown or insufficiently understood
Underexplored or inadequately investigated
Theoretically underdeveloped
Methodologically limited
Inconsistent or contradictory across studies
Unexamined in relevant populations or contexts
Unclear regarding relationships among key concepts or variables
Unresolved despite previous research
Distinguish between a genuine research gap and the simple absence of studies on a particular topic, population, location, or combination of variables. A gap should represent a meaningful deficiency, uncertainty, or unresolved issue in existing knowledge that warrants further investigation.
Determine the type of gap, where applicable:
Empirical gap
Theoretical gap
Methodological gap
Contextual or geographical gap
Population gap
Conceptual gap
Contradictory findings gap
For each potential gap, provide:
Potential Research Gap
Type of Gap
What Is Already Known
What Remains Unknown or Unresolved
Evidence Supporting the Gap
Sources Supporting the Gap
Connection to the Research Problem
Why the Gap Matters
Potential Contribution of Addressing It
Strength of Supporting Evidence
Compare the potential gaps and identify the single strongest research gap based on the quality of supporting evidence, significance, relevance to the research problem, and potential scholarly contribution.
Then develop a concise Research Gap Logic Statement using this progression:
What is known → what remains unknown or unresolved → evidence demonstrating the gap → why the gap matters → need for the present study.
Conclude by drafting 1–2 academically written paragraphs that establish the research gap for inclusion in the article introduction and create a logical transition toward the significance and contribution of the study.
Do not develop the full significance statement, research objectives, or final contribution yet. These will be addressed in the subsequent steps.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent research gaps, findings, contradictions, populations, contexts, theories, statistics, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded sources."

5. Explain the Significance of the Study
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, and scholarly writing specialist. Using the research problem and research gap established in the previous steps, explain why the present study is important and what contribution it can make to existing knowledge.
Analyze the uploaded literature to determine the significance of addressing the identified gap. Where supported, examine:
Theoretical significance
Empirical significance
Methodological significance
Practical significance
Policy relevance
Relevance to specific populations, institutions, sectors, or contexts
Potential contribution to future research
Distinguish clearly between why the problem matters and what the study may contribute. Avoid vague statements such as “this study is important” unless the importance is demonstrated with evidence from the uploaded sources.
For each area of significance, provide:
Type of Significance
Evidence from the Uploaded Literature
Why It Matters
How Addressing the Gap Could Contribute
Relevance to the Present Study
Identify the 2 to 4 strongest areas of significance that should be emphasized in the research article introduction.
Then develop a concise Significance Logic Statement using this progression:
Research problem → validated gap → consequences of leaving the gap unresolved → value of investigating it → potential scholarly contribution.
Conclude by drafting 1–2 academically written paragraphs explaining the significance of the study and creating a logical transition toward the study purpose, objectives, and research questions.
Do not overstate the study’s contribution or claim impacts that have not yet been demonstrated. Frame expected contributions cautiously and proportionately to the evidence.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent implications, contributions, populations, contexts, policy relevance, findings, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded sources."

6. Develop the Research Article Introduction
"Review all uploaded sources and research materials in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, and scholarly writing specialist. Using the research context, existing knowledge, research problem, research gap, and study significance developed in the previous steps, construct a coherent and publication ready introduction for the research article.
Organize the introduction so that the argument progresses logically from the broader research context to the specific purpose of the study.
Integrate the following elements:
Broader research context
Current state of knowledge
Most relevant evidence from previous studies
Specific research problem
Validated research gap
Significance of addressing the gap
Purpose of the present study
Research objectives, where applicable
Research questions or hypotheses, where applicable
Expected scholarly contribution
Structure the introduction using this logical progression:
Research context → existing knowledge → research problem → research gap → significance → study purpose → research objectives or questions → expected contribution.
Synthesize evidence across multiple sources rather than presenting a study by study summary. Ensure that each paragraph advances the argument and creates a clear transition to the next.
Maintain an appropriate balance between background and specificity. Include enough literature to establish the argument for the study without turning the introduction into a full literature review.
Ensure that:
Claims are supported by appropriate citations
Terminology is consistent throughout
The research problem follows logically from the literature
The research gap is explicitly supported by evidence
The significance follows directly from the identified problem and gap
The study purpose directly addresses the research gap
Research objectives, questions, or hypotheses align with the study purpose
The expected contribution is stated cautiously and accurately
No new concepts appear without sufficient explanation
The introduction avoids unnecessary repetition
Present the output under the following headings:
Introduction Logic and Structure
Key Evidence to Include
Recommended Paragraph Sequence
Transition Strategy
Draft Research Article Introduction
Write the Draft Research Article Introduction as continuous, polished academic prose suitable for a journal manuscript. Do not use headings within the draft unless the intended journal or article format requires them.
Conclude with a brief Alignment Check showing how the final paragraphs connect the research problem, gap, significance, purpose, and research questions or hypotheses.
Do not invent research objectives, questions, hypotheses, findings, statistics, contributions, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded materials. If essential information is unavailable, explicitly identify what is missing rather than filling the gap with assumptions."

7. Review and Refine the Research Article Introduction
"Review all uploaded sources, research materials, and the completed introduction in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, journal editor, and peer reviewer. Critically review and refine the research article introduction for clarity, coherence, evidence, academic rigor, and publication readiness.
Evaluate the introduction for:
Clarity of the research context
Quality of the literature synthesis
Strength of the research problem
Evidence supporting the research gap
Significance of the study
Clarity of the study purpose
Alignment of objectives, questions, or hypotheses
Logical flow and transitions
Citation accuracy and evidence
Academic tone, precision, and conciseness
Check for unsupported claims, weakly supported gaps, overgeneralizations, repetition, irrelevant background, inconsistent terminology, vague statements, and unnecessary study by study summaries.
Evaluate whether the introduction follows this progression:
Research context → existing knowledge → research problem → research gap → significance → study purpose → objectives or questions → expected contribution.
For each major issue, provide:
Identified Issue
Original Wording
Recommended Revision
Reason for the Change
Then produce a fully revised research article introduction as coherent, publication ready academic prose while preserving accurate evidence and citations.
Conclude with a Final Readiness Assessment: Ready for Journal Manuscript, Minor Revision Required, Major Revision Required, or Insufficient Evidence to Finalize.
Use only the uploaded materials. Do not invent research gaps, findings, statistics, citations, objectives, questions, hypotheses, or contributions that are not supported by the uploaded sources."
