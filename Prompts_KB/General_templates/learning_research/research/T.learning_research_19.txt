🚨 STOP READING EVERYTHING.
CLAUDE can turn ANY document into the 5 things that matter.
5 prompts to do it in minutes.

1/ Contract Risk Scanner
"Read this contract and act as a lawyer. Highlight every clause that could hurt me, anything I am agreeing to that I might regret, hidden auto-renewals, liability traps, and anything that is non-standard. Explain each one in plain English."

2/ Long Report Summarizer
"Here is a report I need to understand: [paste]. Give me the 5 most important findings, what they mean for someone in my position [describe role], and the 3 decisions I need to make based on this information."

3/ Email Thread Untangler
"Here is a long email chain: [paste]. Tell me what was agreed, what is still unresolved, who is responsible for what, and what I need to reply to move this forward. Write the reply for me."

4/ Research Paper Decoder
"Explain this research paper to me like I am smart but not an expert in this field: [paste or describe]. What did they find, why does it matter, what are the weaknesses in their methodology, and how confident should I be in these results."

5/ Meeting Notes Action Extractor
"Here are my notes from today's meeting: [paste]. Extract every action item, who owns it, the deadline if mentioned, and any decisions that were made. Then write a follow up summary I can send to everyone who attended."

## research like a PhD student Deeper research. Better sources. A clear reading path.
Here are 7 prompts to do it yourself. 👇🏻


1/ The Research System
"Act as a doctoral-level research supervisor and design a rigorous research system for [TOPIC]. Start by identifying the central problem, define the primary research question and its highest-value subquestions, map the major academic perspectives and disagreements, determine the depth required, recommend the most authoritative source types, build a targeted search strategy, and create a reading sequence that moves from foundational knowledge to the latest credible research. Prioritize depth, evidence quality, and intellectual clarity over surface-level summaries."


2/ The Source Triage Pass
"Act as an expert academic research librarian. Audit the sources I collected for [TOPIC] and separate valuable evidence from noise. Evaluate each source by authority, methodology, publication quality, recency, relevance, potential bias, and directness to my research question. Rank the sources by priority, explain why each deserves or does not deserve attention, flag anything that should be replaced, map each source to the specific question it supports, and identify the most important evidence gaps still missing from my research."

3/ The Fast Comprehension Pass
"Act as an exceptional subject-matter tutor. Take me from zero to confident, conversational understanding of [TOPIC] in one intensive learning session. Build the mental model I need first, then explain the essential concepts, vocabulary, competing schools of thought, historical development, common misconceptions, and unresolved debates. Use plain language without oversimplifying. Finish with a 12-term expert glossary, a concise timeline, real-world examples, and five progressively harder questions that reveal whether I truly understand the subject."

4/ The Argument Map
"Act as a neutral but deeply critical analyst. Deconstruct the intellectual debate surrounding [TOPIC]. Identify the major positions, strongest evidence supporting each side, hidden assumptions, methodological differences, disputed definitions, and points where opposing arguments talk past one another. Build a clear argument map showing how the positions connect, present the strongest possible case for each side, expose the weakest link in each argument, and identify what new evidence would genuinely change the debate."

5/ The Sourced Brief
"Act as a senior research strategist writing for an intelligent, time-constrained reader. Transform my research on [TOPIC] into a concise, decision-ready brief. Identify the three most consequential findings, support every major claim with credible sources, distinguish established evidence from interpretation, assign a confidence level to each finding, highlight meaningful uncertainties and unanswered questions, and end with practical recommendations. Remove unnecessary background and make every paragraph earn its place."

6/ The Counterargument Drill
"Act as a hostile peer reviewer whose job is to find every weakness in my research on [TOPIC] before a specialist does. Stress-test my claims, evidence, methodology, definitions, assumptions, causal reasoning, and logical transitions. For every vulnerability, state the strongest objection an expert could make, explain why it matters, identify the evidence required to defend or revise the claim, and provide a prioritized revision plan based on the risk each weakness creates."

7/ The Citation Cleanup
"Act as a meticulous academic editor and citation auditor. Examine my draft on [TOPIC] line by line and identify every claim that requires evidence, every citation that is weak or mismatched, every unsupported assertion, and every passage that may paraphrase a source too closely. Recommend stronger sources where necessary, correct the in-text citations, standardize the reference list according to [CITATION STYLE], flag claims that cannot currently be verified, and produce a final checklist for making the research academically defensible."

🚨 Research doesn’t have to mean drowning in papers.
NotebookLM can turn complex academic sources into literature reviews, study comparisons, presentations, thesis defenses, and actionable insights.
Here are 7 prompts to do it faster. 👇👇

1. Research Presentation
PROMPT:
"Turn the research paper I provide into a clear, well-structured academic presentation. Organize it slide by slide to cover the research problem, research gap, objectives, methodology, key findings, strongest supporting evidence, limitations, conclusions, and practical implications. Select and prioritize the most important information rather than trying to include everything. Arrange the slides in a logical sequence that builds a coherent narrative from the problem the study addresses to its findings and implications. Keep each slide focused, concise, and suitable for an academic audience, and base all content strictly on the research paper without adding unsupported information."

2. Research Comparison
PROMPT:
"Create a systematic comparison of the research studies I provide. Compare their research questions, methodologies, samples, key findings, and conclusions, highlighting where the evidence agrees, differs, conflicts, or remains inconclusive. Explain important differences between the studies that may account for variations in their findings, and evaluate the main strengths and limitations of each study. Organize the comparison clearly so that patterns and contrasts across the studies are easy to identify. Conclude with an overall synthesis based on the strongest available evidence without overstating what the studies can support. Base the entire comparison strictly on the provided studies and clearly identify any questions the available evidence cannot resolve."

3. Conference Presentation
PROMPT:
"Turn the dissertation I provide into a focused and compelling presentation suitable for an academic conference. Structure the presentation around the research problem, knowledge gap, objectives, methodology, most significant findings, original contributions, implications, limitations, and directions for future research. Condense lengthy sections and remove details that are not essential to understanding the study or its contribution. Give particular emphasis to what is novel, significant, and worthy of discussion, while preserving the dissertation’s findings and claims accurately. Organize the slides into a coherent narrative that moves from the research problem to the study’s contribution and broader implications. Keep each slide concise and appropriate for a conference audience, and base all content strictly on the dissertation without introducing unsupported claims or information."

4. Thesis Defense
PROMPT:
"Turn the thesis I provide into a complete, well-structured presentation suitable for an academic defense. Organize the presentation around the research problem, research gap, research questions, relevant literature, methodology, key findings, discussion, conclusions, and recommendations. Clearly connect each major finding to the research question it addresses, explain its significance in relation to the study, and highlight the thesis’s main contribution to the field. Prioritize the evidence and arguments most important for defending the research while removing unnecessary detail. Conclude with a set of likely examiner questions and concise, evidence-based answers grounded in the thesis. Keep each slide focused and academically rigorous, and base all content strictly on the thesis without introducing unsupported claims or information."

5. Literature Review
PROMPT:
"Turn the research papers I provide into a clear, well-structured literature review organized around the major themes that emerge from the studies. Synthesize the literature rather than summarizing each paper separately, comparing evidence across studies to identify key patterns, areas of agreement and disagreement, methodological differences, emerging trends, and important research gaps. Highlight how the studies relate to and build on one another, and distinguish between findings that are well supported, findings that remain uncertain or contested, and questions that have not yet been adequately addressed. Conclude with an overall synthesis of what the literature currently establishes and the most important priorities for future research. Keep the review focused and academically rigorous, and base all claims strictly on the provided papers without introducing unsupported information."

6. Key Research Findings
PROMPT:
"Analyze the academic sources I provide and identify the most significant findings and insights they support. Highlight the key findings, recurring patterns, strongest evidence, contradictions, limitations, and important connections across the sources. Distinguish clearly between conclusions that are strongly supported, those based on limited or mixed evidence, and issues that remain uncertain or unresolved. Identify overlooked relationships or implications only when they can be reasonably inferred from the provided sources, and highlight the most important gaps in the existing research. Organize the findings clearly and rank them by their significance, strength of evidence, and potential impact. Base the analysis strictly on the provided sources without introducing unsupported claims or information."

7. Research Summary and Recommendations
PROMPT:
"Turn the research I provide into a concise, decision-focused summary. Highlight the strongest evidence, most important findings, key implications, major opportunities, risks, uncertainties, and practical recommendations. Distinguish clearly between conclusions that are well supported and areas where the evidence is limited, mixed, or uncertain. Translate the research into clear implications without oversimplifying or overstating what the evidence can support. Present the recommendations in priority order, ranking each one by potential impact and feasibility, and briefly explain the rationale for that ranking. Keep the summary concise, clear, and easy to scan, and base all conclusions and recommendations strictly on the provided research."

## More efficient academic research

1️. Create a Literature Review
“Using only the sources in this NotebookLM notebook, help me develop a literature review on [research topic/question]. Identify and synthesize the major themes, concepts, findings, and theoretical perspectives across the sources. Highlight areas of agreement, disagreement, recurring patterns, limitations, and underexplored areas. Compare studies where differences in methodology, population, context, or findings are important. Organize the review into logical thematic sections and support major claims with citations to the relevant sources. End with a concise synthesis of what the literature establishes and what remains uncertain or insufficiently studied. Do not invent studies, findings, citations, or research gaps; clearly identify anything the available sources do not support.”

2️. Compare Research Studies
“Using only the sources in this NotebookLM notebook, compare the studies related to [research topic/question]. Analyze their research aims, theoretical frameworks, methodologies, participants or samples, data collection and analysis methods, key findings, and limitations. Highlight important similarities, differences, agreements, and contradictions across the studies, and explain how methodological or contextual differences may relate to their findings. Organize the comparison in a clear table followed by a concise synthesis of the most important patterns. Support all claims with citations to the relevant sources. Do not invent information or fill gaps with assumptions; clearly identify details that are not reported in the available sources.”

3️. Identify Research Gaps
“Using only the sources in this NotebookLM notebook, identify research gaps related to [research topic/question]. Analyze the literature for unanswered questions, inconsistent or conflicting findings, methodological limitations, underexplored populations or contexts, and areas requiring further investigation. Explain why each identified gap matters and show which sources provide evidence for it. Prioritize the most significant and researchable gaps, and suggest potential directions for future research without presenting speculation as established evidence. Do not invent research gaps or rely on outside information; clearly distinguish between gaps explicitly stated by the authors and gaps inferred from comparing the available sources.”

4️. Synthesize Research Evidence
“Using only the sources in this NotebookLM notebook, synthesize the evidence related to [research topic/question]. Identify the major findings across the sources and explain what the literature collectively supports, where the evidence is consistent, and where findings are mixed or conflicting. Compare relevant differences in methodology, samples, or contexts that may help explain variations in the evidence. Organize the synthesis around key themes or findings and support major claims with citations to the relevant sources. Do not invent evidence or overstate conclusions; clearly identify areas where the available evidence is limited, inconsistent, or insufficient.”

5️. Create a Research Presentation
“Using only the sources in this NotebookLM notebook, create a clear academic presentation on [research topic/study] for [audience/purpose]. Organize it into a logical sequence covering the research problem, objectives or questions, relevant literature, methodology, key findings, discussion, implications, limitations, and conclusions where supported by the sources. For each slide, provide a concise title, key points, suggested visual or data representation, and brief speaker notes. Support important claims with citations to the relevant sources. Keep slides concise and academically appropriate. Do not invent findings, statistics, citations, or methodological details; clearly identify information that is not supported by the available sources.”
