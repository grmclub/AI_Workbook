Generative AI is increasingly recognized as a valuable tool in academic research, but its use must adhere to ethical and research integrity standards.
Many publishers, research institutions, and funding agencies now allow AI to support tasks such as improving language, summarizing literature, and refining academic writing, provided it is used responsibly.
Here are 10 key guidelines for using AI ethically in academic research:


1) Disclose the Use of AI When Required
Researchers should transparently disclose the use of generative AI when it contributes to the research process or the preparation of a manuscript. Many journals, publishers, and funding agencies require authors to explain how AI tools were used, particularly when they assist with writing, literature synthesis, or analysis. In some cases, minor uses such as basic grammar or spelling correction may not require disclosure, but any substantial AI assistance should be clearly acknowledged.

2) Do Not List AI as a Coauthor
Generative AI systems cannot be credited as authors of scientific publications. Authorship requires accountability, responsibility for the integrity of the work, and the ability to respond to questions about the research. Because AI tools cannot assume legal or ethical responsibility, only human researchers can be listed as authors.

3) Use AI Only as a Support Tool
AI should be used as a support tool rather than a substitute for the researcher’s intellectual contribution. For example, AI may help improve grammar, organize ideas, summarize research articles, or refine the clarity of academic writing. However, developing research questions, interpreting results, and conducting scientific reasoning must remain the responsibility of the human researcher.

4) Never Fabricate Research Data
Using AI to generate fake datasets, fabricate experimental results, or invent research findings constitutes research misconduct. Scientific integrity requires that all reported data and results accurately represent the research that was conducted. Fabricating or falsifying data, whether done manually or with AI, violates widely accepted standards of responsible research conduct.

5) Do Not Misrepresent Scientific Images
AI should not be used to create, modify, or enhance scientific images in ways that distort or misrepresent research findings. Figures, graphs, and visual data must accurately reflect the original data and experimental outcomes. Any adjustments to images must follow the ethical guidelines and image processing policies established by the target journal or publisher.

6) Protect Confidential and Sensitive Information
Researchers should exercise caution when using AI tools with confidential or unpublished material. Uploading sensitive information such as research data, unpublished manuscripts, peer review documents, or grant proposals into public AI systems may expose the information to external servers or third party platforms. Researchers should follow institutional policies and data protection guidelines when handling confidential research materials.

7) Do Not Use AI for Confidential Peer Review
Confidential manuscripts and grant proposals provided for peer review should not be uploaded to generative AI systems. Doing so may violate confidentiality agreements and expose unpublished research to external systems. Peer review is a confidential process, and reviewers must evaluate submissions independently without sharing the content with AI tools.

8) Verify All AI Generated Content
Researchers should carefully verify any content produced by generative AI before including it in academic work. AI systems may generate incorrect statements, inaccurate summaries, or fabricated references. Authors must check the accuracy of information, confirm citations with reliable sources, and ensure that all claims are supported by credible evidence.

9) Follow Journal, Institutional, and Funder Policies
Policies regarding the use of AI in research may differ across journals, publishers, universities, and funding agencies. Researchers should carefully review the guidelines of the organizations governing their work and ensure that their use of AI complies with these policies. Following these requirements helps maintain transparency, credibility, and responsible research practices.

10) Researchers Remain Fully Responsible for the Final Work
Even when AI tools assist with writing, editing, or organizing research content, the human researcher remains fully responsible for the final work. Authors are accountable for the accuracy of the data, the validity of the conclusions, the proper citation of sources, and the overall integrity of the research. AI may assist the research process, but responsibility for the final work always belongs to the researcher.
