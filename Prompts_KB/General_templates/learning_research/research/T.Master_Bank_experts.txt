🧠 7 TYPES OF EXPERTS. 70 PROMPTS.
collected exact questions philosophers, scientists, therapists, strategists, founders, speakers and economists use to think clearly.

Use any of these straight into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. Your AI stops agreeing with everything you say and actually starts helping you think.

🧠 THE PHILOSOPHER’S QUESTIONS
(For when your thinking isn’t clear yet)
    1. "If you’re not sure, say so. Don’t make things up." [Honesty Check] Why it works: Stops AI from confidently guessing wrong answers.
    2. "Point out the blind spot in my thinking." [Blind Spot Finder] Why it works: Forces AI to challenge you, not just agree with you.
    3. "List every assumption you made before answering." [Assumption Reveal] Why it works: Shows the hidden guesses hiding behind the answer.
    4. "Argue against your own answer as hard as you can." [Devil’s Advocate] Why it works: Weak answers fall apart fast under real pressure.
    5. "Rebuild this answer from the most basic truth up. Skip the shortcuts." [First Principles] Why it works: Gets you real logic instead of recycled opinions.
    6. "Break this problem down until it can’t be broken down any further." [Smallest Piece] Why it works: Complex problems get simple once split small enough.
    7. "If everyone did this, what would happen?" [Scale Test] Why it works: Reveals if your idea only works for you, not everyone.
    8. "Separate what is fact from what is your interpretation." [Fact vs Opinion] Why it works: Stops you confusing a guess with the truth.
    9. "Give me three opposite views that could all be true." [Multiple Angles] Why it works: Shows you’re not seeing the full picture yet.
    10. "In the worst case, how could this advice hurt me?" [Worst Case Filter] Why it works: Catches bad advice before you act on it.

🔬 THE SCIENTIST’S QUESTIONS
(For testing an idea, not just believing it)
    1. "Try to prove this wrong first, not right." [Break It First] Why it works: Weak ideas collapse fast when tested this way.
    2. "Does this claim make a prediction we can actually test?" [Testable Claim] Why it works: Filters real insight from empty talk.
    3. "Change only one thing at a time. Keep everything else the same." [One Variable Rule] Why it works: Shows you what actually caused the result.
    4. "Don’t jump to cause and effect without a control to compare it to." [No Control, No Claim] Why it works: Stops false cause and effect conclusions.
    5. "Is this correlation or causation? Split them clearly." [Correlation Check] Why it works: Two things happening together doesn’t mean one caused the other.
    6. "If two explanations both work, pick the simpler one." [Simplicity Wins] Why it works: Simple explanations are usually more accurate.
    7. "Would this result still show up with a different set of data?" [Repeat Test] Why it works: A one time result can be luck, not truth.
    8. "Is the sample big enough? Check for hidden bias." [Sample Check] Why it works: Small or biased samples give misleading answers.
    9. "Tell me your confidence level before you tell me the answer." [Confidence First] Why it works: Shows how sure the answer really is.
    10. "What new evidence would make you change your answer?" [Stay Open] Why it works: Good thinking updates itself with new facts.

💛 THE THERAPIST’S QUESTIONS
(For when emotion is clouding the facts)
    1. "List the facts and my interpretation of the facts separately." [Fact vs Feeling] Why it works: Untangles emotion from what’s actually true.
    2. "What automatic negative thoughts are hiding in what I just said?" [Catch the Pattern] Why it works: Spots thinking traps you don’t notice on your own.
    3. "Help me find the distortion in this thought." [Distortion Finder] Why it works: Names the exact thinking error, not just "you’re overthinking."
    4. "Describe my situation like a stranger watching from outside." [Outside View] Why it works: Creates distance so you can see clearer.
    5. "What do I actually need underneath this emotion?" [Real Need] Why it works: Emotions are signals, not the whole story.
    6. "What is the real chance of the worst case actually happening?" [Reality Check] Why it works: Fear usually exaggerates the odds.
    7. "If my friend had this exact problem, what would I tell them?" [Friend Test] Why it works: We’re kinder and smarter when advising others.
    8. "What am I avoiding, and what does avoiding it give me?" [Avoidance Audit] Why it works: Avoidance often hides a short term reward.
    9. "Is this thought a fact or just a thought?" [Thought Label] Why it works: Thoughts feel true but aren’t always true.
    10. "Confirm how I feel first, then give me advice." [Feel Then Fix] Why it works: People hear advice better after feeling heard first.

♟️ THE STRATEGIST’S QUESTIONS
(For when you have to make a hard call)
    1. "Clearly define what we are actually competing for here." [Define the Battle] Why it works: Half of conflicts are about the wrong thing entirely.
    2. "Put yourself in my opponent’s shoes. How would you attack me?" [Enemy View] Why it works: You defend better once you think like them.
    3. "What are the top 3 reactions they’ll have to this move?" [Think Ahead] Why it works: Good strategy plans several steps, not just one.
    4. "Can my time and resources actually support this plan?" [Resource Check] Why it works: Great plans fail from lack of resources, not lack of ideas.
    5. "Which single link, if it breaks, collapses the whole plan?" [Weak Link] Why it works: Every plan has one point that matters most.
    6. "If I must give up one thing, what should go first?" [Priority Cut] Why it works: Forces real tradeoffs instead of trying to keep everything.
    7. "Design a plan that lets me attack or retreat safely." [Keep Options Open] Why it works: Flexibility beats a rigid plan every time.
    8. "How long can this advantage last, and what keeps it alive?" [Advantage Lifespan] Why it works: Advantages fade, so plan for that now.
    9. "What is the cost of doing nothing at all?" [Cost of Inaction] Why it works: Staying still is a choice with a price too.
    10. "What is the smallest action that creates the biggest shift?" [Small Move, Big Shift] Why it works: Leverage beats brute effort.

🚀 THE FOUNDER’S QUESTIONS
(For testing an idea before you commit to it)
    1. "Whose problem does this actually solve, and how do they solve it now?" [Real Problem] Why it works: No real problem means no real business.
    2. "What proof is there that people will actually pay for this?" [Willingness to Pay] Why it works: Interest is not the same as payment.
    3. "What is the smallest version I can test first?" [Tiny Test] Why it works: Small tests save time and effort.
    4. "What does this cost to run, and what does it bring in?" [Unit Math] Why it works: An idea only works if the math works.
    5. "Why does this need to happen now? What changes in 3 years either way?" [Timing Check] Why it works: Timing decides winners as much as ideas do.
    6. "If I only had half the resources, what would I cut?" [Half Budget Test] Why it works: Reveals what actually matters versus what’s just nice to have.
    7. "Where exactly can I find my first real customers?" [First Customers] Why it works: A vague market means no customers at all.
    8. "If demand grew 10x overnight, what breaks first?" [Breaking Point] Why it works: Growth exposes weak spots fast.
    9. "What are the top 3 reasons this could fail?" [Risk List] Why it works: Naming risks early means you can plan around them.
    10. "What’s the fastest way to test this within a week?" [Fast Validation] Why it works: Fast and cheap beats slow and expensive every time.

🎤 THE SPEAKER’S QUESTIONS
(For making people actually listen)
    1. "Turn my opening into one specific scene or story." [Story Opening] Why it works: Scenes stick in memory, plain facts don’t.
    2. "Which line deserves to be said slower, with a pause?" [Slow It Down] Why it works: Pacing makes your key line actually land.
    3. "Turn this abstract number into something people can feel." [Make Numbers Real] Why it works: "2,000 people" hits harder as "a stadium full."
    4. "Did I use logic, emotion, or credibility? Add whichever is missing." [Missing Piece] Why it works: Real persuasion needs all three, not just facts.
    5. "Mark where I should pause for effect." [Pause Points] Why it works: Silence often says more than words.
    6. "Break my long sentences into short, speakable pieces." [Speak Rhythm] Why it works: Long sentences trip up speech, short ones flow.
    7. "Where can I turn a statement into a question for the audience?" [Ask, Don’t Just Tell] Why it works: Questions wake up a distracted audience.
    8. "Reorganize my main points into three clear parts." [Rule of Three] Why it works: Three points stick better than five.
    9. "Find the filler words I keep repeating." [Cut the Filler] Why it works: Cutting "um" and "so" instantly sounds sharper.
    10. "Rewrite my ending to echo my opening line." [Full Circle Ending] Why it works: Callbacks make a speech feel complete.

📊 THE ECONOMIST’S QUESTIONS
(For seeing the cost hiding behind the decision)
    1. "What is everyone’s incentive in this situation?" [Follow the Incentive] Why it works: People follow incentives more than intentions.
    2. "Is the extra gain worth the extra cost of doing this again?" [Marginal Thinking] Why it works: The next unit matters more than the average one.
    3. "This cost is already spent. Don’t let it affect the next decision." [Ignore Sunk Cost] Why it works: Money already spent shouldn’t control future choices.
    4. "What is the second layer effect of this decision?" [Second Order Effect] Why it works: First effects are obvious, second ones surprise you.
    5. "Did this change happen because of supply or demand? Separate them." [Supply vs Demand] Why it works: Same change, totally different cause and fix.
    6. "Is there an unrelated third party paying the cost for this?" [Hidden Cost] Why it works: Some costs get paid by people who never agreed to it.
    7. "What would happen if I did nothing, compared to doing this?" [Compare to Nothing] Why it works: The real cost is what you give up, not just what you spend.
    8. "Is this a win-win or a zero-sum situation? Where’s the benefit?" [Find the Trade] Why it works: Good trades benefit both sides, not just one.
    9. "Separate the short term and long term effects clearly." [Split the Timeline] Why it works: What looks good short term can be bad long term.
    10. "What does the full range look like behind this average?" [Beyond the Average] Why it works: Averages hide huge gaps in the real numbers.

💡 PRO TIPS
    • Save these prompts in your notes app so they’re always ready to use
    • Start with 2 or 3 prompts. Don’t dump all 70 into one chat at once
    • Mix categories. Try a Scientist prompt then a Therapist prompt on the same issue
    • Add your own details right after the prompt for sharper, more specific answers
    • Use the Strategist and Founder sections together for big decisions
