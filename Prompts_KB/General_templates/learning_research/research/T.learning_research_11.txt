🚨 BREAKING: Claude can now handle hours of deep research while you focus on what actually matters.
Here are 10 powerful research prompts that will save you hours every single week. 👇
Fast Research Publication Hub

1. Deep Topic Researcher
Prompt:
«Act as a senior research analyst. Help me understand [topic] in 20 minutes.
Include:
- A 2-sentence explanation of the core concept.
- The 3 most important ideas to learn first.
- The biggest misconception.
- What experts know that beginners don't.
- One real-world example.
- The single best resource to study next and why.
Keep it clear, practical, and concise.»

2. Competitor Intelligence Report
Prompt:
«Act as a competitive intelligence analyst. My business is [describe] and my top competitors are [list].
Create a report covering:
- Their positioning and pricing.
- Key strengths and weaknesses.
- Opportunities they're missing.
- The biggest market gap I can exploit to stand out and win.
Keep the analysis practical, strategic, and actionable.»

3. Industry Trend Scanner
Prompt:
«Act as an industry analyst. My field is [describe].
Tell me:
- The 3 biggest trends shaping the industry.
- What's overhyped vs. what truly matters.
- The biggest emerging opportunity.
- What I should build or learn before everyone else.
Keep it practical, forward-looking, and actionable.»

5. Source Synthesizer
Prompt:
«Here are my research sources on [topic]: [paste or describe].
Synthesize them into:
- The 3 most important insights.
- Where the sources agree.
- Where they conflict.
- The key question none of them fully answer.
Keep it clear, concise, and actionable.»

6. Expert Knowledge Extractor
Prompt:
«Act as the world's leading expert on [topic]. I have 10 minutes to become credible in this subject.
Give me:
- 5 key concepts to understand.
- 3 influential names and why they matter.
- 2 important statistics.
- 1 nuanced insight that separates experts from beginners.
Keep it practical, concise, and accurate.»

7. Research to Decision Converter
Prompt:
«I've researched [topic] and need to decide [describe]. Here are my notes: [paste].
Tell me:
- What the research actually recommends.
- What's still missing before making a confident decision.
- The single most important factor to prioritize.
Keep it clear, practical, and objective.»

4. Contrarian Research Lens
Prompt:
«Act as a contrarian researcher. The mainstream view on [topic] is [describe].
Find:
- The strongest evidence against it.
- What credible experts disagree on.
- The assumption everyone makes that could be wrong.
Keep the analysis balanced, evidence-based, and concise.»

8. Market Research Brief
Prompt:
«Act as a market research analyst. I'm entering [market] with [offer].
Provide:
- Market size.
- Customer segments ranked by value.
- Top competitors.
- The biggest market gap.
- A final verdict: real opportunity or overcrowded market?
Keep it concise, data-driven, and actionable.»

10. Research Summary & Action Plan
Prompt:
«Here is my research on [topic]: [paste notes].
Create a 5-minute brief with:
- Key findings in plain English.
- What's confirmed vs. uncertain.
- The 3 biggest implications for [my goal].
- The first action I should take.
Keep it concise, practical, and actionable.»

9. Literature Review Builder
Prompt:
«Act as a PhD research assistant. Help me understand the research on [topic].
Summarize:
- The main findings.
- Where researchers disagree.
- Common research methods.
- The biggest research gap.
- The 3 most important papers or sources to read first.
Keep it clear, concise, and evidence-based.»

## How to Study Anything With NotebookLM

Step 1: Setup & Priming
​➔ Upload Everything at Once
Load all your course materials, textbooks, slide decks, and transcripts into a single notebook so NotebookLM can connect relationships across sources.
​➔ Listen Before Reading
Generate a short Audio Overview and listen to it before opening the text.
​➔ Why It Works
Hearing main concepts first builds a mental framework (schema). When you read the text later, details slot into an existing structure rather than arriving as disconnected facts.

Step 2: Visual & Structural Mapping
​➔ Build Video Overviews & Mind Maps
Generate visual overviews and mind maps to see how topics and subtopics connect.
​➔ Fact-Check Visual Summaries
Review generated infographics against your source text. Catching errors forces active engagement and deepens memory.
​➔ Why It Works
Struggling to learn is usually an organization problem, not a memory problem. Mapping out the hierarchy gives every detail a clear place to sit.

Step 3: Deep Interrogation
​➔ Study on the Go
Listen to Deep Dive audio overviews during low-friction activities like walking or commuting to engage your brain's relaxed-mode processing.
​➔ Interactive Q&A (Ask "Why?")
Use interactive audio mode to steer the conversation. Continually ask "Why did this happen?" and "Why does this follow?" to force logical explanations.
​➔ Use Study Guide Mode
Generate short-answer quizzes and glossaries. Active recall through short answers requires pulling information from scratch rather than simple multiple-choice recognition.
​➔ Why It Works
Rereading material creates a false sense of familiarity. Interrogating the concepts proves whether you actually understand the underlying reasoning.

Step 4: Active Retrieval & Testing
​➔ Spaced Repetition Flashcards
Generate flashcards and review them over several days right before you naturally forget the information.
​➔ Cross-Source Quizzes
Run comprehensive quizzes across all uploaded sources. This forces your brain to identify which concept applies to an unknown question, replicating real-world exams and meetings.
​➔ The Feynman Slide Test
Generate a slide deck and present it aloud to an empty room. The exact moment you stumble reveals a critical knowledge gap.
​➔ Why It Works
Pulling information out of your head is the single most effective way to lock it into long-term memory.
