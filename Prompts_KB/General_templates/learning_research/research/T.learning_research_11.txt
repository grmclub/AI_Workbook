🚨 BREAKING: Claude can now handle hours of deep research while you focus on what actually matters.
Here are 10 powerful research prompts that will save you hours every single week. 👇
Fast Research Publication Hub

1. Deep Topic Researcher
Prompt:
«Act as a senior research analyst. Help me understand [topic] in 20 minutes.
Include:
- A 2-sentence explanation of the core concept.
- The 3 most important ideas to learn first.
- The biggest misconception.
- What experts know that beginners don't.
- One real-world example.
- The single best resource to study next and why.
Keep it clear, practical, and concise.»

2. Competitor Intelligence Report
Prompt:
«Act as a competitive intelligence analyst. My business is [describe] and my top competitors are [list].
Create a report covering:
- Their positioning and pricing.
- Key strengths and weaknesses.
- Opportunities they're missing.
- The biggest market gap I can exploit to stand out and win.
Keep the analysis practical, strategic, and actionable.»

3. Industry Trend Scanner
Prompt:
«Act as an industry analyst. My field is [describe].
Tell me:
- The 3 biggest trends shaping the industry.
- What's overhyped vs. what truly matters.
- The biggest emerging opportunity.
- What I should build or learn before everyone else.
Keep it practical, forward-looking, and actionable.»

5. Source Synthesizer
Prompt:
«Here are my research sources on [topic]: [paste or describe].
Synthesize them into:
- The 3 most important insights.
- Where the sources agree.
- Where they conflict.
- The key question none of them fully answer.
Keep it clear, concise, and actionable.»

6. Expert Knowledge Extractor
Prompt:
«Act as the world's leading expert on [topic]. I have 10 minutes to become credible in this subject.
Give me:
- 5 key concepts to understand.
- 3 influential names and why they matter.
- 2 important statistics.
- 1 nuanced insight that separates experts from beginners.
Keep it practical, concise, and accurate.»

7. Research to Decision Converter
Prompt:
«I've researched [topic] and need to decide [describe]. Here are my notes: [paste].
Tell me:
- What the research actually recommends.
- What's still missing before making a confident decision.
- The single most important factor to prioritize.
Keep it clear, practical, and objective.»

4. Contrarian Research Lens
Prompt:
«Act as a contrarian researcher. The mainstream view on [topic] is [describe].
Find:
- The strongest evidence against it.
- What credible experts disagree on.
- The assumption everyone makes that could be wrong.
Keep the analysis balanced, evidence-based, and concise.»

8. Market Research Brief
Prompt:
«Act as a market research analyst. I'm entering [market] with [offer].
Provide:
- Market size.
- Customer segments ranked by value.
- Top competitors.
- The biggest market gap.
- A final verdict: real opportunity or overcrowded market?
Keep it concise, data-driven, and actionable.»

10. Research Summary & Action Plan
Prompt:
«Here is my research on [topic]: [paste notes].
Create a 5-minute brief with:
- Key findings in plain English.
- What's confirmed vs. uncertain.
- The 3 biggest implications for [my goal].
- The first action I should take.
Keep it concise, practical, and actionable.»

9. Literature Review Builder
Prompt:
«Act as a PhD research assistant. Help me understand the research on [topic].
Summarize:
- The main findings.
- Where researchers disagree.
- Common research methods.
- The biggest research gap.
- The 3 most important papers or sources to read first.
Keep it clear, concise, and evidence-based.»

## How to Study Anything With NotebookLM

Step 1: Setup & Priming
​➔ Upload Everything at Once
Load all your course materials, textbooks, slide decks, and transcripts into a single notebook so NotebookLM can connect relationships across sources.
​➔ Listen Before Reading
Generate a short Audio Overview and listen to it before opening the text.
​➔ Why It Works
Hearing main concepts first builds a mental framework (schema). When you read the text later, details slot into an existing structure rather than arriving as disconnected facts.

Step 2: Visual & Structural Mapping
​➔ Build Video Overviews & Mind Maps
Generate visual overviews and mind maps to see how topics and subtopics connect.
​➔ Fact-Check Visual Summaries
Review generated infographics against your source text. Catching errors forces active engagement and deepens memory.
​➔ Why It Works
Struggling to learn is usually an organization problem, not a memory problem. Mapping out the hierarchy gives every detail a clear place to sit.

Step 3: Deep Interrogation
​➔ Study on the Go
Listen to Deep Dive audio overviews during low-friction activities like walking or commuting to engage your brain's relaxed-mode processing.
​➔ Interactive Q&A (Ask "Why?")
Use interactive audio mode to steer the conversation. Continually ask "Why did this happen?" and "Why does this follow?" to force logical explanations.
​➔ Use Study Guide Mode
Generate short-answer quizzes and glossaries. Active recall through short answers requires pulling information from scratch rather than simple multiple-choice recognition.
​➔ Why It Works
Rereading material creates a false sense of familiarity. Interrogating the concepts proves whether you actually understand the underlying reasoning.

Step 4: Active Retrieval & Testing
​➔ Spaced Repetition Flashcards
Generate flashcards and review them over several days right before you naturally forget the information.
​➔ Cross-Source Quizzes
Run comprehensive quizzes across all uploaded sources. This forces your brain to identify which concept applies to an unknown question, replicating real-world exams and meetings.
​➔ The Feynman Slide Test
Generate a slide deck and present it aloud to an empty room. The exact moment you stumble reveals a critical knowledge gap.
​➔ Why It Works
Pulling information out of your head is the single most effective way to lock it into long-term memory.

## 7 Gemini Notebook prompts to help you develop a strong research problem, from identifying the core issue to reviewing and refining the final problem statement. 👇👇

1. Identify the Core Research Problem
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, and scholarly literature analyst. Identify and clearly define the core research problem that emerges from the uploaded literature.
Systematically examine the sources to determine what important issue, challenge, unresolved question, contradiction, persistent difficulty, or inadequately understood phenomenon requires further investigation.
Distinguish between a broad research topic and an actual research problem. The research problem must represent a specific condition, issue, or knowledge deficiency that can be investigated through systematic research.
Analyze the literature to identify:
What is already known about the topic
What remains unclear, unresolved, inconsistent, or insufficiently understood
Recurring problems or concerns reported across studies
Contradictory or inconsistent findings
Populations, contexts, or settings that remain insufficiently examined
Practical or theoretical issues requiring further investigation
Evidence demonstrating that the problem actually exists
Compare evidence across the uploaded sources rather than relying on a single study. Identify areas where multiple sources converge or disagree and explain how this evidence supports the existence of the proposed research problem.
Develop 2 to 3 potential research problems where the literature supports multiple possibilities. For each one, explain its significance, supporting evidence, researchability, and potential contribution. Then recommend the strongest research problem based on the available evidence.
Present the output under the following headings:
Research Context
What Is Already Known
Unresolved Issues Identified
Evidence of the Problem
Potential Research Problems
Recommended Core Research Problem
Justification for the Selection
Preliminary Scope of the Problem
Issues Requiring Further Investigation
Conclude with a concise statement clearly expressing what the problem is, where or among whom it occurs where supported, what remains unknown or unresolved, and why further research is necessary.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent research gaps, problems, statistics, contexts, populations, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded literature."

2. Identify and Validate the Research Gap
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, and literature review expert. Identify and validate the research gap that supports the core research problem developed in the previous step.
Systematically analyze the uploaded literature to determine what knowledge, evidence, theory, method, population, context, relationship, or practical issue remains insufficiently investigated.
Examine the literature for:
Empirical gaps
Theoretical gaps
Methodological gaps
Contextual or geographical gaps
Population gaps
Contradictory or inconsistent findings
Underexplored variables or relationships
Outdated or limited evidence
Areas repeatedly recommended for future research
Distinguish between a genuine research gap and a topic that is merely less frequently studied. Do not classify something as a gap unless the uploaded literature provides clear evidence that it remains unresolved, inconsistent, underdeveloped, or inadequately examined.
For each potential gap, explain:
What is currently known
What remains unknown or uncertain
The type of research gap
The studies that support the existence of the gap
Why the gap is important
How the gap connects to the proposed research problem
What new knowledge could be generated by addressing it
Compare the strength of evidence supporting each potential gap and rank them from strongest to weakest.
Present the output under the following headings:
Current State of Knowledge
Potential Research Gaps
Evidence Supporting Each Gap
Type of Gap
Significance of Each Gap
Relationship to the Core Research Problem
Gap Strength Assessment
Recommended Primary Research Gap
Justification for the Recommended Gap
Conclude with a concise, evidence-based research gap statement that clearly explains what is known, what remains unknown, and why further investigation is necessary.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent research gaps, citations, findings, populations, contexts, statistics, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded literature."

3. Establish the Evidence and Significance of the Research Problem
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, and scholarly literature analyst. Establish a strong, evidence-based justification for the research problem identified in the previous steps.
Systematically examine the uploaded literature to determine the nature, extent, persistence, and significance of the problem. Build a clear chain of evidence demonstrating that the problem exists, matters academically or practically, and warrants further investigation.
Analyze the uploaded sources to establish:
Evidence that the research problem exists
The nature and extent of the problem
Populations, organizations, communities, sectors, or contexts affected, where supported
Consequences or implications associated with the problem
Evidence that the problem remains unresolved
Limitations of existing knowledge or current approaches
The academic, theoretical, practical, social, organizational, or policy significance of the problem, where applicable
Why further research is necessary
Compare evidence across multiple sources and identify areas of agreement, disagreement, and uncertainty. Prioritize recent and directly relevant evidence where available while recognizing important foundational studies.
Do not assume that a research gap automatically makes the problem significant. Evaluate whether the uploaded literature provides sufficient evidence that addressing the problem could make a meaningful contribution to knowledge, theory, practice, policy, or the specific research context.
Present the output under the following headings:
Evidence That the Problem Exists
Nature and Extent of the Problem
Who or What Is Affected
Consequences of the Problem
Evidence That the Problem Remains Unresolved
Limitations of Existing Knowledge
Significance of the Research Problem
Need for Further Research
Strength of the Available Evidence
Conclude with a concise evidence-based justification of the research problem that explains what the problem is, what evidence demonstrates its existence, why it matters, what remains unresolved, and why additional research is warranted.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent statistics, consequences, affected populations, citations, findings, claims, or evidence that are not supported by the uploaded literature."

4. Define and Delimit the Research Problem
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher and research methodologist. Define and delimit the research problem identified in the previous steps so that it becomes specific, focused, researchable, and appropriately bounded.
Clarify the exact issue to be investigated and identify, where applicable:
The specific phenomenon or problem
The key concepts or variables
The target population or unit of analysis
The study context or setting
The geographical boundaries
The relevant time frame
The theoretical or conceptual boundaries
What should be included in the study
What should be excluded from the study
Explain why each boundary is justified by the uploaded literature and how narrowing the problem improves its clarity, feasibility, and researchability.
Present the output under the following headings:
Broad Research Problem
Specific Research Problem
Key Concepts or Variables
Target Population or Unit of Analysis
Research Context and Setting
Scope and Delimitations
Explicit Exclusions
Justification for the Boundaries
Researchability and Feasibility Assessment
Conclude with a concise, delimited research problem statement that clearly specifies what will be investigated, among whom or within what unit of analysis, and in what context.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent populations, variables, locations, contexts, time frames, or other details that are not supported by the uploaded materials."

5. Develop the Research Problem Statement
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, and scholarly writer. Develop a clear, concise, evidence-based research problem statement using the research problem, validated gap, supporting evidence, significance, and delimitations established in the previous steps.
Construct the problem statement so that it clearly explains:
The broader context of the problem
What is currently known from the literature
The specific problem that exists
Evidence demonstrating the existence and significance of the problem
What remains unknown, unresolved, inconsistent, or insufficiently understood
The research gap underlying the problem
Who or what is affected, where supported
The consequences or significance of leaving the problem unresolved
Why further research is necessary
The specific aspect of the problem that the proposed study should investigate
Develop the argument logically from the broader research context to the specific problem. Synthesize evidence across multiple uploaded sources rather than presenting studies as an isolated list.
Ensure that the problem statement distinguishes clearly between the practical or empirical problem and the research or knowledge gap. Do not treat the absence of research alone as sufficient evidence that an important research problem exists.
Avoid vague statements, unsupported claims, excessive background information, proposed solutions, methodological details, and conclusions that belong elsewhere in the research.
Present the output under the following headings:
Research Context
Evidence of the Problem
Specific Research Problem
Research Gap
Significance of the Problem
Need for Further Investigation
Proposed Research Focus
Final Research Problem Statement
Conclude with a polished, academically rigorous research problem statement that moves logically from what is known, to what the problem is, to what remains unknown, and finally to why the proposed research is necessary.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent statistics, research gaps, populations, contexts, consequences, citations, findings, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded literature."

6. Align the Research Problem with the Study
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, and scholarly reviewer. Evaluate and strengthen the alignment between the research problem and the other core components of the proposed study.
Review the research problem statement developed in the previous step and assess whether it logically aligns with the research gap, purpose of the study, research objectives, research questions, hypotheses where applicable, and theoretical or conceptual framework.
Evaluate the alignment between:
Research problem and research gap
Research problem and study purpose
Research problem and research objectives
Research objectives and research questions
Research questions and hypotheses, where applicable
Research problem and theoretical or conceptual framework
Key concepts or variables across all components
Target population, context, and scope across all components
Identify any inconsistencies, missing connections, unnecessary variables or concepts, differences in terminology, overly broad objectives, research questions that do not address the problem, or hypotheses that are not logically derived from the proposed relationships.
For every alignment issue identified, explain why it creates a problem and recommend a specific revision. Preserve the meaning of the research problem unless the uploaded evidence demonstrates that modification is necessary.
Present the output as a structured Research Alignment Matrix containing:
Research Problem
Research Gap
Study Purpose
Research Objective
Research Question
Hypothesis, where applicable
Relevant Construct or Variable
Theoretical or Conceptual Connection
Alignment Assessment
Identified Issue
Recommended Revision
Conclude with a revised set of aligned research components and explain how each research objective and question directly contributes to investigating the identified research problem.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent objectives, questions, hypotheses, variables, theoretical relationships, populations, contexts, citations, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded materials."

7. Review and Refine the Research Problem
"Review all uploaded sources in Gemini Notebook and use them as the sole basis for this task.
Act as an experienced academic researcher, research methodologist, peer reviewer, and scholarly editor. Critically review the complete research problem development process and refine the final research problem statement for clarity, significance, researchability, and academic rigor.
Evaluate whether the research problem is:
Clearly defined and specific
Supported by sufficient evidence from the uploaded literature
Distinct from the broader research topic
Grounded in a genuine and validated research gap
Significant enough to justify further investigation
Appropriately delimited in terms of population, context, variables, or scope where applicable
Feasible and researchable
Logically aligned with the study purpose, objectives, research questions, hypotheses, and theoretical or conceptual framework
Free from unsupported claims, vague language, unnecessary background, and premature solutions
Identify any weaknesses, inconsistencies, overgeneralizations, missing evidence, unclear boundaries, or alignment problems. Recommend specific revisions and explain why each change is necessary.
Present the output under the following headings:
Overall Assessment
Strengths of the Research Problem
Areas Requiring Revision
Evidence and Significance Assessment
Research Gap Assessment
Scope and Delimitation Assessment
Alignment Assessment
Recommended Revisions
Final Revised Research Problem Statement
Final Readiness Assessment
Conclude with a polished, concise, and academically rigorous research problem statement suitable for inclusion in a research proposal, thesis, dissertation, or journal article.
Use only the uploaded sources. Do not invent research gaps, statistics, populations, variables, contexts, consequences, citations, findings, or claims that are not supported by the uploaded materials."
