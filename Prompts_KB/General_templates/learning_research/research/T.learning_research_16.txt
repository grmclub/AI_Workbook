1/ The Research Brief System
"Act as a senior research analyst and editor. Build a rigorous, decision-ready brief on [TOPIC]. Define the real question, key sub-questions, relevant fields, major debates, common misconceptions, and what a decision-maker needs to know. Specify the best source types and evidence standards, then structure the brief with an executive summary, key findings, evidence, counterarguments, and practical implications."

2/ The Question Sharpener
"Turn my vague topic into a strong research question. Give me 5 versions—from narrow and answerable to broad and ambitious. For each, state the evidence needed, estimated research time, and what would remain unknown. Recommend the best question for a single afternoon.
Topic: [INSERT TOPIC]"

3/ The Source Map
"Map where reliable answers to this question live. Identify the key source types—primary data, peer-reviewed research, industry reports, official statistics, and expert accounts. For each, explain what it’s best for, what it may miss, and 2 questions to ask before trusting its data.
Research Question: [INSERT QUESTION]"

4/ The Steel Man Pass
"Steel-man the position I disagree with. Present its strongest reasoning and evidence, explain where it challenges my view, then identify what I should accept as valid—and the single piece of evidence most likely to change my mind.
Position: [INSERT POSITION]"

5/ The Evidence Table
"Turn my research into an evidence table. For each source, capture the claim, evidence type, scope/sample, date, producer/funder, and strength of support. Highlight where sources agree, conflict, or rely on the same original study. Flag the 2 most important hidden source dependencies.
Research: [PASTE YOUR SOURCES/FINDINGS]"

6/ The Gap Finder
"Audit my draft for what’s missing. Flag claims based on one source, numbers lacking context, correlation treated as causation, overlooked counterexamples, and questions a skeptical reader would raise early. Rank every gap by potential damage and tell me exactly what to fix.
Draft: [PASTE DRAFT]"

7/ The Plain Language Rewrite
"Rewrite my brief for a smart reader outside the field. Replace jargon with plain language, shorten long sentences, lead with conclusions, and turn the 2 most abstract points into concrete examples. Keep every number and caveat unchanged. End by identifying the sentence most likely to lose the reader.
Brief: [PASTE BRIEF]"

8/ The One-Page Summary
"Compress my research brief into one page. Start with the answer in 2 sentences, then give the 3 strongest findings, the strongest counterpoint and why it doesn’t overturn the conclusion, your confidence level and why, and the one thing to verify before acting. Keep every bullet to 2 lines max.
Brief: [PASTE BRIEF]"

1. The Research Gap Sniper (Stop being generic)
Act as a PhD supervisor with 20 years in [YOUR FIELD]. I've uploaded 15 papers: [ATTACH TOP PAPERS]. Don't just summarize. Do this: 1. Create a table: Author / Year / Method / Finding / Limitation 2. Find 3 contradictions between them 3. Find 2 populations/methods they ALL ignored 4. Give me 3 specific, researchable gaps with WHY they matter and HOW I can fill them. No generic "more research is needed" BS.

2. The Literature Review Architect
I need a Literature Review for [YOUR TOPIC]. My research question is: [QUESTION]. Structure it like a top-tier journal in [FIELD]. 1. Group these papers into 3-4 themes (not just list them) 2. For each theme: What do we know? What is debated? What is missing? 3. End with a funnel: Broad -> Narrow -> My Gap. 4. Write it in academic tone with in-text citations APA 7th, and suggest where I need transition sentences. Use only the papers I uploaded.

3. The Methodology Defender
My topic is: [TOPIC], Gap is: [GAP], Question is: [QUESTION]. My supervisor will attack my methodology. Act as an external examiner. Design my methodology and defend it: 1. Recommend: Qualitative/Quantitative/Mixed + WHY 2. Exact design (e.g., semi-structured interviews with 15 participants) 3. Sampling + Justification 4. Data analysis plan (Thematic Analysis with Braun & Clarke) 5. How to answer: "Why didn't you do X instead?" Give me a bulletproof paragraph for my thesis.

4. The Data Analysis Whisperer
I have [INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPTS / SURVEY DATA]. Don't just code it. Act as a qualitative researcher: 1. Code this data line-by-line, give me initial codes 2. Group codes into 4-5 themes 3. For each theme: 2 direct quotes as evidence + my interpretation 4. Find 1 surprising pattern I missed. Output as a table: Theme / Codes / Evidence / Meaning. Be rigorous, not nice.

5. The Argument Builder (For Discussion Chapter)

WHY did I get this result? Give me 3 theoretical explanations 3. What is the practical implication? 4. What is 1 limitation that makes me look smart, not stupid? Write 500 words, academic, no fluff.

6. The Supervisor-Proof Editor

Check: Every paragraph must have Topic Sentence -> Evidence -> Analysis -> Link 3. Flag any claim without a citation 4. Make it sound human, like a final-year Masters student in [FIELD] at [UNIVERSITY], not AI. Keep my meaning, change my style. Show tracked changes: Before vs After.

7. The Viva / Defense Killer
My thesis title is: [TITLE]. My full thesis is: [UPLOAD PDF]. Act as my external examiner. Grill me: 1. Ask me 10 toughest questions you'll ask in my viva/defense 2. For each, give me a model answer in 90 seconds (STAR method) 3. Ask 3 trap questions: "What would you do differently?" "What is your contribution?" "Why should we give you this degree?" Prepare me so I don't freeze.
