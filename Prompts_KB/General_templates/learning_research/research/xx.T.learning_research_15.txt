7 prompts to learn & focus

1.
"Here’s the result I want: [outcome]. Reverse engineer the fastest, simplest path to get there."
→ Start from the end.

2.
"What am I missing that could completely change this outcome: [situation]?"
→ Find blind spots.

3.
"For this goal: [goal], what actions will create most of the results?"
→ 80/20 everything.

4.
"Turn this into a simple, repeatable system I can follow: [process]."
→ Consistency wins.

5.
"Be brutally honest. What’s wrong, weak, or likely to fail here: [idea]?"
→ Truth > comfort.

6.
"What opportunities am I overlooking in this situation: [context]?"
→ Hidden leverage.

7.
"If I could only take one action to move this forward, what should it be: [goal]?"
→ Momentum starts.

Most people want more tools.
But the real edge is:
• better thinking
• sharper focus
• faster execution

##Quick summary and briefs

1.
The Executive Brief
“Turn this into a one-minute executive briefing. Include the key insights, major risks, and recommended actions: [text].”

2.
The 80/20 Summary
“Apply the 80/20 principle and extract the 20% of information that delivers 80% of the value: [text].”

3.
The Action-Only Summary
“Don't summarize everything. List only the practical takeaways and specific actions I should take after reading this: [text].”

4.
The Beginner Version
“Explain the main ideas as if you're teaching someone with no prior knowledge. Use simple language and real-world examples: [text].”

5.
The Decision Maker Prompt
“Summarize this from the perspective of someone who has to make a decision. Highlight opportunities, risks, trade-offs, and missing information: [text].”

6.
The Memory Prompt
“Convert this into concise bullet points that are easy to remember. End with the three most important insights.”

8.
The Critical Thinker Prompt
“Summarize the main arguments, then identify weak assumptions, potential biases, and questions that remain unanswered.”

7.
The Compare & Contrast Prompt
“Summarize this, then compare it with common alternatives, highlighting the biggest differences and why they matter.”

Most people ask for shorter text.
Power users ask for:
• clarity
• decisions
• action
That's where the real value is.
