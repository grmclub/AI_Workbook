7 Claude prompts to learn anything faster


1. The Learning Blueprint
"I want to learn [skill] as fast as possible. I’m starting from zero. Build me the most efficient 30-day learning plan possible. Skip anything that isn’t essential. Focus only on the 20% of knowledge that gives me 80% of the results. Tell me exactly what to study each day and in what order. I have [X hours] per day to dedicate to this."

2. The Mental Model Builder
"Teach me the 5 most important mental models I need to understand [skill or topic]. For each one explain what it is in simple terms, give me a real life example I can relate to, and tell me how to apply it immediately. I want to think like an expert not just memorize information."

3. The Feynman Explainer
"Explain [concept] to me like I’m 12 years old. Use a simple analogy. No jargon. No complicated words. Then explain why this concept matters and how it connects to the bigger picture of [skill]. If I don’t understand something ask me what part is confusing and explain it a different way."

4. The Speed Reader
"I need to understand [book, topic, or subject] quickly. Give me the 10 most important ideas from it. For each idea give me one sentence summary, one real world example, and one action I can take today based on what I just learned. I want the knowledge without reading 300 pages."

5. The Accelerated Practice Designer
"Give me 7 daily micro-practice exercises for learning [skill]. Each one should take under 20 minutes. Make them progressively harder as the week goes on. Design them so I’m building real skill not just consuming information. Include a way to measure whether I’m actually improving."

6. The Mistake Analyzer
"I’ve been learning [skill] for [time period]. Here’s where I keep getting stuck [describe it]. Don’t just fix my mistake. Help me understand why I keep making it. What mental pattern is causing this? What do I need to rewire in my thinking to stop repeating this error? Then give me a drill to fix it."

7. The Expert Interviewer
"Pretend you are a world class expert in [skill]. I’m going to interview you. Answer my questions the way a top expert would, with depth and real insight, not textbook answers. Start by telling me the one thing most beginners get completely wrong about learning [skill] and how to fix it immediately."

1. Diagnose the Complexity
P: Act as a Learning Strategist. I need to understand [complex topic/concept] but have no formal background in [relevant field]. Identify exactly what makes it difficult—terminology, abstract logic, or missing prerequisites. Then list the essential concepts I should learn first.

2. Build the Foundation
P: Act as a Patient Subject Matter Tutor. Based on the prerequisite gaps for [complex topic], explain only the essential concepts I’m missing in simple language. Keep each concept to 2–3 sentences, and skip anything not directly needed to understand the topic.

3. Simplify the Core
P: Act as an Expert Explainer. Explain [complex topic] in the simplest possible terms using a clear analogy or everyday example. Remove unnecessary jargon while keeping the explanation accurate. Teach it as if I’m smart but completely new to the field.

4. Build Toward Mastery
P: Act as a Progressive Complexity Tutor. Using the simplified explanation of [complex topic], gradually add the nuance and complexity needed for real understanding. Introduce one layer at a time, ensuring each builds logically on the previous one.

5. Connect to What You Know
P: Act as a Learning Integration Coach. For [complex topic], connect the new ideas to what I already know from [background/field/interests]. Create clear mental bridges that make the concept easier to remember and use—not just memorize.

6. Test Your Understanding
P: Act as a Socratic Tutor. Test my understanding of [complex topic] with 3 questions that require real comprehension, not memorization. Start with a basic application, then increase the difficulty to a novel scenario I haven’t seen before.

7. Find Your Blind Spots
P: Act as a Knowledge Gap Auditor. Based on my answers about [complex topic], identify what I truly understand versus what I’m only pattern-matching. Be direct about my remaining knowledge gaps and explain exactly what I need to improve.

8. Prove You’ve Mastered It
P: Act as a Mastery Verification Coach. Ask me to explain [complex topic] in my own words as if teaching a beginner. Then analyze my explanation for gaps, oversimplifications, and inaccuracies that reveal incomplete understanding.

2. Structural Mapping
Act as a Book Structure Analyst. For [book title], map its structural skeleton: identify the major sections or parts, explain the role each plays in developing the overall argument, and distinguish the sections most likely to contain the book’s core original contributions from those focused primarily on supporting context, evidence, or examples.

3. Core Argument Extraction
Act as an Academic Synthesis Specialist. Identify and clearly articulate the central thesis or core argument of [book title] in simple, original language. Distinguish the author’s main claim from the examples, evidence, and supporting arguments used to develop it. Summarize the core argument in 2–3 sentences, as if explaining the book to someone who has never read it.

4. Chapter-by-Chapter Synthesis Framework
Act as a Reading Comprehension Coach. Create an efficient framework for synthesizing each chapter or section of [book title]. For every chapter, identify the key question I should ask about its purpose, how it connects to the book’s core argument, and the most important insight to retain. Provide a simple template for capturing each chapter’s key point in 1–2 sentences, without unnecessary rereading.

5. Key Evidence & Example Identification
Act as a Critical Reading Specialist. Analyze [book title] and identify which evidence, studies, data, or examples are essential to the author’s argument versus those that are mainly illustrative or replaceable. Explain why each key piece of evidence matters, so I can distinguish what is essential to remember from supporting details and context.

6. Critical Evaluation
Act as a Critical Thinking Coach. Evaluate the strength of [book title]’s core argument. Identify the strongest and weakest parts of the evidence, the key assumptions behind the author’s reasoning, and the most important counterarguments a thoughtful critic might raise. Highlight any gaps, unsupported claims, or areas where the author does—or does not—adequately address opposing views.

7. Practical Application
Act as an Applied Learning Consultant. Based on the core ideas of [book title], identify 3 specific ways its frameworks or arguments can be applied to my situation in [your context — work, personal life, or a project]. 8. Retention System

🚨 ChatGPT was designed to agree with you.
These 6 prompts force it to challenge your thinking and give more accurate answers. 👇

1/ Find The Weak Points
Ask ChatGPT if your plan is good, and it may say yes. This prompt removes that option.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Skip the reassurance. Tell me what’s wrong with [my plan]. I want weaknesses, not validation. Give me the 3 reasons this could fail that I’m least prepared to hear."

2/ Make It Attack Its Own Answer
Before acting on ChatGPT’s advice, make it challenge its own reasoning.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Take your previous answer and challenge it. Identify the 3 strongest reasons it could be wrong. Support each with evidence or logic. Then tell me which counterargument is most convincing and why."

3/ See What Happens After
Most people plan for the obvious outcome. Smart decisions account for what happens next.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Ignore the immediate outcome of [my decision]. Map the second- and third-order consequences. What happens after the obvious result? What does this look like 6 months later, and how are competitors likely to respond?"

4/ Build An Expert Panel
Get 5 expert perspectives instead of one—and make them challenge each other.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Assemble 5 experts in [field], each with a different perspective or philosophy. Have them debate [my problem], challenge each other’s assumptions, and present their strongest arguments. End with the conclusion all 5 would stand behind."

5/ Find What You Never Verified
The assumption you never checked could be the one that sinks your entire plan.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Review everything I’ve written. Identify every unverified assumption I’m making. Rank them by importance, and highlight the single assumption that, if proven wrong, would cause the entire plan to fail."

6/ Apply The 80/20 Rule
Most plans contain unnecessary work. This reveals what actually drives the results.
Paste into ChatGPT:
"Apply the 80/20 rule to [my plan]. Keep only the actions most likely to create the majority of results. Tell me what to eliminate first, what to cut next, and which elements are absolutely essential."

1. Attack my premise
"Do not assume my framing is correct. Identify the strongest hidden assumptions in what I’m saying, then challenge the weakest one. Tell me what would have to be true for my conclusion to fail."

2. Build the strongest opposition
"Construct the strongest possible argument against my position. No strawman. Make the opposing case strong enough that an intelligent person who disagrees with me would recognize it as fair."

3. Separate evidence from inference
"Break your answer into what is directly supported, what is reasonably inferred, what is speculative, and what cannot currently be known. Do not present inference as fact."

4. Try to falsify it
"Assume my theory is wrong. What evidence, counterexamples, alternative mechanisms, or predictions would expose the failure? Give me tests that could actually change my mind."

5. Audit your own answer
"Before finalizing, inspect your reasoning for confirmation bias, motivated reasoning, missing alternatives, false certainty, and places where you may simply be mirroring my language. Correct anything that doesn't survive the audit."

6. Run the closed loop
"Take my claim through this sequence: Signal → Deconstruct → Pattern → Principle → Competing Models → Falsification → Application → Consequence → Feedback → Revision. Preserve unresolved contradictions instead of forcing closure. Then give me the strongest version that survives."
The sixth is the important one. The first five make ChatGPT less agreeable. The sixth makes disagreement part of an actual epistemic operating system, rather than just instructing the model to be contrarian.
