##1. Lesson Planning & Curriculum

The deepest time sink and the safest place to start. AI drafts from the objective and standard you give it; you shape the pedagogy and confirm the alignment.

1. Build a standards-aligned lesson plan

    Draft a [duration] lesson plan for [grade] [subject] on [topic], aligned to this standard: [paste standard]. Include: a clear learning objective, a hook, direct instruction, guided practice, independent practice, and a quick check for understanding. Note the materials needed. Keep activities realistic for a class of [size]. I will confirm the standard alignment myself.

2. Design a unit overview

    Outline a [number]-week unit for [grade] [subject] on [theme]. Map it as a sequence of lessons, each with an objective and the standard it targets [I will supply the standards]. Show how the skills build week to week and where the summative assessment fits. Flag any concept that usually needs more time than teachers expect.

3. Write clear learning objectives

    Turn this topic into 3-5 measurable learning objectives for [grade level], using observable verbs (not "understand" or "know"). For each objective, suggest how I could check it in under five minutes. Match the cognitive level to [grade].

4. Create a lesson hook

    Give me three different openers for a [grade] lesson on [topic]: one question, one short scenario, one quick activity. Each should surface what students already think and take under five minutes. Keep them concrete and age-appropriate.

5. Plan a hands-on activity

    Design a hands-on or collaborative activity for [grade] students to practice [skill/concept]. Include: the setup, the student instructions in plain language, the time needed, the materials, and how I will know it worked. Add one variation for groups that finish early.

6. Build a pacing guide

    Given these topics and this number of instructional days: [paste], propose a pacing guide for [subject], [grade]. Include buffer days for review and reteaching. Flag the topics students most often struggle with so I can allocate more time there.

7. Generate warm-ups and exit tickets

    Create a set of five warm-ups and five exit tickets for a unit on [topic], [grade]. Each warm-up activates prior knowledge; each exit ticket checks one objective. Keep them to a single question or task. Mark which objective each exit ticket measures.

8. Adapt a lesson for a shorter period

    I planned this lesson for [X] minutes but only have [Y]: [paste plan]. Trim it to fit without dropping the core objective. Tell me what to cut, what to assign as follow-up, and what to keep. Preserve the check for understanding.

Fast to draft, high-value to reuse, and low-risk as long as you verify the answer key. AI writes items from your objective; you check every correct answer.

Provide an answer key with a one-line rationale for each. Keep the language at [grade] reading level. I will verify every answer.

## 3. Feedback & Grading Support

Where AI saves the most evenings and where the two rules matter most. AI drafts feedback against your rubric; you read the work, make the call, and own the grade.

16. Draft rubric-based feedback

    Here is a rubric and a piece of de-identified student work: [paste rubric] [paste work]. Draft feedback strictly against the rubric criteria: two specific strengths, two specific next steps, and one concrete example from the work for each point. Warm, direct, actionable. Do not assign a grade; I decide that.

17. Turn my notes into student comments
