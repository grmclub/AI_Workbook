##1. Lesson Planning & Curriculum

The deepest time sink and the safest place to start. AI drafts from the objective and standard you give it; you shape the pedagogy and confirm the alignment.

1. Build a standards-aligned lesson plan

    Draft a [duration] lesson plan for [grade] [subject] on [topic], aligned to this standard: [paste standard]. Include: a clear learning objective, a hook, direct instruction, guided practice, independent practice, and a quick check for understanding. Note the materials needed. Keep activities realistic for a class of [size]. I will confirm the standard alignment myself.

2. Design a unit overview

    Outline a [number]-week unit for [grade] [subject] on [theme]. Map it as a sequence of lessons, each with an objective and the standard it targets [I will supply the standards]. Show how the skills build week to week and where the summative assessment fits. Flag any concept that usually needs more time than teachers expect.

3. Write clear learning objectives

    Turn this topic into 3-5 measurable learning objectives for [grade level], using observable verbs (not "understand" or "know"). For each objective, suggest how I could check it in under five minutes. Match the cognitive level to [grade].

4. Create a lesson hook

    Give me three different openers for a [grade] lesson on [topic]: one question, one short scenario, one quick activity. Each should surface what students already think and take under five minutes. Keep them concrete and age-appropriate.

5. Plan a hands-on activity

    Design a hands-on or collaborative activity for [grade] students to practice [skill/concept]. Include: the setup, the student instructions in plain language, the time needed, the materials, and how I will know it worked. Add one variation for groups that finish early.

6. Build a pacing guide

    Given these topics and this number of instructional days: [paste], propose a pacing guide for [subject], [grade]. Include buffer days for review and reteaching. Flag the topics students most often struggle with so I can allocate more time there.

7. Generate warm-ups and exit tickets

    Create a set of five warm-ups and five exit tickets for a unit on [topic], [grade]. Each warm-up activates prior knowledge; each exit ticket checks one objective. Keep them to a single question or task. Mark which objective each exit ticket measures.

8. Adapt a lesson for a shorter period

    I planned this lesson for [X] minutes but only have [Y]: [paste plan]. Trim it to fit without dropping the core objective. Tell me what to cut, what to assign as follow-up, and what to keep. Preserve the check for understanding.

Fast to draft, high-value to reuse, and low-risk as long as you verify the answer key. AI writes items from your objective; you check every correct answer.

## 3. Feedback & Grading Support

Where AI saves the most evenings and where the two rules matter most. AI drafts feedback against your rubric; you read the work, make the call, and own the grade.

Draft feedback strictly against the rubric criteria: two specific strengths, two specific next steps, and one concrete example from the work for each point. Warm, direct, actionable.     Build a one-page study guide for [unit], [grade]. Include: the key ideas, the must-know vocabulary, a few worked examples, and five self-check questions with answers. Organize it so a student can actually revise from it, not just read it.

    Create a graphic organizer for [thinking task, e.g. comparing two texts / sequencing events / cause and effect], [grade]. Describe the layout, the prompts in each section, and how students fill it in. Keep it simple enough to use without a long explanation.

## 7. Administration & Productivity

The operational work that runs a classroom and never shows up in a lesson plan. Low risk, high time savings.

40. Draft a permission slip or letter home

    Draft a [permission slip / field-trip letter / general notice] for a [grade] activity: [details]. Include what families need to know, what to return and by when, and a tear-off or reply section. Clear, brief, and easy to sign. Leave logistics as placeholders for me.

41. Write a classroom policy or procedure

    Draft a clear, positively framed classroom procedure for [routine, e.g. entering the room / turning in work / group transitions], [grade]. Steps students can follow, the reason behind it in one line, and what "doing it well" looks like. Keep it age-appropriate.

42. Draft a recommendation letter

    Draft a recommendation letter for a student based only on these de-identified notes about their strengths and achievements: [paste]. Specific, warm, and credible, with concrete examples rather than generic praise. Leave the name and specifics as placeholders. I will confirm every claim.

43. Plan a substitute-teacher packet

    Create a substitute plan for a day in my [grade] classroom: [paste the lessons or activities]. Include the schedule, clear instructions for each block, classroom routines, what to do with early finishers, and who to contact for help. Written so someone who does not know my class can run the day.

44. Build a meeting agenda

    Draft a focused agenda for a [team / department / parent] meeting on [topic], to fit [duration]. List the items with time boxes, the decision or outcome each item needs, and a two-line pre-read. Keep it tight enough to actually finish on time.

45. Summarize a long document

    Summarize this [policy / report / email thread] into what I actually need to act on: [paste, de-identified]. Give me the key points, any deadline or action assigned to me, and anything that needs a decision. Flag anything ambiguous rather than guessing.

## 8. Professional Development & Reflection

AI as a patient thinking partner for your own growth. These prompts make it earn its keep.

46. Summarize PD into classroom actions

    I took these rough notes at a [PD session / conference / book study]: [paste]. Turn them into five key takeaways, then three concrete changes I could try in my [grade] classroom next week, each small enough to actually do. Mark anything I should read more about before trying.

47. Reflect on a lesson that did not land

    A lesson on [topic] did not go the way I hoped: [describe what happened, no student identifiers]. Ask me the questions a thoughtful instructional coach would ask to help me figure out why, then help me plan one specific change for next time. Do not just reassure me.

48. Brief me on a topic I need to teach

    I have to teach [topic] and want to be a step ahead of my students. Teach it to me: the core ideas, the common misconceptions, the questions students always ask, and the two or three places I am most likely to get a tricky question. I will verify before teaching.

49. Design a mini professional-growth plan

    Help me build a small professional-growth goal for this [term / year] around [skill, e.g. questioning techniques / formative assessment]. Give me a measurable goal, two or three practices to try, and a simple way to track whether it is working. Realistic for a full teaching load.

50. Draft a presentation for colleagues

    Help me outline a [PD session / staff meeting share-out] on [topic] for fellow teachers, to fit [duration]. Give me a clear structure, the three things they should leave with, one activity to keep it interactive, and the examples I should prepare. I will supply the classroom evidence.
