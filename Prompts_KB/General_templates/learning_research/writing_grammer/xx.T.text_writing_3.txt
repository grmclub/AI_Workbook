A smaller creator can be the smarter choice.
Audience trust can beat raw follower numbers.
Here are the 7 chȩcks 👇

1/
Follower count tells you how many people a creator can potentially reach.
It does not tell you whether that creator should represent your brand.
Before choosing a creator, evaluate seven things:
Audience overlap
Ask how much of their real audience resembles the customer you want.
Look beyond age and location. Check interests, problems, buying intent, professional level, lifestyle, and reasons people follow that creator.
A creator with 40,000 highly relevant followers can be more useful than one with 800,000 people who have little connection to your category.
Values
Look at what the creator consistently supports, criticizes, recommends, and refuses to promote.
A partnership creates an association between two reputations.
If your brand positions itself around simplicity, expertise, sustainability, affordability, premium quality, privacy, or another clear value, the creator's existing behavior should make that association believable.
Do not judge this from their bio alone.
Review several months of content and previous partnerships.

2/
Expertise
Ask:
"Why would this audience believe this particular person talking about our product?"
Expertise does not always require formal credentials.
It may come from:
• professional experience
• years creating around a niche
• demonstrated skill
• trusted product reviews
• lived experience
• repeated educational content
A cybersecurity company may gain more credibility from a respected security educator than from a much larger general technology creator.
Language
Study how the creator naturally communicates.
Do they sound analytical, entertaining, practical, aspirational, technical, beginner-friendly, premium, rebellious, or highly conversational?
Then compare that with how your brand needs to be understood.
Do not choose a creator and then force them to speak like your corporate advertising.
If their natural language cannot comfortably carry your positioning, the partnership is probably wrong.
Customer trust
Engagement rate alone does not measure trust.
Read the comments.
Look for signs that followers ask for advice, report trying recommendations, request opinions before buying, return for follow-up content, or challenge the creator when something feels inconsistent.
You are looking for influence over decisions, not simply reactions to content.
Deloitte's 2026 research found that recommendations from trusted creators can meaningfully increase consideration and purchasing behavior, while perceived authenticity is strongly connected with creator-influenced purchases.

3/
Content environment
Your product does not appear alone.
It appears between everything else the creator publishes.
Review:
• topics around the sponsored content
• recurring content formats
• production quality
• humor and tone
• frequency of sponsorships
• competing products promoted
• controversial themes
• how naturally products enter their content
Imagine your brand appearing inside that feed for six months.
Does it strengthen the way you want customers to perceive you?
That is a better test than asking whether one sponsored post would receive views.
Brand associations
Write down five words customers currently associate with the creator.
Then write down five words you want associated with your brand.
Example:
Creator:
credible
practical
technical
independent
detailed
Brand wants:
credible
professional
advanced
reliable
practical
That is potentially useful alignment.
Now imagine:
Creator:
luxury
celebrity
entertainment
trends
status
Brand wants:
affordable
simple
everyday
practical
accessible
Large reach does not solve that positioning conflict.
Every creator partnership teaches the market something about who your brand is.

4/
Here is the difference between reach-first and positioning-first creator marketing.
Reach-first:
"We need awareness."
→ Search for creators with large audiences
→ Compare follower counts and average views
→ Negotiate one sponsored post
→ Give everyone a similar campaign brief
→ Measure impressions, clicks, and immediate sales
→ Move to another group of creators next campaign
The creator is treated mainly as media inventory.
Positioning-first:
"We want customers to see us as the easiest accounting platform for independent businesses."
→ Define the customer and desired brand association
→ Find creators already trusted by freelancers and small-business owners
→ Check audience overlap and previous partnerships
→ Select creators whose expertise supports the positioning
→ Design the partnership around their normal content
→ Give them enough product knowledge to speak accurately
→ Allow their own voice to carry the message
→ Repeat the association over multiple pieces of content
→ Measure both commercial performance and whether the creator attracts the right audience
Now the creator is not simply distributing an advertisement.
They are helping reinforce what the brand means.

5/
A simple creator-fit scorecard can prevent follower count from dominating the decision.
Score every potential creator from 1 to 5 on:
Audience overlap: /5
Values alignment: /5
Relevant expertise: /5
Language and tone fit: /5
Audience trust: /5
Content environment: /5
Desired brand associations: /5
Then separately record:
Follower count
Average relevant-content views
Engagement
Cost
Previous brand partnerships
Competitor conflicts
Content quality
Expected deliverables
Keep "reach" and "fit" separate.
That makes it harder for a huge audience to hide a weak strategic match.
BCG's 2026 creator-marketing research found that high-performing marketers are more likely to prioritize audience trust rather than treating follower count and cost as the main selection signals. BCG also found leading programs are more likely to develop long-term creator relationships.
Deloitte's 2026 research makes a similar case: a smaller creator with strong influence and brand alignment may be more valuable than a larger creator whose audience is poorly aligned, and sustained partnerships can build familiarity and credibility that one-off campaigns cannot.
The practical progression is:
Find the right creator.
Test the working relationship.
Learn what content resonates.
Improve the collaboration.
Then deepen the partnership when the fit proves itself.
Reach can buy attention.
Fit determines what that attention teaches people about your brand.
