Prompt 1: Track Regional Macro Drivers
text
Act as a senior APAC equity strategist. Analyze how recent macroeconomic shifts—specifically regional central bank interest rate decisions, currency fluctuations against the USD, and regional inflation data—are impacting major APAC equity indices (e.g., Nikkei 225, ASX 200, Hang Seng, Nifty 50). Prompt 2: Evaluate Regulatory Shifts & Policy Impacts
text
Act as a financial regulatory analyst specializing in Asian markets. Review recent policy updates, security regulations, and trade restrictions originating from major APAC economies. Detail how these regulatory changes are influencing foreign institutional capital inflows and outflows across emerging versus developed APAC equity markets.


Prompt 3: Assess Cross-Border Supply Chain Vulnerabilities
text
Act as an equity research analyst covering the APAC technology and manufacturing sectors. Evaluate how ongoing regional supply chain reconfigurations and geopolitical tensions are impacting the earnings outlooks of top-tier semiconductor and hardware equities. Identify which specific geographic sub-regions are benefiting as alternative manufacturing hubs.


Prompt 4: Scan for Valuation Anomalies
text
Act as a quantitative equity analyst. Create a framework to identify sector-wide valuation anomalies across APAC markets. Compare current Forward P/E, P/B, and EV/EBITDA ratios against their 5-year historical averages for the Financials, Consumer Discretionary, and Technology sectors. Highlight which countries appear historically undervalued despite strong fundamentals.


Prompt 5: Monitor Sector-Specific Capital Flows
text
Act as a portfolio manager tracking APAC institutional data. Analyze recent sector-level capital flows, focusing heavily on Clean Energy, Electric Vehicles, and Banking sectors across the region. Synthesize market sentiment data to predict which sectors are positioned for the highest institutional momentum over the next 30 to 60 days.

##Details on trading circuit breakers and sidecar mechanics
https://global.krx.co.kr/contents/GLB/01/0109/0109000000/guide_to_trading_in_the_korean_stock_market.pdf
https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/6f915-korea-exchange-kospi-sell-sidecar

To maintain structural stability during periods of extreme volatility, the Korea Exchange (KRX) deploys two distinct layers of emergency market interventions: Sidecars and Circuit Breakers. [1]
Though they are often mentioned together, they serve very different mechanical functions in controlling rapid, panic-driven sell-offs. [1, 2, 3, 4]

1. The Sidecar Mechanic: The Preemptive "Speed Bump"
Think of a sidecar as a surgical, preemptive tool designed to cool down high-speed algorithmic and program trading before it can crash the broader spot market. [1, 2]
    • Targeted Impact: It does not stop the market. It only suspends automated, computer-driven program trading orders for 5 minutes.
    • Retail Exception: Manual, retail buy and sell orders from individual investors continue to execute completely uninterrupted.
    • Operational Limit: Can only be triggered once per trading day per market. It becomes completely inactive during the final 40 minutes of the trading day (after 15:10).
	
Trigger Thresholds by Index
=========================================================================================================================
Market        |Underlying Futures Index Monitored|Trigger Conditions (Must last ≥ 1 minute)
=========================================================================================================================
KOSPI Bourse  |KOSPI 200 Futures                 |Price moves ±5% or more from the previous day's close.
KOSDAQ Bourse |KOSDAQ 150 Futures                |Price moves ±6% (or up to ±3-5% depending on specific contract tiers)

=========================================================================================================================

2. Market-Wide Circuit Breakers: The Full Emergency Brake
If a sell-off deepens past programmatic volatility, the KRX activates its comprehensive, Market-Wide Circuit Breakers. Unlike the sidecar, this acts as a total trading freeze.
    • Total Freeze: Suspends all trading—including stocks, futures, options, after-hours trades, and treasury stock purchases.
    • The 1-Minute Rule: The drop must be sustained for at least one consecutive minute to trigger a phase.
    • Phase Restrictions: Phase 1 and Phase 2 cannot be triggered after 14:50. Phase 3 can trigger at any time until market close. [1]
The Three-Phase Trigger Hierarchy
    1. Phase 1 (8% Drop): If the index falls 8% or more below the previous day's close, the entire market is entirely suspended for 20 minutes. (Only order cancellations are allowed during this freeze). 
    2. Phase 2 (15% Drop): If the index drops 15% or more from the previous day's close AND falls an additional 1% or more from the Phase 1 activation point, market trading is suspended for another 20 minutes. 
    3. Phase 3 (20% Drop): If the index plummets 20% or more from the previous day's close AND drops an additional 1% or more from the Phase 2 activation point, the market closes for the remainder of the trading day.

Summary of Differences
    • Sidecars look exclusively at the futures market to temporarily block program algorithms for 5 minutes, allowing liquidity to naturally buffer. [1, 2]
    • Circuit Breakers look directly at the spot market indexes (KOSPI/KOSDAQ) and legally shut down the exchange floor to stop systemic panic

## 🚨 BREAKING: Claude has a hidden "STOCK ANALYSIS MODE."

Most people research stocks by jumping between charts, earnings reports, news, and financial statements.

With the right prompts, Claude can help you analyze a company like an equity research analyst, from fundamentals and valuation to risks, catalysts, and competitive advantage.

Here are 9 Claude prompts to activate it👇

1. Get a complete stock snapshot
Act as an equity research analyst. Analyze [COMPANY NAME / TICKER] as of [DATE] for an investor with a [SHORT/MEDIUM/LONG]-term horizon.
Cover:
• Business model and main revenue sources
• Customer segments and geographic exposure
• Competitive advantages
• Latest revenue, profit, margins, free cash flow and debt
• Recent stock-price performance
• Three potential growth drivers
• Three major risks
Use current, reputable sources when available. Attach a date and source to every important figure. Clearly label estimates, missing information and uncertain conclusions. Finish with a 5-bullet beginner-friendly summary.

2. Analyze the company’s fundamentals
Evaluate the fundamentals of [TICKER] using its latest [ANNUAL/QUARTERLY] results.
Create a table showing:
• Revenue growth
• Gross, operating and net margins
• EPS growth
• Free cash flow
• Cash and total debt
• Return on equity
• Share-count changes
Compare each metric with the previous [3/5] years and explain whether the trend is improving, stable or weakening. Flag one-time items and accounting concerns. Do not invent unavailable numbers. End with the company’s two strongest and two weakest financial characteristics.

3. Review the latest earnings report
Analyze the latest earnings release and conference call for [TICKER].… See more

4. Estimate a reasonable valuation
Build a valuation analysis for [TICKER] using current financial data as of [DATE].
Use the valuation methods appropriate for this company, such as:
• P/E
• EV/EBITDA
• Price-to-sales
• Free-cash-flow yield
• Discounted cash flow
Compare its valuation with [3–5 PEERS] and its own historical range. Create conservative, base and optimistic scenarios using clearly stated assumptions. Estimate a possible value range—not a guaranteed price target—and show which assumptions have the greatest impact. Flag unreliable or missing data.

5. Compare competing stocks
Compare [TICKER 1], [TICKER 2] and [TICKER 3] as potential investments over [TIME HORIZON].
Score each company from 1–10 on:
• Revenue growth
• Profitability
• Balance-sheet strength
• Cash-flow quality
• Valuation
• Competitive advantage
• Management execution
• Risk
Show the supporting evidence in a table. Explain where each company wins or loses and identify which type of investor each may suit. Do not select a winner until completing the comparison. State what new information could change the ranking.

6. Find catalysts and hidden risks
Create a catalyst-and-risk map for [TICKER] covering the next [6/12/24] months.
For every catalyst and risk, include:
• Expected time frame
• Likely probability: low, medium or high
• Possible business impact
• Metrics or events to monitor
• Evidence supporting the assessment
Include company-specific, industry, regulatory, competitive and macroeconomic factors. Separate confirmed events from speculation. Rank the five factors most likely to change the investment thesis.

7. Build a bull, base and bear case
Develop bull, base and bear cases for [TICKER] over [TIME HORIZON].
For each case, specify:
• Revenue-growth assumption
• Margin assumption
• Valuation assumption
• Major catalysts
• Major risks
• Conditions required for the scenario
• Plausible valuation range
Assign a probability to each case and explain the reasoning without pretending the probabilities are precise. Then list three measurable signals that would indicate which scenario is unfolding.

8. Perform technical analysis
Analyze the price and volume data for [TICKER] over [TIME PERIOD]. Use live market data only if you can verify it; otherwise ask me to upload or paste the data.
Evaluate:
• Primary trend
• Support and resistance zones
• 20-, 50- and 200-day moving averages
• Relative Strength Index
• Volume behavior
• Volatility
• Potential breakout or breakdown levels
Explain every indicator in simple language. Provide conditional scenarios—not predictions—and identify the price level that would invalidate each scenario. State the data date and market session used.

9. Create a disciplined investment checklist
Turn the research on [TICKER] into a decision checklist for an investor with:
• Portfolio size: [AMOUNT]
• Risk tolerance: [LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH]
• Time horizon: [PERIOD]
• Maximum acceptable loss: [PERCENTAGE]
• Existing sector exposure: [DETAILS]
Include:
1. Reasons to consider the stock
2. Reasons to avoid it
3. Questions still unanswered
4. Metrics to monitor each quarter
5. Conditions that would strengthen the thesis
6. Conditions that would invalidate it
7. Key diversification and concentration concerns
Do not tell me to buy or sell or claim a guaranteed return. Highlight any information that requires verification by a licensed financial professional.
