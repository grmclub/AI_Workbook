🚨 BREAKING: CLAUDE has a feature called Travel Planner Mode.
You can use it to plan a whole trip down to the day, like a personal travel agent.
Here are 6 prompts to unlock it:

1/ The Activation
“Act as a seasoned travel agent who has planned thousands of trips people actually loved. Ask me my destination, dates, budget, travel style (chill or packed), and who’s coming with me. Once I answer, build the plan like you know this place personally, not like you’re summarizing a search result.”
Why it works: Most people skip this step and just ask for an itinerary right away. This prompt forces Claude to ask questions first, so the plan actually fits you instead of a generic tourist.

2/ The Day by Day
“Build me a full day by day plan for this trip. Balance the must see spots with real downtime. Group things that are close together so I’m not wasting hours in transit. Leave room in the schedule to wander and stumble onto something on my own.”
Why it works: A list of attractions isn’t a plan. This prompt makes Claude think like a local guide, grouping locations by area and pacing the days so you don’t burn out by day two.

3/ The Local Only List
“Skip the tourist traps completely. Give me the spots locals in this destination actually go for food, coffee, and evenings out. Include at least one thing most tourists never even find.”
Why it works: Claude has read thousands of blogs, forums, and local recommendations. This prompt pulls from that instead of the same five spots every travel site repeats.

4/ The Real Cost
“Break down the real cost of this trip. Flights, stays, food, transport, and activities. Show me exactly where I can save without ruining the experience, and flag anything that’s usually more expensive than people expect.”
Why it works: Budgets fall apart because of hidden costs, not the big ones. This prompt catches the small stuff before it wrecks your trip.

5/ The Packing Formula
“Build me a packing list based on the weather and what I’m actually doing on this trip. Nothing forgotten, nothing extra. Split it into carry on versus checked bag.”
Why it works: Generic packing lists ignore your actual plans. This one ties every item to a real day, real weather, and real activity, so you’re not carrying dead weight.

6/ The Plan B
“For each day, give me a backup plan in case of rain, closures, or something falling through. One bad break should never wreck the whole day.”
Why it works: Trips fail on the days nobody planned for. This prompt builds in flexibility before you even land.
Be careful using this for places that need real, deep local expertise. Somewhere remote, off the grid, or hard to navigate (think places like Madagascar) still needs a real travel agent or someone who has actually been there. Claude can’t feel a neighborhood, and it doesn’t know what closed last week.

⚡ Pro Tips

- Give real dates, not vague ones like “sometime in spring”
- Always state your travel style upfront, chill, packed, or mixed
- Tell it who’s traveling. Solo, couple, family with kids changes everything
- If one day feels off, ask Claude to redo just that day, not the whole trip
- Ask for two or three options per activity, not just one
- Push back if a suggestion feels too “top 10 list” generic
- Always ask “what would a local skip” as a follow up question

The Truth

Claude is not a real travel agent.
It doesn’t know if that hidden gem restaurant closed last month.
It can’t feel the energy of a street at night.
It’s a research assistant, not a replacement for someone who has actually been there.
Use it to skip hours of googling and tab hoarding.
Don’t skip double checking anything that actually matters, safety, opening hours, visa rules, or remote areas with limited info online.

🚀 Quick Start

1. Open Claude and paste the Activation prompt
2. Answer its questions honestly, don’t rush this part
3. Ask for the Day by Day plan
4. Ask for the Local Only List
5. Ask for the Real Cost breakdown
6. Ask for the Packing Formula and the Plan B for each day
7. Cross check anything time sensitive with a quick real search

🧳 Bottom Line: Claude can save you hours of planning and make your trip feel like someone actually thought it through. But it’s a first draft, not the final word, especially anywhere remote or unpredictable. Plan smart, then double check the parts that matter.

suggest common prompts for travel planning 

Planning a trip usually involves several stages—from brainstorming where to go to building a day-by-day itinerary. Here are some of the most effective prompts you can use, broken down by stage:


1. Destination & Idea Generation
Use these when you know how you want to feel or travel, but don't know where to go yet.
    By Vibe & Budget:
        "Suggest 3 destination ideas for a 7-day trip in [month/season] with a focus on [nature/food/history/nightlife]. Budget is around $[amount] total, starting from [home city]."
    By Weather & Season:
        "Where are the best places to travel in [month] for warm weather, low rainfall, and manageable crowds?"
    Family or Group Travel:
        "Recommend 3 trip destinations that work well for [e.g., a family with a 5-year-old and an 8-year-old / a group of 6 adults with mixed mobility]."

2. Itinerary Building
Use these once you have a destination and dates locked in.
    Paced Day-by-Day:
        "Create a realistic [number]-day itinerary for [City/Country]. Keep it [relaxed/moderate/fast-paced], grouping activities by neighborhood so we don't waste time commuting."
    First-Timer's High-Level Plan:
        "I'm spending [number] days in [City]. What are the top 'must-see' highlights, and how should I divide my days between them?"
    Balanced Interests:
        "Build a 5-day itinerary for [City] that balances historical sight-seeing in the mornings with relaxed local food/café exploration in the afternoons."

3. Food, Culture & Local Experiences
Use these to find authentic experiences away from typical tourist traps.
    Food & Dining:
        "What are 5 iconic local dishes I must try in [City], and what neighborhood or type of restaurant is best to get them?"
    Hidden Gems:
        "Give me 4 off-the-beaten-path activities or lesser-known spots in [City] that most tourists miss."
    Day Trips:
        "What are the best day trips reachable by public transit from [City] within 1.5 hours?"

4. Practical Logistics & Packing
Use these to handle the tedious details before you leave.
    Neighborhood & Base Selection:
        "Which neighborhood in [City] should I stay in if I want to be [walkable to sights / near great nightlife / quiet and family-friendly]?"
    Custom Packing List:
        "Create a packing checklist for a [number]-day trip to [Destination] in [Month]. Factor in [activities like hiking, fancy dinners, swimming] and carrying only [hand luggage / a checked bag]."
    Cultural Etiquette & Tips:
        "What are crucial cultural etiquette rules, tipping customs, and common safety/scam tips I should know before visiting [Country]?"

##Help me create a customized day-by-day itinerary for an upcoming trip

I'd love to help you build a personalized, day-by-day itinerary!
To get started, please share a few details about your upcoming trip:
    Destination: Where are you going (or which cities/regions are you visiting)?
    Dates & Duration: How many days will you be there, and what time of year are you traveling?
    Pace & Vibe: Do you prefer a packed schedule covering all the main highlights, or a more relaxed, flexible pace with free time?
    Interests & Priorities: What are your main interests? (e.g., food & dining, historical landmarks, outdoor adventure, shopping, museums, nightlife, local hidden gems)
    Travel Group & Logistics: Who is traveling? (e.g., solo, couple, family with young kids, group of friends) Do you plan to walk, use public transit, or rent a car?

Once you give me those details, I'll draft a neighborhood-organized itinerary for you!


##Suggest some travel destinations based on my budget and preferred season.

To give you the best recommendations tailored to you, tell me a bit more about what you're looking for:
    Season or Month: When are you planning to travel?
    Budget: What is your target budget per person (or overall), and does that include flights?
    Starting Location / Departure Region: Where will you be flying or traveling from?
    Vibe & Activities: Are you looking for beach/relaxation, historical sightseeing, food and culture, nature/hiking, or vibrant city life?
    Trip Length: How many days or weeks do you plan to be away?
Once you share a few of these, I'll tailor 3–4 destination options with estimated costs, highlights, and best areas to stay!
