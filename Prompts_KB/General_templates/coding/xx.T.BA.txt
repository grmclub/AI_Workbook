1/ The BA role is changing
Business Analysts have always connected business teams with technology teams. AI is changing what that connection requires.
Traditional BA work often focused on gathering requirements, documenting processes, and translating business needs for technical teams.
Now, AI adds another layer.
A strong BA must understand what the business actually needs, where AI can create value, and where AI is the wrong solution.
The goal is no longer simply to translate requirements.
It is to help the organization make better decisions before development begins.

2/ Ask better questions first
The biggest advantage of an AI-aware BA is not knowing every AI tool.
It is knowing which questions to ask before anyone starts building.
For example:
➔ What business problem are we actually solving?
➔ Is AI necessary, or would a simpler solution work better?
➔ What data would the solution need?
➔ How would we measure whether it creates value?
➔ What risks could affect users, operations, or decisions?
➔ Where should humans remain involved?
These questions prevent teams from building impressive technology that solves the wrong problem.

3/ Understand both sides deeply
An effective BA needs enough business understanding to identify the real pain point and enough technology understanding to evaluate possible solutions.
That means looking at:
➔ Business goals and measurable outcomes.
➔ Existing workflows and operational bottlenecks.
➔ Data availability and quality.
➔ AI capabilities and limitations.
➔ Technical feasibility and dependencies.
➔ User needs and adoption challenges.
You do not need to become a full-time developer or AI researcher.
But you do need enough technical understanding to have meaningful conversations with the people building the solution.

4/ Become the bridge before building begins
The highest-impact BA is not simply the person who passes requirements from one team to another.
They help both sides understand what should actually be built.

A practical workflow is:
➔ Start with the business outcome.
➔ Identify the real process or problem.
➔ Question whether AI is appropriate.
➔ Explore possible AI-enabled approaches.
➔ Define measurable success criteria.
➔ Identify risks, constraints, and human oversight.
➔ Then translate the decision into clear requirements.
That is where the BA becomes more than a bridge between business and technology.
They become part of the decision-making layer that determines whether the right thing gets built.
