Here are 20 marketing experiments you can run 👇

1/ Test Your Offers & Messaging
Start with experiments that reveal what makes people pay attention.
➔ Test two landing page headlines.
➔ Compare a feature-focused offer with an outcome-focused offer.
➔ Test two value propositions.
➔ Try different calls to action.
➔ Compare different discount or incentive structures.
The important part is not making the test complicated.
Choose one hypothesis, change one meaningful variable, and measure the outcome. A small test can tell you more than weeks of debating which message sounds better.

2/ Experiment With Content
You can run useful content experiments without spending heavily on advertising.
➔ Test two email subject lines.
➔ Compare short-form and long-form content.
➔ Test different post formats.
➔ Repurpose one idea across multiple platforms.
➔ Test different hooks.
➔ Turn one successful experiment into a content series.
The goal is to discover what your specific audience responds to.
Track the metric connected to your objective, such as clicks, engagement, signups, replies, or conversions.

3/ Test Low-Cost Acquisition Channels
Don't assume you need a large ad budget to discover a new acquisition channel.
Experiment with:
➔ A small Google Search campaign.
➔ A niche creator collaboration.
➔ Guest content.
➔ Community participation.
➔ Referral campaigns.
➔ Local events or small pop-ups.
➔ Guerrilla marketing ideas.
The key is to start small.
Your first objective isn't massive revenue. It's learning whether a channel can consistently attract the right audience at an acceptable cost.

4/ Experiment With Conversion & Customer Experience
You can also test what happens after someone discovers your business.
Try:
➔ Shortening your lead form.
➔ Testing a different CTA.
➔ Changing your onboarding sequence.
➔ Testing two pricing presentations.
➔ Offering a different lead magnet.
➔ Testing a referral incentive.
➔ Comparing two follow-up sequences.
Change one important variable at a time.
If you change the headline, offer, CTA, and page design simultaneously, you won't know what caused the result.

5/ Turn Every Experiment Into a Learning System
The most valuable part of a marketing experiment isn't the result.
It's what you learn from it.
Use this simple process:
➔ Hypothesis: What do you think will happen?
➔ Variable: What exactly will you change?
➔ Metric: What will determine success?
➔ Test: Run the experiment under comparable conditions.
➔ Result: Record what happened.
➔ Insight: Explain why it may have happened.
➔ Next test: Decide what to test next.
Document the process, including failures.
A failed experiment can become useful marketing content when you explain the hypothesis, method, result, and lesson. This turns your own marketing activity into original research that can build credibility and generate future content.
The goal isn't to find one magical growth tactic.
It's to build a habit of testing, learning, and continuously improving.
