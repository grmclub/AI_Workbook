## Claude is replacing interns, analysts, consultants, marketers, and project managers.
The people winning with AI aren't asking better questions.
They're building better systems.
Here are 8 Claude prompts that feel like hiring an entire team 👇

1. The Personal Board of Directors Prompt
Prompt:
"<role>
Act as my personal board of directors made up of five expert advisors: a CEO, an investor, a marketer, an operator, and a psychologist.
</role>
<task>
Review my goal from multiple expert perspectives, challenge my assumptions, identify risks, and combine the best advice into one clear action plan.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my goal, current situation, resources, timeline, constraints, and biggest concern before starting
2. Create five expert personas: CEO, investor, marketer, operator, and psychologist
3. Have each expert analyze the goal from their perspective
4. For each expert, provide recommendations, challenged assumptions, risks, and improvements
5. Identify where the experts agree and disagree
6. Combine the strongest advice into one practical action plan
7. Define the first 3 moves I should make
</steps>
<rules>
- Each expert must think differently
- Do not give generic motivational advice
- Recommendations must be specific and actionable
- Challenge weak assumptions directly
- Risks must be realistic
- Final plan must combine the best thinking, not repeat everything
- Test: would this help me see the goal from multiple angles before acting
</rules>
<output>
Goal Summary → CEO Perspective → Investor Perspective → Marketer Perspective → Operator Perspective → Psychologist Perspective → Expert Agreement/Disagreement → Final Action Plan → First 3 Moves
</output>"
Why this works:
Most people only think from one perspective.
That's dangerous.
Claude can simulate multiple viewpoints instantly.
Instead of one answer...
You get a room full of experts debating your idea.
That leads to smarter decisions and fewer costly mistakes.

2. The $10K Offer Builder Prompt
Prompt:
"<role>
Act as a high-ticket offer strategist who turns skills, expertise, and experience into premium offers people are willing to pay more for.
</role>
<task>
Turn my skill into a premium offer with clear positioning, target audience, pricing, delivery model, bonuses, guarantee, objection handling, and sales messaging.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my skill, experience level, proof, target audience, niche, current offer if any, and income goal before starting
2. Identify the most valuable problem my skill can solve
3. Define the ideal high-ticket buyer
4. Create a premium offer promise and positioning angle
5. Build the delivery model: done-for-you, done-with-you, coaching, consulting, system, audit, or hybrid
6. Recommend pricing and package structure
7. Add bonuses, guarantee options, objection handling, and sales messaging
8. Create a simple validation plan before selling at scale
</steps>
<rules>
- Offer must solve an urgent and valuable problem
- Do not make the offer premium only by raising the price
- Pricing must match proof, market demand, and transformation value
- Bonuses must remove objections or increase success
- Guarantee must be realistic and ethical
- Sales messaging must be clear and believable
- Test: would a qualified buyer understand why this is worth paying premium for
</rules>
<output>
Skill → Valuable Problem → Target Buyer → Premium Positioning → Offer Promise → Delivery Model → Pricing → Bonuses → Guarantee → Objection Handling → Sales Messaging → Validation Plan
</output>"

Why this works:
Most people sell services.
Top performers sell outcomes.
Claude helps package expertise into something customers actually want to buy.
The difference between a $500 offer and a $10,000 offer is often positioning.

3. The Competitor Intelligence Prompt
Prompt:
"<role>
Act as a competitive intelligence analyst who studies competitors’ positioning, pricing, offers, content, audience, strengths, weaknesses, and exploitable gaps.
</role>
<task>
Analyze competitors in my niche and identify where I can differentiate, compete smarter, and capture market gaps.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my niche, business, target audience, offer, top competitors, and current positioning before starting
2. Analyze each competitor’s strengths, weaknesses, pricing, offer structure, and audience
3. Study their positioning and core messaging
4. Analyze their content strategy and distribution channels
5. Identify what they do well and where they create customer friction
6. Find gaps they ignore, underserve, or communicate poorly
7. Recommend positioning, offer, content, and pricing counter-moves
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not copy competitors
- Separate visible facts from strategic assumptions
- Weaknesses must be specific
- Gaps must matter to customers
- Counter-moves must be realistic for my resources
- Avoid competing only on price
- Test: would this help me create a sharper market position
</rules>
<output>
Competitor Map → Strengths → Weaknesses → Positioning → Pricing → Audience → Content Strategy → Market Gaps → Counter-Moves
</output>"
Why this works:
Most businesses copy competitors.
Smart businesses study competitors.
Claude helps uncover opportunities hiding in plain sight.
The goal isn't being better.
The goal is being different.

5. The One-Person Company Prompt
Prompt:
"<role>
Act as an automation-first entrepreneur who designs lean one-person companies powered by tools, workflows, AI systems, content engines, and scalable operations.
</role>
<task>
Show me how to run a one-person company around my business idea using automation, simple systems, and low-friction workflows.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my business idea, target customer, offer, budget, technical skill level, available time, and income goal before starting
2. Define the simplest business model for a solo operator
3. Map the core workflows: content, lead generation, sales, delivery, onboarding, support, and reporting
4. Recommend tools for each workflow
5. Identify what to automate, template, delegate, or keep manual
6. Build a content system that attracts leads consistently
7. Design a lightweight customer support system
8. Create a scaling strategy that does not require a large team
</steps>
<rules>
- Keep the company lean and simple
- Do not automate chaos
- Use low-cost tools where possible
- Automations must save time or increase revenue
- Delivery must remain high-quality
- Scaling strategy must fit a one-person operation
- Test: could I run this without burning out or hiring immediately
</rules>
<output>
Business Idea → One-Person Business Model → Core Workflows → Tool Stack → Automations → Content System → Customer Support System → Scaling Strategy
</output>"
Why this works:
The internet rewards leverage more than ever.
One person with AI can now outperform entire teams.
Claude helps identify where technology can replace repetitive work.
That's how modern businesses scale efficiently.

7. The AI Operations Manual Prompt
Prompt:
"<role>
Act as an operations consultant who creates SOPs, workflows, checklists, and documentation that make a business more efficient, consistent, and scalable.
</role>
<task>
Create operational systems for my business so tasks can be repeated, delegated, improved, and scaled.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask for my business model, core services/products, recurring tasks, team structure, tools, bottlenecks, and quality standards before starting
2. Identify the most important workflows that need documentation
3. Create SOPs for high-frequency or high-risk tasks
4. Build checklists for execution and quality control
5. Design workflow maps for core processes
6. Identify where automation, delegation, or simplification would improve efficiency
7. Create a documentation structure for the business
8. Recommend the first 5 SOPs to build
</steps>
<rules>
- SOPs must be practical, not bloated
- Workflows must reduce confusion
- Checklists must prevent errors
- Documentation must be easy to update
- Optimize for consistency before scale
- Do not over-systemize tasks that rarely happen
- Test: could someone else follow the process without asking many questions
</rules>
<output>
Business Overview → Core Workflows → SOPs Needed → Workflow Maps → Checklists → Automation/Delegation Opportunities → Documentation Structure → First 5 SOPs
</output>"
Why this works:
Most businesses depend on memory.
Great businesses depend on systems.
Claude can turn messy processes into repeatable frameworks.
That's how chaos becomes execution.

3. Predict how customer behavior, technology, competition, regulation, and economics may change over the next 3 years
4. Identify threats that could weaken my position
5. Identify opportunities I can move on early
6. Recommend skills I should learn or strengthen
7. Suggest strategic moves I should make now
8. Build a 12-month preparation roadmap
</steps>
<rules>
- Separate likely trends from speculative hype
- Explain the logic behind each prediction
- Focus on practical strategic implications
- Threats and opportunities must be specific
- Skills must connect to future demand
- Avoid vague futurism
- Test: would this help me prepare before the market shifts
</rules>
<output>
Industry Context → 3-Year Trends → Threats → Opportunities → Skills to Learn → Strategic Moves → 12-Month Roadmap
</output>"
Why this works:
Most people react too late.
The biggest opportunities belong to people who prepare early.
Claude helps you think ahead instead of constantly catching up.
That's a huge competitive advantage.
