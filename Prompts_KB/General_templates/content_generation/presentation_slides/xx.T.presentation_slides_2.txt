1. Start Any Presentation Right

Prompt:
<role> Act as a presentation coach applying Patrick Winston's MIT framework - every talk must open with an empowerment promise that tells the audience exactly what they will know by the end that they didn't know at the beginning. </role>

<task>Write a powerful opening for my presentation that makes the audience immediately understand why staying is worth every minute of their time. </task>

<steps>
1. Ask for my presentation topic, audience, and desired outcome before starting
2. Identify the single most valuable thing my audience will walk away knowing
3. Write the empowerment promise - specific, outcome-driven, impossible to ignore
4. Design the first 60 seconds - promise, context, and why this matters now
5. Flag everything that should be cut from the opening - jokes, thank yous, apologies
</steps>

<rules>
• Never open with a joke - audience isn't ready
• Never open with "thank you for having me" - weak and forgettable
• Empowerment promise must be specific - not "you'll learn about X" but by the end you'll be able to do Y
• First 60 seconds must earn the next 60 minutes
• Cut everything that doesn't serve the promise </rules>

<output>Empowerment Promise -> First 60 Seconds -> What to Cut -> Opening Script</output>

2. Eliminate Your Slide Crimes

Prompt:
<role>Act as a slide audit expert using a strict MIT-style framework. Identify and remove anything that causes audiences to disengage. </role>
<task>Review my slides and fix every issue that reduces clarity, focus, or engagement.</task>

<steps>
1. Pause and ask me to share my slides or describe each one (title, text, visuals). Don't proceed without this.
2. Evaluate each slide against key issues:
too many slides, too much text, small fonts, reading slides word-for-word, poor positioning, lack of white space, cluttered backgrounds, weak final slide, and more.
3. For every issue, specify: slide number, problem, and exact fix.
4. Redesign the final slide to clearly restate what the audience has learned. 5. Provide a clean summary: what to keep, remove, and improve.
</steps>

<rules>
• Every issue must include a clear fix.
• Minimum font size: 40pt. No exceptions.
• Final slide must reinforce key takeaways, not "thank you" or "questions."
• White space improves understanding. Use it intentionally.
• Slides should support the talk, not replace it.
</rules>

<output>Slide Audit -> Fixes -> Final Slide Redesign -> Clean Summary</output>

3. Make Your Ideas Unforgettable

Prompt:
<role>Act as a brand strategist using a proven framework (Symbol, Slogan, Surprise, Salient Idea, Story) to make ideas stick.</role>

<task>Transform my core idea into something memorable that stays with the audience long after the presentation.</task>

<steps>
1. Pause and ask three questions: (a) core idea in one sentence, (b) target audience, (c) one key takeaway they should remember after a week. Wait for answers.
2. Symbol: suggest a clear visual or object that instantly represents the idea.
3. Slogan: create a short, repeatable phrase (under 8 words).
4. Surprise: highlight a belief the audience holds, then flip it with a counterintuitive truth.
5. Salient idea: define the ONE core idea that stands above everything else.
6. Story: write a short narrative (4-6 sentences) explaining how it works and why it matters.
7. Combine everything into a single, clear summary.
</steps>

<rules> Keep the symbol visual and specific. Make the slogan simple and repeatable. Ensure the surprise truly challenges assumptions. Focus on one core idea only. Keep the story specific yet relatable. Ask instead of assuming missing details. </rules>

<output>Symbol -> Slogan -> Surprise -> Salient Idea -> Story -> Final Summary</output>

4. Structure Any Talk That Persuades

Prompt:
<role>Act as a persuasion strategist using a proven framework (vision, proof, contributions) to make any talk convincing and action-driven.</role>

<task>Organize my talk so the audience quickly understands the vision, trusts the work, and clearly remembers the value delivered. </task>

<steps>
1. Pause and ask three questions: (a) goal of the talk, (b) target audience, (c) one action they should take after. Wait for answers.
2. Define the vision: clearly state the problem and your unique approach (2-3 sentences).
3. Show proof: list 3-5 concrete actions that demonstrate real work done.
4. Write the first 5 minutes word-for-word (600-750 words) to establish credibility and direction.
5. Create the closing slide: clearly list what the audience now gains.
6. Map the full structure, ensuring each section reinforces either vision or proof.
</steps>

<rules> Establish the vision within the first 5 minutes. Use specific actions as proof-not vague claims. Make the opening and closing mirror each other. Keep the contributions slide visible during Q&A. Every part of the talk must support vision or proof. </rules>

<output>Vision -> Proof -> Opening Script -> Contributions -> Full Structure</output>

5. Use Props and Stories to Teach Anything

Prompt:
<role>Act as a teaching strategist using proven prop and storytelling methods to turn complex ideas into clear, tangible experiences. </role>

<task>Create a prop or demo that makes a difficult concept feel simple and easy to grasp.</task>

<steps>
1. Pause and ask four questions: (a) the concept to explain, (b) audience, (c) presentation format (in-person/virtual), (d) what confuses people most. Wait for answers.
2. Define the core confusion in one clear sentence.
3. Design a physical prop or demo that removes that confusion (adapt for virtual if needed).
4. Build a 3-part story: tension (the confusion), demonstration (prop in action), resolution (clarity).
5. Write a 200-300 word script guiding attention, including when to show or use the prop.
6. Deliver a complete, ready-to-practice teaching flow.
</steps>

<rules> Use real, physical props. not slides. Start with real confusion before resolving it. Guide attention clearly during the demo. Ensure the demo teaches even if something goes wrong. For virtual, use real objects on camera. </rules>

<output>Confusion -> Prop -> Story -> Script -> Teaching Flow</output>

1. Turn Any Idea Into a Full Presentation
Prompt:
Act as an expert presentation strategist. Create a complete presentation about [topic/idea] for [target audience]. The goal of the presentation is to [educate/persuade/sell/explain]. Build a logical story from beginning to end with around [10–15] slides. For every slide, give me a strong slide title, the key message, concise supporting content, and a suggestion for the best visual, chart, diagram, screenshot, or image to use. Keep the slides simple, engaging, and easy to understand. Avoid long paragraphs and do not invent statistics or facts.

2. Create a Business Pitch Deck
Prompt:
Turn my business idea into a professional pitch deck. My business is [describe the business], our target customers are [audience], and the main problem we solve is [problem]. Create a clear presentation covering the problem, current alternatives, our solution, how the product works, target market, business model, competitive advantage, marketing strategy, traction, future roadmap, and final call to action. Make each slide focused on one main idea and suggest visuals that would make the pitch easier to understand. If important information is missing, mark it as information I need to add instead of making it up.

3. Turn a Report Into Presentation Slides
Prompt:
Turn the following report, document, or long piece of text into a presentation: [paste content]. Identify the most important ideas and remove unnecessary details. Organize the information into a logical [number] slide presentation for [audience]. Give each slide a clear headline, 3–5 concise supporting points, and a recommended visual or chart where appropriate. Highlight important numbers, findings, trends, problems, and recommendations. Keep the meaning of the original information accurate and do not add unsupported facts.

4. Create a Sales Presentation
Prompt:
Create a persuasive sales presentation for [product/service] aimed at [target customer]. Their main problems are [problems], and my product helps by [solution]. Structure the presentation so it first makes the customer understand the problem, then shows the cost of continuing with the current approach, introduces our solution, explains the major benefits, handles likely objections, shows proof or examples I provide, and ends with a strong next step. Write short slide copy instead of long paragraphs and suggest useful visuals for every slide.

5. Create a Training Presentation
Prompt:
Create a beginner-friendly training presentation that teaches [topic/skill] to [audience]. Assume they currently know [beginner/intermediate level] about the topic. Start by explaining why the skill matters, then teach the process step by step using simple language, practical examples, common mistakes, best practices, and an action checklist. Create approximately [number] slides. For each slide, provide the headline, key teaching points, an example where useful, and a visual suggestion. Make the presentation practical enough that someone can take action immediately after viewing it.

6. Create a Data or Performance Presentation
Prompt:
Create an executive-style presentation from the following performance data: [paste data/results]. The presentation will be shown to [manager/client/team/investors] and its purpose is to explain what happened, why it happened, and what we should do next. Organize the presentation around the most important KPIs, major changes, positive results, problems, trends, possible causes, opportunities, and recommended next actions. Suggest the most suitable chart for each important dataset and write clear takeaway-style slide headlines instead of generic headings such as “Results” or “Data.”

7. Create a Proposal Presentation
Prompt:
Turn this proposal into a professional presentation for [client/company]: [describe or paste proposal]. Structure it around their current situation, the main problem, the desired outcome, our recommended solution, how the process will work, deliverables, timeline, expected benefits, pricing or investment if provided, and the next step. Make the presentation focused on the client's needs rather than talking excessively about us. Give me the exact content for every slide and recommend visuals that can make the proposal look clearer and more convincing.

8. Create a Webinar or Workshop Deck

Create a presentation for a [30/45/60]-minute webinar or workshop about [topic] for [audience]. Start with a strong hook that explains why people should care, then introduce the problem, teach the most important concepts, show practical examples, provide a step-by-step framework, cover common mistakes, and finish with an action plan and call to action. Tell me approximately how much time to spend on each section. Keep individual slides visually simple, and include suggestions for demonstrations, examples, audience questions, or interactive moments.

9. Create a Product Launch Presentation
Prompt:

Build excitement around the problem first, then introduce the product as the solution. Include slides for the major features, benefits, how it works, real-world use cases, comparison with the old way of doing things, proof or testimonials I provide, pricing if available, and a strong final call to action. Write punchy slide headlines and minimal supporting text, and recommend a visual concept for every slide.

Then rebuild the presentation so every slide communicates one clear idea and naturally leads into the next slide. Rewrite weak titles as strong takeaway headlines, shorten overly long text, identify slides that should be removed or combined, and recommend additional slides only where something important is missing. For every final slide, give me the title, final copy, and recommended visual or layout.
