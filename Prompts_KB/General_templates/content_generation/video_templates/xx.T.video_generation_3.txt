## Tested the "Free AI Video" Posts. Here's What Actually Works.
Most of these posts recycle the same 6 tools and never mention the limits.
Here's my honest 2026 version. 10 tools, real prompts, no fluff.

BOTTOM LINE
Use 2 tools to draft, 1 to finish, 1 as backup. That's the whole game.
Comment STACK and I'll send you the one-page prompt sheet — all 10 starters, plus a face-lock version and a product version.
Follow for the next list. I only post tools I can still open this week.

QUICK START
1. Pick one photo with a clear face or product
2. Test tiny motion on Vheer or Dola
3. Take the same photo to Kling, use the face-lock prompt
4. If it feels flat, remake it in Hailuo or Flow with a camera move
5. Phone only? Use YouTube Create, 9:16
6. Save the prompt that worked — only change one line next time
7. Export, crop, add captions in CapCut or YouTube Create
8. Post it. Then try tool 2 on the same idea.

THE TRUTH
There's no magic "unlimited perfect video" button in 2026.
Free tiers are for testing. Credits run out. Queues get slow. Faces still break if you ask for a dance, a zoom, and an outfit change all at once.
Smart creators aren't relying on one tool. They draft on Qwen, Vheer, or Dola. Lock the face on Kling. Add cinematic touches with Hailuo, Wan, or Flow. Publish from YouTube Create when it's available. Render extras on FramePack if they have the GPU for it.
That combination beats any single "best AI tool" post.

PRO TIPS
1. One clip, one job — face OR product OR place, not all three
2. Write your camera moves like a shot list: push in, orbit, track, hold
3. Lock identity first, then add action
4. Stick to 5-8 seconds — longer free clips fall apart
5. Draft ugly first, remake the winner in higher quality
6. Download immediately — some links expire
7. Keep a prompt notebook — your third rewrite is usually the keeper
8. Use 9:16 for Reels/Shorts, 16:9 only for YouTube B-roll
9. Say what you want, not everything you don't want — one "no extra fingers" line is enough
10. Never pay a site claiming to be FramePack — official is GitHub only

1. Qwen Chat — for fast daily testing
https://chat.qwen.ai/
Good for throwing 10 rough ideas at the wall without leaving your chat window. Free login, works with text and images. Quality is average compared to the top tools, but it's quick.
Try this prompt:
A handheld 9:16 night market clip. A young street cook flips noodles in a steel wok, orange fire bloom for one second, steam hits the lens, neon signs smear in the background, shallow depth of field, natural skin texture, no extra fingers, no text on screen, 5 seconds, camera slowly pushes in then holds on the plate.
Why it works: one place, one subject, one lighting moment, one camera move. Vague prompts turn into mush.


2. Kling AI — for faces that don't morph
https://klingai.com/
This is my go-to when a face needs to stay recognizable. Free daily credits, solid at blinking, breathing, small head turns. Free clips may have a watermark and get stuck in queue during busy hours, so test in standard mode first.
Try this prompt:
Animate this portrait. Keep the exact face, hairline, and outfit. The person looks at camera, blinks once, then smiles and turns 15 degrees to the right as wind moves a few strands of hair. Soft window light from the left, slow push in, no morphing, no extra limbs, photoreal, 5 seconds, 9:16.
Why it works: small actions protect identity. Big motion is when faces fall apart.
Kling AI: Next-Gen AI Video & Image Generator

3. PixVerse — for punchy short clips and effects
https://pixverse.ai/
Great for Reels-style motion — whip pans, stylized effects on a still image. You get signup credits plus a small daily top-up. Free exports are lower quality and marked, so use this as a testing ground, not your final file.
Try this prompt:
Cinematic product hero shot of a glass perfume bottle on wet black stone. Camera orbits 90 degrees from left to right, a single drop slides down the glass, warm rim light, slow motion mist in the air, luxury commercial look, sharp label, no extra bottles, no warped text, 5 seconds, 9:16.
Why it works: one object, one camera path, one light source. Keep it simple.
The Generative Core Behind Digital Worlds and Experiences | PixVerse

4. Vheer — for zero-hassle first tests
https://vheer.com/
Drop a photo in your browser, get motion out. No account needed for most features, often no watermark. This is the "open tab, test idea, decide fast" tool. Download your clip right away — premium models eat through credits quickly.
Try this prompt:
Turn this photo into a living clip. Subtle breathing, slight hair movement, eyes shift toward camera, background stays locked, natural daylight, no face warp, no extra people entering frame, smooth 4 second loop, 9:16.
Why it works: small, believable motion looks better than a full scene. Ask for too much and you get melted hands.
Free AI Image & Video Generator - Unlimited, No Signup | Vheer

5. Hailuo AI — for cinematic body movement
https://hailuoai.video/
Best for full-body motion, walking, camera tracking — scenes that feel filmed, not just wiggled. Strong image-to-video, director-style camera controls, and some routes now include native sound. Free queues can be slow.
Try this prompt:
A woman in a long coat walks toward camera on a rainy Tokyo side street at blue hour. Tracking shot at chest height, neon reflections on wet asphalt, light rain, she glances left then back to camera, coat fabric moves with real weight, photoreal film grain, no text, no extra faces, 6 seconds, 9:16.
Why it works: weather, a tracking shot, and one glance give the model something real to work with. Empty prompts like "cinematic walking" turn into a slideshow.
Hailuo AI: MiniMax H3 LIVE NOW. Top-Tier Quality, Versatile References

6. Google Flow — for the cleanest Google video stack
https://labs.google/fx/tools/flow
This gives you Veo and Gemini Omni with camera control, first/last frame options, and scene extension. Omni even lets you talk to edit your clip after the first render. Access depends on your Google AI plan and country.
Try this prompt:
Wide to medium shot of a red vintage bicycle leaning on a sunlit Lisbon wall. Start frame is the still photo. Camera dollies in, a curtain in the window lifts once, dust floats in the beam, warm late afternoon light, photoreal, no people, no warped wheels, 8 seconds, 16:9. After generate: keep the same bike and wall, only add a cat walking behind the wheel.
Why it works: one starting frame, one camera move, one follow-up edit. That's exactly what Omni is built for.
Google Flow - AI Creative Studio for Video, Images & Custom Tools

7. YouTube Create — free Gemini Omni on your phone
YouTube Create app, or try Shorts Remix inside YouTube
Good for vertical clips or filling in missing footage. Gemini Omni can build ~10 second vertical clips from text plus up to 3 reference photos. Rollout is still limited in some regions — if you don't see "Generate video," try Flow instead.
Try this prompt:
Vertical cooking Short. Close up of hands folding a breakfast wrap on a wooden board, steam rising, soft kitchen window light, slow tilt up to the finished plate, appetizing, no logos, no extra hands, 8 seconds, 9:16. Lighting: soft morning light.
Why it works: clear shot size, one action, one lighting style. Long storylines get cut off.


8. Dola AI — Seedance-style clips in a simple chat
https://www.dola.com/ | Video desk: https://www.dolai.video/
Simple chat interface for text or photo to video. Free daily credits, decent motion, mobile app available. Great for style tests — remake your favorite in Kling or Flow if you need a cleaner face.
Try this prompt:
A quiet 9:16 cottage kitchen at sunrise. Golden light through linen curtains, a ceramic mug steams on a wooden table, dust in the air, camera slowly pushes from the doorway to the mug, photoreal, warm, no people, no warped cups, 8 seconds, soft ambient birds outside.
Why it works: give it mood, texture, and one object to land on. It handles slow camera work well.

9. Wan Video — for a different look and longer clips
https://wan.video/ (open models also on Hugging Face Spaces)
Output here often looks less generic than other tools. Supports text, image, and multiple reference inputs, plus longer clips than the usual 5-second limit. Site can feel Chinese-first — use browser translate. Stick to the official site, fake clones exist.
Try this prompt:
Dreamlike 16:9 shot of a glass train gliding over a dark ocean at sunset. Warm sun on the horizon, waves catch orange light, camera tracks beside one carriage, passengers are only silhouettes, no readable text, no broken geometry, cinematic, 8 to 10 seconds, gentle engine hum.
Why it works: mood words plus a clean camera path. Overloaded prompts cause distortion.
Wan AI: Leading AI Video Generation Model

10. FramePack — run it on your own PC, no credits needed
Official code: https://github.com/lllyasviel/FramePack
Project page: https://lllyasviel.github.io/frame_pack_gitpage/
For longer image-to-video work once you've burned through web credits. You render locally, so it needs setup and a decent GPU. Warning: framepack.net and similar sites are NOT official — only use GitHub.
Try this prompt:
Start from this photo. Keep the same person and room. She looks down at a book, turns one page, then looks up toward the window as afternoon light shifts across the desk. Slow and natural motion only, no teleport, no extra fingers, 5 to 10 seconds.
Why it works: small continuous actions stay stable. Big jumps cause glitches.

## 40 Prompts Every Creator Needs (Steal These)
I built these after watching creators waste hours typing the same question into C… See more

These 40 prompts write your hooks, scripts, captions, and repurposed content for you. Copy one, paste your topic in the brackets, done.
Simple English. Zero fluff. Works with ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

###CATEGORY 1: HOOKS
1. Freeze Frame
Give me 15 first lines for a post about [TOPIC] that would make someone stop mid scroll, each testing a different emotion like shock, curiosity, humor, fear, and pride.
Why it works: Testing across emotions shows you which trigger actually lands instead of guessing one angle and hoping.

2. Flip The Script
Write 10 hooks that take the popular belief about [TOPIC] and argue the exact opposite, backing each one with a reason.
Why it works: People stop scrolling to argue with you. Disagreement is free engagement.

3. Open Loop
Write 10 hooks for [TOPIC] that start in the middle of the action, so the reader has to keep reading to find out what happened.
Why it works: Your brain hates an unfinished story. It reads on just to close the loop.

4. Count It Up
Turn [TOPIC] into 10 hooks using an odd, specific number instead of a round one, each promising one clear result.
Why it works: A number like 7 feels more researched and honest than a round number like 10.

5. Smart Vs Struggling
Write 8 hooks that show the easy way to do [TOPIC] side by side with the hard way most people are still doing.
Why it works: Contrast makes the benefit obvious in a single line, no explaining needed.

6. First Person Cold Open
Give me 5 story hooks about [EXPERIENCE] that start right in the middle of the moment, not the backstory.
Why it works: Starting at the peak of the action grabs attention before you explain any context.

7. Ask What They Won't
Write 10 questions [AUDIENCE] thinks about [TOPIC] but feels a little embarrassed to ask out loud.
Why it works: Naming an unspoken thought builds instant trust because it feels like you read their mind.

8. Costly Mistake
Write 8 hooks built around the one mistake with [TOPIC] that quietly wastes the most time.
Why it works: People engage more with avoiding a loss than they do with chasing a gain.

9. Say It Bold
Write 8 confident, provable statements about [TOPIC] I can open a post with.
Why it works: A bold claim forces a reaction, agree or push back, and both drive comments.

10. Hook Autopsy
Here are 5 hooks I wrote for [TOPIC]. Score each one from 1 to 10, explain the weak point, then rewrite it stronger: [PASTE]
Why it works: Feedback plus a rewrite teaches you the actual pattern instead of handing you one fix.

### CATEGORY 2: REELS
1. Half Minute Script
Write a 30 second reel script about [TOPIC] with a hook, three fast points, and one clear call to action.
Why it works: Reels under 30 seconds get rewatched more, and rewatches boost reach.

2. Article Into Video
Turn this blog post into a 30 second talking head script I can film today: [PASTE]
Why it works: Repurposing lets one idea earn attention in two different formats without extra research.

3. Rapid Fire Tips
Script a fast 5 tip reel on [TOPIC], one punchy line per tip, ending with a memorable close.
Why it works: Short, tip by tip pacing keeps watch time high because every line delivers value.

4. Turn Life Into A Reel
Turn [EXPERIENCE] into a 40 second story reel that ends with the lesson I learned.
Why it works: Personal stories with a clear takeaway build connection and get saved for later.

5. Shot List Builder
For this script, list the exact b roll shots to film for every line so I am never guessing on set: [PASTE]
Why it works: Planning shots ahead of time cuts editing time and makes the final cut feel smoother.

6. Speak Naturally
Give me a loose talking point outline on [TOPIC] so I can speak in my own words instead of reading a script.
Why it works: Natural delivery feels more trustworthy on camera than a memorized script ever does.

7. On Screen Timing
Write the on screen text overlays for this reel, timed line by line so the captions match the voice: [PASTE]
Why it works: Synced text keeps people watching with the sound off, which is most of your audience.

8. React And Remix
Write a reaction script responding to [TREND or POST] that adds my own take in the first five seconds.
Why it works: Riding a trend early borrows its reach while your own angle still makes it stand out.

9. Find The Drop Off
Look at this script and tell me exactly where viewers will lose interest, then fix each spot: [PASTE]
Why it works: Retention drops happen at predictable moments, and fixing them keeps people watching longer.

10. Episode Plan
Turn [TOPIC] into a 5 part reel series with a title for each episode so people come back for the next one.
Why it works: Series content trains both the algorithm and your audience to expect your next post.

### CATEGORY 3: CAPTIONS
1. Hook Value Ask
Write a caption for a post about [TOPIC] in this order, one line hook, one useful tip, one clear ask.
Why it works: Giving value before asking for anything makes the ask feel earned, not pushy.

2. Keyword Friendly
Write a caption for [TOPIC] that naturally includes searchable keywords plus 8 relevant hashtags.
Why it works: Keyword rich captions help your post show up in search, not just in the feed.

3. Four Line Story
Turn [TOPIC] into a four line caption that reads like a tiny story and lands on one lesson.
Why it works: Short stories are easy to read in one glance and easy to remember afterward.

4. Worth Saving
Write a caption for [TOPIC] that gives people a clear reason to save the post for later.
Why it works: Saves signal high value content to the algorithm more strongly than likes do.

5. Ending Options
Give me 10 different ways to end a caption about [TOPIC], from a question to a challenge to a soft ask.
Why it works: Testing different endings shows you exactly which action your audience responds to.

6. Slide By Slide Recap
Write a caption for a carousel that teaches [TOPIC], summarizing each slide in one short line.
Why it works: A recap caption helps people who only skim the slides still walk away with the full point.

7. Three Hashtag Tiers
Give me three tiers of hashtags, wide reach, niche, and small community, for a [NICHE] post.
Why it works: Mixing hashtag sizes helps you reach new people while still ranking in the smaller tags.

8. Comment Magnet
Write a caption ending in a specific question that [AUDIENCE] would actually stop to answer.
Why it works: Specific questions get real answers. Generic ones like "thoughts?" get ignored.

9. Sound Like Me
Rewrite this caption in my voice, casual, direct, no filler words: [PASTE]
Why it works: A consistent voice builds recognition before people even see your name on the post.

10. Pin This First
Write a strong first comment to pin under a post about [TOPIC] that adds context or a resource.
Why it works: A pinned comment adds extra value without making the caption itself too long.

### CATEGORY 4: REPURPOSE
1. Ten Formats
Turn this one post into 10 formats, reel, carousel, tweet, thread, email, and more: [PASTE]
Why it works: One idea reused across formats multiplies your reach without any extra research time.

2. Thread From Long Form
Turn this [BLOG or TRANSCRIPT] into a punchy 8 part thread with one idea per post.
Why it works: Threads break dense content into bite sized pieces people can actually skim and finish.

3. Weekly Wrap
Turn this week's posts into a short newsletter with one main lesson tying them together: [PASTE]
Why it works: A newsletter builds an owned audience that isn't at the mercy of an algorithm change.

4. Long Video Into Clips
Cut this long video transcript into 3 short form clips with exact timestamps: [PASTE]
Why it works: Clipping long content into shorts extends its life across more platforms with less effort.

5. Quote Card Miner
Pull 8 quotable lines from this content that would work as standalone quote graphics: [PASTE]
Why it works: Quote cards get shared easily because they need no extra context to make sense.

6. Slide Titles
Turn [TOPIC] into a 7 slide carousel outline with one clear title per slide.
Why it works: Strong slide titles let people understand the value before they even swipe once.

7. Comment Mining
Read these comments and turn the most repeated questions into new content ideas: [PASTE]
Why it works: Real audience questions guarantee your next post already has built in demand.

8. Platform Switch
Adapt this Instagram caption for LinkedIn and for X, adjusting tone and length for each: [PASTE]
Why it works: Every platform rewards a different tone, so a straight copy and paste usually underperforms.

9. Make It Timeless
Rewrite this timely post as an evergreen version I can repost months from now: [PASTE]
Why it works: Evergreen content keeps earning views long after the original trend has faded away.

10. Repost Without Repeat
Give me 5 ways to repost [OLD POST] so it feels new instead of recycled.
Why it works: A fresh angle on old content saves you time while still feeling original to your audience.

🛠️ PRO TIPS
Always paste real content into the [PASTE] prompts. Real input beats a made up example every time.
Run the same prompt twice. AI gives different options each time, so a second run often beats the first.
Feed the AI 2 or 3 of your best past posts before asking it to write in your voice.
Keep captions under 150 words unless you are telling a full story.
Test every hook as a spoken line and as text on screen. It has to work both ways.
Save your top five prompts in a note so you are never starting from a blank page again.
Batch one category at a time. Do all your hooks first, then all your captions, instead of jumping around.
Read your AI output out loud before posting. If it sounds robotic, ask it to simplify the language.

THE TRUTH
Nobody grows because they found one magic prompt.
They grow because they show up with a system every single day.
A great hook still needs a real point behind it.
A great caption still needs an audience that trusts you.
AI can write the words fast, but it cannot make you consistent.
That part is still on you.
These prompts remove the blank page.
They do not remove the work of actually posting.
