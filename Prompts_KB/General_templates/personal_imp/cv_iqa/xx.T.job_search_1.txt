## My cousin had been struggling to find a job for months.
So I helped him use Claude to apply to 300 jobs.

The next morning, his phone was blowing up. 9 interview calls in 24 hours.
These were the 10 exact prompts we used 👇

1. The "Apply to Jobs for Me" Master Prompt
Act as my AI job-search assistant. My target role is [JOB TITLE], preferred locations are [LOCATIONS/REMOTE], minimum salary is [$X], and I have [X YEARS] of experience in [SKILLS/INDUSTRY].
Use my resume/profile below to find jobs posted within the last [7/14/30] days that match at least [70]% of my background. Prioritize jobs where I meet the core requirements, not aspirational roles.
For every suitable job:
1. Check that it is still open.
2. Avoid duplicates, staffing spam, commission-only roles, and jobs requiring skills I clearly don’t have.
3. Tailor my resume summary and top 5 bullets to the job description without inventing experience.
4. Write a short personalized cover note under 150 words.
5. Prepare answers for common application questions using only information I provide.
6. Add the company, role, salary, link, match score, date, and application status to a tracker.
If you have permission and tools to submit applications, show me the completed application for approval before submitting. Otherwise, prepare everything so I can apply quickly.
My resume/profile: [PASTE RESUME]

2. Find 100 High-Match Jobs Instead of Randomly Applying
Find [100] currently open [JOB TITLE] positions matching my profile. Search for these related titles too: [TITLE 1, TITLE 2, TITLE 3].
Filters: [REMOTE/HYBRID/ONSITE], [LOCATION], salary above [$X], experience level [LEVEL], posted within [X DAYS].
Exclude: [INDUSTRIES/COMPANIES/CONTRACT ROLES/etc.].
Score every job from 0–100 using: skills match 40%, experience match 25%, industry match 15%, salary/location fit 10%, and qualifications match 10%.
Only keep jobs scoring 75+. For each result, give me: company | role | location | salary | match score | strongest matching skills | biggest gap | application link.
My background: [PASTE RESUME]

3. Tailor My Resume Automatically for Every Job
I will give you my resume and a job description. Create an ATS-friendly version of my resume specifically for that position.
Keep all facts truthful. Do not invent employers, achievements, certifications, skills, or numbers.
Identify the 10–15 most important keywords in the job description and naturally incorporate relevant ones into my summary, skills, and experience bullets. Rewrite weak bullets using: action + task + measurable result whenever my existing information supports it.
Keep the resume to [1/2] pages and place the most relevant experience first.
At the end, show:
Keywords added: [...]
Important gaps: [...]
Estimated match before: X/100
Estimated match after: X/100
Resume: [PASTE]
Job description: [PASTE]

4. Create a Different Cover Letter for Every Application
Write a personalized cover letter for this specific job. Do not use generic phrases like "I am excited to apply" unless necessary.
Structure it in 3 short paragraphs:
• Why my background fits their biggest need
• 2 relevant achievements from my resume
• Why this specific company/role interests me
Keep it between 120–180 words, professional but conversational. Mention the company and role naturally. Never invent knowledge about the company; if information is missing, leave [EDIT THIS] placeholders.
Resume: [PASTE]
Job description: [PASTE]

5. Answer Those Annoying Application Questions
Act as my job-application answer writer. Using only my resume and information below, create concise answers to application questions such as:
"Why do you want to work here?"
"Why are you a good fit?"
"Tell us about yourself."
"Describe a relevant accomplishment."
"What makes you different from other candidates?"
Keep each answer between 60–120 words unless the form specifies otherwise. Make every answer specific to the job description and avoid unsupported claims.
For questions requiring information you don't have—salary history, visa status, demographics, disability, legal attestations, or personal preferences—write NEEDS MY INPUT instead of guessing.
My resume: [PASTE]
Job description: [PASTE]

6. Turn One Resume Into 5 Job-Specific Versions
Analyze my resume and create 5 positioning versions targeting these roles:
[ROLE 1]
[ROLE 2]
[ROLE 3]
[ROLE 4]
[ROLE 5]
For each version, rewrite only these sections:
Headline, professional summary, skills, and the 6 most relevant experience bullets.
Preserve the same factual work history. Change emphasis and keywords based on what employers hiring for each role are likely to prioritize.
Also tell me which experiences should appear near the top for each version.
Resume: [PASTE]

7. Reject Bad Jobs Before I Waste Time Applying
Review this job description like a strict job-search filter. Compare it against my resume and tell me whether it passes these requirements:
Must-have: [SALARY], [LOCATION], [REMOTE/HYBRID], [SENIORITY], [INDUSTRY], [VISA/WORK AUTHORIZATION REQUIREMENTS], [OTHER].
Check for: missing salary information, unrealistic requirement lists, major experience gaps, misleading titles, commission-only compensation, suspicious company details, excessive unpaid assignments, and responsibilities significantly above/below the advertised level.
Return:
Match: X/100
Requirements I meet: [...]
Requirements I don't meet: [...]
Potential red flags: [...]
Questions I should verify before applying: [...]
Resume: [PASTE]
Job description: [PASTE]

8. Create My 300-Job Application System
Design a system for applying to 300 highly relevant jobs over [X] weeks without sending identical applications everywhere.
Divide applications into three groups:
Tier A: dream companies/highest match — heavily personalized
Tier B: strong match — moderately personalized
Tier C: acceptable match — lightweight personalization
Recommend how many applications I should complete daily in each tier. Create a tracker containing: company, role, link, date found, match score, resume version, cover note, application date, recruiter, follow-up date, response, interview stage, and notes.
Also create rules for avoiding duplicates and tell me which applications deserve the most customization.
My target: [ROLE]
Location: [LOCATION]
Salary: [RANGE]
Background: [PASTE SUMMARY]

9. Follow Up After Every Application
Write a short follow-up message for a job I applied to [X] days ago.
Role: [JOB TITLE]
Company: [COMPANY]
Contact: [RECRUITER/HIRING MANAGER NAME]
Relevant achievement: [ACHIEVEMENT]
Keep it under 90 words. Mention one specific reason my background fits the role, politely reaffirm interest, and ask whether I can provide anything helpful for the hiring process.
Make it sound human and confident—not desperate, pushy, or AI-generated.
Give me versions for LinkedIn DM and email.

10. Turn Every Interview Invite Into Interview Prep
I received an interview for this role. Analyze the job description and my resume and create a targeted interview preparation pack.
Include:
• The 10 questions I am most likely to be asked
• Strong answer outlines using my actual experience
• 5 STAR stories I should prepare
• Skills or experience the interviewer may challenge
• A strong 60-second "Tell me about yourself" response
• 7 intelligent questions to ask the interviewer
• A concise explanation for any noticeable resume gaps or weaker qualifications without exaggerating
Resume: [PASTE]
Job description: [PASTE]
Interview type: [RECRUITER/HIRING MANAGER/TECHNICAL/PANEL]
