## 5 prompts I use daily that have transformed my day to day; they could do the same for you 👇

1. Daily Strategic Planning
Turn chaos into a clear execution plan.
Prompt: Act as an executive productivity coach.
Help me organize my day with the following information:
Goals for today: [list of goals]
Tasks: [list of tasks]
Meetings: [list of meetings]
Deadlines: [list of deadlines]
Then:
1. Identify my top 3 priorities
2. Suggest a structured schedule
3. Highlight tasks that can be automated or delegated
4. Recommend the highest impact activities for today

2. Thinking Clarification Prompt
Turn messy thoughts into structured ideas.
Prompt: I will share a general idea or unstructured thoughts.
Your task is to:
Clarify the main idea
Organize it logically
Identify missing parts
Suggest improvements
Here is the idea:
[Paste your thoughts]

3. Research Assistant
Get structured information in minutes.
Prompt: Act as a professional research analyst.
Research the following topic: [topic]
Provide:
1. Key information
2. Current trends
3. Important statistics
4. Main companies or market players
5. Opportunities and risks in this sector

4. Learning Acceleration Prompt
Learn complex topics much faster.
Prompt: Act as an expert teacher in [topic].
Explain this concept clearly and concisely:
[Topic]
Structure the explanation as:
Simple explanation
Key principles
Practical examples
Common mistakes
A brief summary for quick recall

5. Decision Making Prompt
Make smarter decisions.
Prompt:
Help me evaluate the following decision:
[Describe the situation]
Analyze:
1. Pros and cons
2. Potential risks
3. Long term implications
4. Alternative options
Then, recommend the most rational option

## Save 20 Hours a Week. Use these 8 prompts instead 👇

1/ HOOK TOOL
Act as an elite content strategist. I’m creating content about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE]. Generate 20 hooks that create curiosity without clickbait. Mix contrarian takes, mistakes, surprising facts, open loops and specific outcomes. Keep each under 12 words. Pick the strongest 5 and explain why they work.

2/ CEO PRIORITY TOOL
Here’s everything on my plate: [PASTE]. Act as my chief of staff. Rank everything by revenue impact, urgency and strategic importance. Tell me what to DO, DELEGATE, AUTOMATE or DELETE. Give me the 3 tasks that deserve my attention today.

3/ INBOX TOOL
Here’s an email/thread: [PASTE]. Tell me what they actually want, what decision I need to make and anything I might be missing. Then write the shortest useful response in my tone. No corporate filler.

4/ MEETING TOOL
Here’s my meeting transcript: [PASTE]. Extract decisions, commitments, unresolved questions, action items, owners and deadlines. Draft every follow-up message I need to send. Flag anything with no clear owner.

5/ RESEARCH TOOL
I need to understand [TOPIC] to make this decision: [DECISION]. Research it like an analyst. Separate facts from assumptions, compare my options, identify risks and opportunities, and show me what information would change the decision. Finish with a one-page decision brief.

6/ AUTOMATION TOOL
I repeatedly do this task: [DESCRIBE WORKFLOW]. Map every step from trigger to completion. Label each KEEP, AUTOMATE, DELEGATE or REMOVE. Then design the simplest AI automation, including tools, triggers, actions, human approvals and exceptions.

7/ WRITING TOOL
Here are my messy thoughts: [PASTE]. Turn them into a clear [EMAIL/POST/PROPOSAL]. Preserve my opinions and natural voice. Remove repetition, jargon and filler. Make every sentence earn its place. Give me the finished version first.

8/ WEEK-BACK TOOL
Audit how I spend a normal week: [PASTE TASKS + HOURS]. Find where I’m losing time through repetition, unnecessary meetings, context switching and manual work. Build a new weekly system designed to save me 20 hours. Show exactly where each saved hour comes from.

I FOUND 5 PROMPTS THAT MAKE AI FEEL LIKE A DIFFERENT TOOL.
Copy these before they disappear into your notes: 👇

1. The Strategic Thinker
Instead of asking AI for an answer, make it think like a strategist:
"Act as a senior strategist. Before giving me a recommendation, identify the real problem, list the key constraints, consider 3 possible approaches, compare their trade-offs, then recommend the strongest option. Explain your reasoning clearly."

2. The Brutal Critic
Want AI to find what's wrong instead of telling you everything looks good?
"Act as a brutally honest expert. Analyze my idea and identify its 5 biggest weaknesses, hidden assumptions, risks, and potential failure points. Don't praise it unless the praise is genuinely justified. Then show me exactly how to improve it."

3. The Expert Researcher
Don't just ask AI to explain something.
Give it a research framework:
"Analyze this topic like a senior researcher. Separate established facts from assumptions, identify the most important variables, explain competing viewpoints, highlight uncertainties, and finish with the 5 most important conclusions."

4. The Problem Solver
When you're stuck, don't ask:
"What's the solution?"
Use this:
"Help me solve this problem systematically. First define the problem precisely. Then identify the root causes, separate symptoms from causes, generate multiple solutions, evaluate each against the constraints, and give me the best next action."

5. The AI Operator
This is the one I use when I want AI to actually help execute something:
"Act as my AI operator. Take the goal below and break it into the smallest actionable steps. Identify what information you need from me, make reasonable assumptions where possible, complete the work step-by-step, and only ask me questions when they are genuinely necessary."

14 Techniques That Can Make You Ridiculously Productive! Most people barely use them 👇

1. The 5-Second Rule
Stop thinking. Start moving.
When you catch yourself hesitating on something you already decided to do, count:
5... 4... 3... 2... 1... GO.
Then physically start the first step.
Use it for:
Getting out of bed
Starting a workout
Making that call
The goal: Give yourself less time to talk yourself out of starting.

2. The Feynman Technique
If you can't explain it simply, you probably don't understand it yet.
Pick something you're learning.
Explain it in plain language as if you're teaching a beginner.
Get stuck?
That's the part you need to study again.
Learn → Explain → Find gaps → Relearn

3. The Eisenhower Matrix
Stop treating every task like an emergency.
Divide your tasks into 4 boxes:
Urgent + Important: Do it
Important + Not Urgent: Schedule it
Urgent + Not Important: Delegate it
Neither: Eliminate it
Now your to-do list has priorities.

4. The 2-Minute Rule
Tiny tasks can create a surprising amount of clutter.
When something comes up, ask:
"Can I finish this in about 2 minutes?"
If yes, consider doing it immediately.
Reply to the email.
File the document.
Send the message.
Less accumulation. Less mental clutter.

5. The Pomodoro Technique
25 minutes. One task. Zero distractions.
Set a timer for 25 minutes.
Work on ONE thing.
Then take a 5-minute break.
Walk. Stretch. Hydrate.
Repeat.
After several rounds, take a longer break.
Simple structure. Serious focus.

6. Curate Your Inputs
Your attention is being trained every day.
Look at what you repeatedly consume:
Who do you follow?
What do you read?
What fills your feed?
Keep the sources that teach, inspire or challenge you.
Remove the ones that consistently waste your attention.
Choose your inputs on purpose.

7. The 1% Rule
Forget massive overnight transformations.
Pick ONE skill or habit.
Then make a tiny improvement consistently.
Read another page.
Practice another 5 minutes.
Send another outreach message.
Small improvements can become meaningful when repeated over time.
Think consistency, not perfection.

8. Deep Work Blocks
Your best work needs uninterrupted time.
Block focused time on your calendar.
Then:
Turn off notifications.
Put your phone away.
Close unnecessary tabs.
Choose ONE important task.
Protect that time like an appointment.
Depth beats constant switching.

9. The Premortem
Try to find the problems BEFORE they happen.
Before starting an important project, imagine:
"It's 6 months later and this failed. Why?"
List the possible reasons.
Bad timing?
Poor execution?
Wrong assumptions?
Then build safeguards into your plan now.
Anticipate problems before solving them.

10. Habit Stacking
Make a new habit easier to remember.
Start with something you already do consistently.
Then attach your new habit directly after it.
Example:
"After I pour my morning coffee, I'll read for 10 minutes."
Existing habit → New habit
Your old routine becomes the reminder.

11. The 80/20 Principle
Not every action produces equal results.
Look for the small number of activities producing a large share of your progress.
Then ask:
What should I do MORE of?
What should I reduce?
What can I delegate?
What can I eliminate?
Focus on your highest-impact work.

12. The Two-List Strategy
Your "not doing" list matters too.
Write down your most important goals.
Then create another list:
Everything competing for your attention.
New projects.
Random opportunities.
Low-priority ideas.
Don't automatically chase all of them.
Focus often requires saying no.

13. Reflective Journaling
Give yourself a 5-minute feedback loop.
At the end of the day, answer 3 questions:
What went well?
What didn't?
What will I change tomorrow?
That's it.
Keep it short.
Reflect → Adjust → Repeat

14. Spaced Repetition
Don't learn something once and expect to remember it.
Review important information again over increasingly spaced intervals.
For example:
Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7 → Day 14
Use flashcards, practice questions or your own notes.
Even better: try recalling the answer before looking.
Spacing and retrieval practice are among the better-supported strategies in learning research.

## 8 ChatGPT Prompts to Save You 20 Hours a Week 👇

1/ Turn a messy brain dump into a plan
PROMPT: Here’s everything I need to get done: [PASTE YOUR LIST]
Organise this into a prioritised action plan. Rank each task by impact and urgency, estimate how long each will take, and tell me what I should do first, delegate, automate or ignore.

2/ Clear your inbox faster
PROMPT: Here’s an email I received: [PASTE EMAIL]
Summarise what they actually want in one sentence. Identify every action item, question and deadline. Then draft a concise reply in my natural tone that addresses everything required. Remove unnecessary corporate language and keep it clear and human.

3/ Turn meetings into action items
PROMPT: Here’s my meeting transcript or notes: [PASTE NOTES]
Turn this into a clear meeting summary. Extract the key decisions, action items, owners, deadlines, unanswered questions and anything requiring follow-up. Finish with a short follow-up email I can send to everyone involved.

4/ Do 60 minutes of research in 5
PROMPT: I need to understand: [TOPIC]
Give me the 20% of information that will give me 80% of the understanding. Explain it simply without assuming prior knowledge. Separate confirmed facts, assumptions and unknowns. Highlight anything commonly misunderstood, then finish with the 5 most important things I should know or do next.

5/ Create a week of content at once
PROMPT: My expertise is [TOPIC]. My audience is [AUDIENCE]. My goal is [GOAL].
Turn one strong core idea into 7 days of content. Give me 3 scroll-stopping hooks, a LinkedIn post, short-form video script, email, carousel outline and 5 repurposing ideas. Make every piece feel native to its platform, useful and specific. Avoid generic advice and corporate language.

6/ Stop staring at spreadsheets
PROMPT: Analyse this data: [PASTE OR UPLOAD DATA]
Find the biggest trends, anomalies, opportunities and problems I might have missed. Quantify anything important and explain the findings in plain English. Tell me why each insight matters, then recommend 5 specific actions I should take based on the data.

7/ Automate repetitive work
PROMPT: I repeatedly do this task: [DESCRIBE TASK]
Map the entire process step by step. Identify which steps can be eliminated, simplified, templated, delegated or automated with AI. Then design the simplest improved workflow and show me the exact triggers, actions, tools and human approval points required to implement it.

8/ Make ChatGPT your daily chief of staff
PROMPT: Act as my chief of staff.
My goals are: [GOALS]
Today I need to complete: [TASKS]
I have [X HOURS] available. Build the highest-impact schedule for my day. Prioritise tasks based on their contribution to my goals, challenge anything that doesn’t deserve my time, identify what I can delegate or automate, and give me the 3 non-negotiable outcomes I should complete today.
