🚨Claude Opus 5.5 can now handle tasks you usually do manually.
Here are 10 prompts that could save you hours: 👇

1. CLEAN UP YOUR INBOX
"Review my connected inbox for old newsletters, promotions, receipts and rarely opened messages. Group them, identify safe unsubscribe options and flag anything needing a reply. Show me the proposed changes before taking action."

2. CUT YOUR SOFTWARE COSTS
"Review my connected email, documents and expense files for software subscriptions. Identify unnecessary tools, suggest cheaper alternatives with similar features and calculate potential monthly savings. Do not cancel anything; show me the comparison first."

3. FIND RELEVANT JOBS
"Search public job boards and company career pages for recent [JOB TITLE] openings in [LOCATION]. Remove duplicates, match the roles against my CV and create a spreadsheet with the company, role, location, posting date, requirements and application link."

4. BUILD MY WEEKLY BUSINESS REPORT
"Review my sales, website data, customer feedback, tasks and project updates. Create a concise report covering what worked, what underperformed, what customers want, key changes from last week and my three priorities for next week. Flag missing information."

5. FIND WORK AI CAN TAKE OVER
"Review my connected apps and files for repetitive tasks AI could handle or simplify. For each opportunity, estimate the time saved, tools required, risks and human review needed, then rank them by impact and difficulty."

6. COMPLETE A BROWSER TASK
"Use the browser to complete this task: [TASK]. Navigate the required websites, gather information and fill in fields when necessary. Stop before submitting forms, making purchases, accepting terms or changing account settings unless I approve."

7. TEST MY WEBSITE LIKE A CUSTOMER
"Open [WEBSITE] and test it as a first-time visitor. Check navigation, signup, buttons, forms, links, mobile layout and checkout up to payment. Document anything broken or confusing with screenshots and recommend specific fixes."

8. ANALYZE MY COMPETITORS
"Research [COMPETITOR 1], [COMPETITOR 2] and [COMPETITOR 3] using public information. Compare their websites, pricing, offers, features, positioning and signup experiences, then identify their strengths, weaknesses and five practical opportunities for my business."

9. BUILD A SIMPLE ONLINE TOOL
"Create a simple online tool for [AUDIENCE] that solves [PROBLEM]. Make it intuitive, provide a useful result, include a natural email signup and connect users to my product or service. Build and test the prototype, then identify what remains before publishing."

10. PREPARE ME FOR TOMORROW’S MEETINGS
"Review tomorrow’s meetings in my connected calendar and find relevant emails, documents and previous notes. For each meeting, summarize the people involved, objective, previous discussions, preparation needed and three useful questions. Flag anything you cannot verify."

10 secret codes to improve productivity


1. EXPERT:
EXPERT: [Your topic]
Changes ChatGPT from friendly intern to $500/hr consultant. No fluff, no beginner stuff. Just what a top 1% person would do.
Ex: EXPERT: How do I grow on Facebook

2. ELI5:
ELI5: [Complex topic]
Explain Like I'm 5. It removes all jargon and uses real-life analogies. Perfect when you feel stupid reading the docs.
Ex: ELI5: What is SEO

3. TEAR-DOWN:
TEAR-DOWN: [Paste your post / ad / CV / website]
It goes savage. Finds every weak line and tells you why it's not working. No "Great job!". Just brutal fixes.
Ex: TEAR-DOWN: My Instagram bio

4. DEVIL:
DEVIL: [Your plan / idea]
Devil's Advocate mode. It attacks your idea from every angle so you don't fail in public. Investors do this. You should too.
Ex: DEVIL: I want to sell ebooks about fitness

5. BLUEPRINT:
BLUEPRINT: [Your goal]
Turns vague goals into step-by-step system with checkboxes. Not motivation, just the system.
Ex: BLUEPRINT: Make my first $1k online in 30 days

6. SOCRATIC:
SOCRATIC: [Topic you want to learn]
It stops lecturing and starts asking YOU questions until you figure it out yourself. This is how you actually remember it. Feynman Technique built-in.

7. STEAL:
STEAL: [Niche / Competitor]
Reverse-engineers what's already working. Structure, hooks, psychology. Not copy-paste, but "why it went viral."
Ex: STEAL: The top 3 fitness Reels this week

8. PROMPTIFY:
PROMPTIFY: [Your bad prompt]
You paste your bad prompt and it rewrites it into a perfect, pro-level prompt. Then runs it. Best code for beginners.

9. 80/20:
80/20: [Skill / Book / Subject]
Gives you the 20% that drives 80% of results. Cuts 10 hours of learning into 20 minutes.

10. UPGRADE:
UPGRADE: [Your text / email / script]
Takes your 6/10 draft and makes it 10/10. Keeps your voice, removes weak words, adds punch.
Ex: UPGRADE: My DM to a client

My daily stack:
Need to learn? ELI5 + SOCRATIC
Need to work? EXPERT + BLUEPRINT
Need to post? TEAR-DOWN + UPGRADE
You don't need 1000 prompts. You need 10 codes.

🚨 BREAKING: If you're not using Claude at your job, you're already behind!
Copy paste these 19 prompts👇

1. Turn an overloaded task list into a realistic day
"Plan my workday from [start time] to [end time]. My tasks, deadlines, and estimated durations: [paste list]. Fixed meetings: [list]. My top priority is [outcome]. Create a time-blocked schedule with breaks and 30 minutes for unexpected work. If everything won’t fit, tell me what to postpone and why. Label any time estimates you make."

2. Sort a messy inbox into clear next steps
"Review these emails: [paste emails with sender, date, and subject]. Group them into: respond today, schedule work, waiting on someone, and no action needed. For each actionable email, list the requested action, stated deadline, and consequence of waiting. Flag missing deadlines instead of inventing them. Finish with the three actions I should take first."

3. Answer a difficult email without making things worse
"Draft a reply to this email: [paste]. The situation is [context]. My desired outcome is [outcome]. I can offer [options], but cannot commit to [limits]. Acknowledge the concern, explain the relevant facts, and propose a specific next step. Keep it [warm/direct/formal] and under [150] words. Don’t invent excuses, promises, or dates."

4. Turn meeting notes into an action tracker
"Convert these meeting notes into a follow-up email and action table: [paste notes]. Separate confirmed decisions, proposed ideas, and unresolved questions. For each action, show the task, owner, deadline, and dependency. Write ‘not assigned’ or ‘not specified’ where needed. End the email by asking participants to confirm missing owners and dates."

5. Catch up on a conversation you missed
"Read this conversation in chronological order: [paste thread]. Explain the original issue, what changed, decisions that still stand, and unresolved disagreements. My role is [role]. List anything requiring my input, separating explicit requests from your suggestions. Draft a short reply that moves the conversation forward without repeating questions already answered."

6. Read a long document for a specific purpose
"I need to understand this document before [meeting/decision]: [paste document]. My responsibility is [role], and I need to decide [question]. Produce a one-page brief covering the main conclusion, supporting evidence, implications for my work, and unanswered questions. Reference section names where available. Clearly distinguish the document’s statements from your interpretation."

7. Turn a project into a workable plan
"Build a delivery plan for [project]. Success means [measurable outcome]. Deadline: [date]. Team and weekly availability: [details]. Constraints: [budget/tools/approvals]. Break the work into deliverables with owners, estimated effort, dependencies, and completion criteria. Label estimates as assumptions. Identify the biggest scheduling risk and propose a smaller scope if the deadline looks unrealistic."

8. Turn a vague request into a clear assignment
"My [manager/client] asked: ‘[paste request].’ Turn this into a draft work brief with the objective, audience, deliverable, scope, exclusions, and acceptance criteria. Use this context: [details]. Separate known facts from assumptions. Then draft a short clarification message containing only the three questions most likely to prevent rework."

9. Compare proposals without getting distracted by sales language
"Compare these [vendor/freelancer/software] proposals: [paste]. Our requirements are [list], budget is [amount], and non-negotiables are [list]. Build a table comparing total stated cost, included work, exclusions, timing, support, and requirement coverage. Mark missing information as unknown. Recommend the strongest fit based on our priorities and list questions to resolve before committing."

10. Get a spreadsheet formula you can actually use
"I’m using [Excel/Google Sheets]. My column headers and letters are [list], and sample rows are [paste]. I want to [specific calculation or lookup]. The formula will go in [cell] and be copied [down/across]. Give me the exact formula, explain it in plain English, and show expected results for the sample rows. Explain how blanks, duplicates, and missing matches are handled."

11. Document a task so someone else can take it over
"Turn this walkthrough into a step-by-step procedure for a colleague doing it for the first time: [paste rough steps]. The task is [task], using [tools]. Include prerequisites, numbered steps, the expected result after each major step, common mistakes, and a final checklist. Flag gaps in my instructions instead of inventing buttons, settings, or approval rules."

12. Build a presentation around one clear outcome
"Create a [7]-slide presentation outline for [audience] about [topic]. After the presentation, I want them to [decision/action]. Use only these notes and facts: [paste]. For each slide, provide a takeaway headline, up to three bullets, a suggested visual, and brief speaker notes. Flag missing evidence. Finish with a specific ask and next step."

13. Write a decision memo that makes the tradeoffs clear
"Help me decide [decision]. Options: [A/B/C]. Our priorities, ranked, are [list]. Available evidence: [paste]. Write a short decision memo comparing benefits, costs, risks, reversibility, and missing information. Recommend an option using the stated priorities. Identify the assumption most likely to change your recommendation and explain what evidence would resolve it."

14. Push back on extra work professionally
"Help me discuss a new assignment with my manager. Current commitments and deadlines: [list]. New request: [details]. My available capacity is [hours]. Draft a short message explaining the tradeoff and offering three choices: move a deadline, reduce scope, or reassign work. Use the actual tasks and dates. Keep the tone cooperative and avoid committing to an option before they choose."

15. Find useful patterns in customer feedback
"Analyze these customer comments: [paste numbered comments]. Group them into specific themes and count the unique comments mentioning each theme; a comment may belong to multiple themes. Include supporting comment numbers, one representative quote, and the underlying need. Separate reported problems from requested features. Recommend three improvements based on frequency and stated impact, noting the limits of this sample."

16. Improve a draft while protecting the facts
"Edit this [proposal/report/email] for [audience]: [paste draft]. Its purpose is [outcome]. Make the main point clear early, remove repetition, and replace vague wording with concrete language. Preserve all facts, numbers, commitments, and qualifications. Don’t strengthen claims beyond the evidence. Return the revised draft, then flag any statements that need clarification or supporting evidence."

17. Write a weekly update people can scan quickly
"Turn these work notes into a weekly update for [manager/team]: [paste]. Use four sections: completed, in progress, risks or blockers, and decisions needed. Connect completed work to outcomes where the notes support it. For each blocker, name the help needed and deadline if provided. Keep it under [200] words and exclude activity that adds no useful context."

18. Turn your work history into a strong self-review
"Draft my performance self-review using these accomplishments, feedback, and setbacks: [paste]. My role expectations are [list]. Organize it around impact, collaboration, and growth. Connect each achievement to an expectation and include metrics only when supplied. Explain setbacks with the lesson and corrective action. End with three specific next-period goals. Flag places where stronger evidence would help."

19. Reduce the time spent on a recurring task
"I repeat [task] [frequency], and it takes [time]. Here is my current process: [numbered steps]. Tools I already have: [list]. Identify unnecessary steps, work I can batch, reusable templates, and possible automation opportunities. Rank changes by likely effort and benefit, labeling estimates. Give me one improvement to try this week, its setup steps, and a simple way to measure time saved."
