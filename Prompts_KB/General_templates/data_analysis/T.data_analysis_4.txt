Here's my Ultimate Types of Data Analysis Cheatsheet:
There are 10 different types and knowing which one to use for which business problem is what separates a junior analyst from a senior one.

Descriptive Analytics
-- What happened?
-- Summarizes past data to track performance and trends
-- Used in dashboards, reports, KPIs

Diagnostic Analytics
-- Why did it happen?
-- Identifies root causes behind trends or issues
-- Drill down, segment data, find correlations

Predictive Analytics
-- What will happen?
-- Forecasts outcomes using data and models
-- Train models, evaluate, predict

Prescriptive Analytics
-- What should we do?
-- Recommends actions using predictions and optimization
-- Guides decision-making

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
-- What patterns exist?
-- Finds patterns, anomalies, and insights
-- Prepares data for analysis

Inferential Analytics
-- Can we generalize this?
-- Applies findings to larger populations
-- Uses hypothesis testing

Real-Time Analytics
-- What’s happening now?
-- Analyzes live data for instant decisions
-- Triggers alerts and dashboards

Qualitative Analytics
-- What do people think?
-- Analyzes text, feedback, and opinions
-- Finds themes and sentiment

Quantitative Analytics
-- What do numbers say?
-- Analyzes numerical data and metrics
-- Uses statistical methods

Causal Analytics
-- Does X cause Y?
-- Identifies cause-effect relationships
-- Uses experiments and validation

Here is what I see most analysts get wrong:
They jump straight to Predictive or Prescriptive analytics before they have mastered Descriptive and Diagnostic.
You cannot forecast what will happen if you do not first understand what already happened and why.

Start with Descriptive. Master Diagnostic. Then move up.
The analysts who get promoted are not the ones who built the most complex models.
They are the ones who picked the right type of analysis for the right business question — every single time.

## Exploratory Data Analysis: Understand the Data Before You Model It

EDA is where raw data starts turning into useful information. Before building models or drawing conclusions, you need to understand what the data contains, how variables behave, and what relationships or anomalies may exist.

A structured EDA process typically moves through:

• Data Understanding – inspect rows, columns, data types, summaries, and overall structure
• Univariate Analysis – study individual variables using distributions and descriptive statistics
• Bivariate Analysis – examine relationships between two variables through comparisons and correlations
• Multivariate Analysis – explore patterns and interactions involving several variables
• Insights & Reporting – translate findings into conclusions and meaningful visualizations

Important statistical measures include mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, IQR, skewness, and kurtosis.

Visualizations such as histograms, box plots, bar charts, scatter plots, correlation heatmaps, and pair plots help reveal patterns that summary numbers alone may not show.

With Python, libraries such as Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Seaborn can support the entire workflow, from initial inspection to deeper exploration.

EDA is not just about making charts. It helps you spot anomalies, understand distributions, discover relationships, and decide what should happen next in the analysis.
