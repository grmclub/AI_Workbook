Claude can understand your Excel file better than you.
Here are 7 prompts to do it all 👇

1/ ANALYZE THE ENTIRE WORKBOOK
<role>
Act as a senior Excel analyst who can inspect spreadsheets, understand workbook structure, identify important metrics, and explain the data in clear business language.
</role>
<task>
Analyze the Excel file I upload and give me a complete overview of what the workbook contains and what matters most.
</task>
<steps>
1. Inspect every relevant worksheet
2. Identify what each sheet, table, column, and major calculation appears to represent
3. Identify the most important KPIs and business metrics
4. Find trends, spikes, drops, unusual values, and potential anomalies
5. Identify missing values, duplicate records, inconsistent formatting, or suspicious data
6. Identify important relationships between sheets
7. Summarize the 5–10 most important insights
8. Recommend the next analyses I should run
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not merely describe column names
- Focus on what the data means
- Separate confirmed findings from assumptions
- Flag unclear fields instead of guessing
- Do not invent missing context
- Test: would a manager understand the workbook after reading this summary
</rules>
<output>
Workbook Map → Sheet Purposes → Important KPIs → Data Quality Issues → Trends → Anomalies → Key Insights → Recommended Next Analyses
</output>

2/ FIX MY MESSY DATA
<role>
Act as a data-cleaning specialist who audits Excel files for duplicates, missing values, inconsistent categories, formatting problems, broken dates, and unreliable records.
</role>
<task>
Review my workbook and create a safe cleaning plan before any analysis.
</task>
<steps>
1. Inspect the raw data tables
2. Find duplicate rows and suspicious repeated records
3. Identify missing or incomplete values
4. Find inconsistent spelling, categories, capitalization, dates, currencies, and percentages
5. Identify numbers stored as text or malformed fields
6. Find impossible or suspicious outliers
7. Recommend how each issue should be corrected
8. Create a clean-data checklist in priority order
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not silently change uncertain data
- Use "needs review" when the correct value cannot be known
- Preserve raw data before cleaning
- Explain why each issue matters
- Separate formatting problems from actual data problems
- Test: would the cleaned data be reliable enough for reporting
</rules>
<output>
Duplicate Issues → Missing Data → Formatting Problems → Category Inconsistencies → Outliers → Recommended Fixes → Cleaning Checklist
</output>

3/ WRITE THE EXACT FORMULAS FOR ME
<role>
Act as an Excel formula expert who creates accurate formulas based on the actual workbook structure and desired calculation.
</role>
<task>
Write the exact Excel formulas I need and explain where each one should go.
</task>
<steps>
1. Inspect the workbook or ask me to identify the relevant sheets, columns, and calculation goal
2. Determine what each formula needs to calculate
3. Choose the most appropriate Excel functions
4. Write the exact copy-paste-ready formula
5. Specify the cell, column, or table where it belongs
6. Add error handling where appropriate
7. Explain the formula in plain English
8. Provide an alternative formula if there is a simpler or more compatible approach
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not give generic examples when the workbook provides real references
- Use structured references when appropriate
- Prefer readable formulas over unnecessarily complex formulas
- Do not hide errors with IFERROR unless that behavior makes sense
- Test: could I paste this formula directly into my workbook
</rules>
<output>
Calculation Needed → Exact Formula → Placement → Explanation → Error Handling → Alternative Formula
</output>

4/ FIND BROKEN OR SUSPICIOUS FORMULAS
<role>
Act as an Excel formula auditor who finds broken references, inconsistent formulas, logic errors, hidden hard-coded values, and calculation risks.
</role>
<task>
Audit the formulas in my workbook and identify anything that may be producing incorrect results.
</task>
<steps>
1. Inspect formula patterns across rows, columns, and sheets
2. Find #REF!, #VALUE!, #DIV/0!, #N/A, circular references, and other calculation errors
3. Identify formulas that unexpectedly differ from neighboring cells
4. Detect hard-coded numbers inside formulas where references should likely be used
5. Identify incorrect absolute or relative references
6. Check lookup formulas for missing or duplicate matches
7. Identify fragile formulas that may break as the workbook grows
8. Recommend safer replacements
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not assume every unusual formula is wrong
- Explain why a formula is suspicious
- Preserve intended business logic
- Flag uncertainty when workbook context is missing
- Prioritize errors that materially affect important outputs
- Test: would this catch mistakes that could change a business decision
</rules>
<output>
Formula Errors → Inconsistent Formulas → Hard-Coded Values → Reference Problems → Lookup Risks → Fragile Logic → Recommended Fixes
</output>

5/ FIND THE HIDDEN INSIGHTS
<role>
Act as a senior data analyst who finds patterns, opportunities, risks, and business insights hidden inside spreadsheets.
</role>
<task>
Analyze my workbook and find insights that are not obvious from looking at the raw numbers.
</task>
<steps>
1. Understand the business question behind the dataset
2. Segment the data by relevant dimensions such as time, customer, product, region, salesperson, channel, or category
3. Compare performance across segments
4. Identify trends, anomalies, concentration risks, and underperforming areas
5. Identify the strongest drivers of growth or decline
6. Look for relationships worth investigating further
7. Rank findings by business impact
8. Convert the findings into practical actions
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not force patterns where the evidence is weak
- Separate correlation from causation
- Do not judge performance from one metric alone
- Quantify insights where possible
- Make every recommendation traceable to the data
- Test: would this reveal something useful that was easy to miss manually
</rules>
<output>
Key Segments → Performance Differences → Hidden Patterns → Risks → Opportunities → Growth Drivers → Ranked Insights → Recommended Actions
</output>

6/ BUILD MY EXECUTIVE DASHBOARD
<role>
Act as an Excel dashboard strategist who turns raw spreadsheet data into a clean, decision-ready management dashboard.
</role>
<task>
Design the ideal dashboard for this workbook.
</task>
<steps>
1. Ask who will use the dashboard and what decisions they need to make
2. Identify the 5–10 KPIs that deserve visibility
3. Identify comparisons, trends, and exceptions that matter
4. Recommend KPI cards, charts, tables, filters, and conditional formatting
5. Choose the best chart type for each insight
6. Design the page layout in order of importance
7. Identify formulas, pivot tables, or source tables needed to power the dashboard
8. Write takeaway-style titles for each visual
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not turn every field into a chart
- Do not use visuals for decoration
- Keep the dashboard understandable in under 60 seconds
- Prioritize decisions, not data density
- Avoid misleading chart choices
- Test: could an executive tell what needs attention without opening the raw sheets
</rules>
<output>
Dashboard Audience → Key Decisions → KPI Cards → Recommended Charts → Filters → Dashboard Layout → Supporting Calculations → Executive Takeaway Titles
</output>

7/ TURN THE WORKBOOK INTO AN EXECUTIVE REPORT
<role>
Act as an executive reporting analyst who converts spreadsheet data into a concise report explaining what happened, why it matters, and what should happen next.
</role>
<task>
Analyze my workbook and create a decision-ready executive report.
</task>
<steps>
1. Identify the reporting period, audience, and business objective
2. Extract the most important KPIs
3. Compare current performance with previous periods, targets, budgets, or benchmarks when available
4. Identify the largest positive and negative changes
5. Explain the likely drivers supported by the data
6. Highlight risks and opportunities
7. Identify the decisions leadership should consider
8. Write a concise executive summary
9. Create a list of recommended next actions
</steps>
<rules>
- Do not dump raw spreadsheet data into the report
- Focus on decision-relevant findings
- Use numbers only when they strengthen the point
- Separate evidence from interpretation
- Flag missing benchmarks or context
- Test: would leadership know what changed and what to do next
</rules>
<output>
Executive Summary → KPI Snapshot → Major Changes → Drivers → Risks → Opportunities → Decisions Needed → Recommended Actions
</output>
