Mastering Microsoft Copilot prompts requires specific keywords 
based on the proven Goal + Context + Source + Expectations formula.

1. Action Keywords (The "What")
Start your prompt with strong verbs that tell Copilot exactly what task to perform. 

    Summarize / Catch up: Synthesize, condense, brief me, extract.
    Create / Brainstorm: Draft, generate, design, compile.
    Edit / Refine: Proofread, rewrite, rephrase, format.
    Analyze: Compare, categorize, review for inconsistencies.
	
2. Context Keywords (The "Who" and "Why")
These keywords help Copilot understand the situation, relationship, and audience, ensuring the output feels natural. 

    Audience: Senior management, board of directors, non-technical audience, prospective clients.
    Relationship: New client, close colleague, vendor.
    Industry / Role: Marketing campaign, sales record, software development.	
	
3. Expectations Keywords (The "How" and "Format")
Define boundaries for the output style, layout, and length to avoid generic, robotic text. 

    Length: Under 150 words, concise, 1-page briefing.
    Structure: Bullet points, table, step-by-step checklist, numbered list.
    Tone: Diplomatic, direct, professional, data-driven, executive, empathetic.
    Focus: Highlight top 3 priorities, outline key decisions, identify risks.
	
4. Source Keywords (The "Where")
Direct Copilot to your exact data so it references the right information. 

    Document references: Use this [file name], based on the [presentation], from the meeting transcript.
    Email integration: Summarize this email thread, reference my latest email to [name]. 

Real-World Prompt Examples

    For Meeting Prep: "Compile a 1-page briefing for my meeting with [Team/Name]: recent email exchanges, open action items, and key talking points I should raise."
    For Email Rewrites: "Rewrite this email to [Client Name] to be [professional/diplomatic] but firm. Output under 100 words with 2 action bullets."
    For Data Analysis: "From these notes, extract: decisions made, action items with [owners] and [deadlines], and open questions that still need resolution.

The most used keywords in Microsoft Copilot prompts generally fall into four categories: Action, Source, Expectations, and Context. Common high-frequency keywords include verbs like summarize, draft, rewrite, and analyze, combined with format specifiers like bullet points, table, and email.

1. Action Keywords **
These tell Copilot exactly what to do with your data. 

    Summarize or Recap (used heavily for meeting notes or email threads).
    Draft or Create (used for generating new emails, documents, or presentations).
    Rewrite, Proofread, or Refine (used for altering the tone of a message).
    Analyze (used for Excel data sets or lengthy reports). 
	
2. Formatting & Expectation Keywords
These establish constraints on how the output should look and feel. 

    Bullet points
    Table
    Summary
    Professional
    Concise
    Under [X] words	

3. Source Keywords
These point Copilot to the specific document, thread, or file it needs to reference. 

    File name or [document]
    Email thread
    This data or [paste text]
    Meeting notes

4. Context Keywords
These add critical situational information so Copilot can tailor its output. 

    Audience: For example, senior leaders, non-technical, or clients.

The 5-Element Copilot Prompt Formula

Every effective Copilot prompt includes these five elements:

    Action — what you want (create, rewrite, summarise, improve)
    Content Type — slide type or section (agenda, executive summary, comparison, roadmap)
    Topic & Data — what it’s about and the key numbers/messages
    Audience — who will see it (board, investors, internal team, clients)
    Tone & Style — how it should sound and look (executive, concise, data-driven, clean layout)

Formula: Action + Content Type + Topic/Data + Audience + Tone/Style

Executive Summary & High-Level Slides (Prompts 1-5)

1. Executive One-Slider: “Create a one-slide executive summary for [audience] explaining [project/initiative]. Include: 1 key headline, 3 bullet points on impact, and 1 clear ask. Write for very busy senior leaders.”

2. Board-Level Update: “Create a board update slide summarising [topic, e.g., Q4 performance]. Focus on: results vs target, 3 key drivers, and 2 decisions required from the board. Use concise, non-technical language.”

3. Strategic Recommendation: “Create a strategic recommendation slide that compares Option A vs Option B for [decision]. Show: summary, pros/cons, risks, and a recommended option with one-sentence justification.”

4. Leadership Snapshot: “Create a one-slide ‘Leadership Snapshot’ for [initiative]. Include: current status (RAG), top 3 wins, top 3 risks, and the next major milestone with date.”

5. Vision Slide: “Create a vision slide for [programme/strategy] that explains: where we are now, where we want to be in 3 years, and the high-level path to get there. Use simple, inspiring language.”
